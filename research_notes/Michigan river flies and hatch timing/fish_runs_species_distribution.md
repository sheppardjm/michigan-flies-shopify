# Michigan River Salmonids: Distribution, Run Timing, and Wild vs. Stocked Sources

Research date: 2026-09-28. Scope: Michigan Great Lakes tributaries plus the shared St. Marys River. Every dated figure notes its year. "Snippet" flags a claim seen only in a search-result excerpt where the full page could not be retrieved (michigan.gov and canr.msu.edu block automated fetches; several pages were recovered via curl, others were not).

## Key Question 1: Steelhead — rivers, entry timing, holdover, spawn timing/temps, Skamania summer run, weir data

### Takeaway
Michigan DNR states steelhead enter spawning streams from late October to early May, with fall-run fish overwintering and spawning first (often March) and spring-run fish spawning in April; the Little Manistee weir (an unstocked, mostly wild run) is boarded in mid-March and eggs are stripped in mid-April, with counts falling from ~6,000 (2002) to <2,000 (2020). Skamania summer-runs enter the St. Joseph from mid/late June and hold until spawning in February–March; UP Lake Superior runs peak later (early–mid May) than Lower Peninsula rivers; SE Michigan (Huron/Clinton/Belle) runs are almost entirely stocked fish.

### Cited Findings

**Statewide life history and timing (MDNR)**
- "Lake steelhead enter their spawning streams from late October to early May. Although many steelhead enter the rivers in the fall and overwinter there, spawning does not occur until spring. The fall-run fish are typically the first to spawn, often in March, followed by the spring run fish in April." Spawning occurs in fine gravel in a riffle above a pool; steelhead are iteroparous and most home to their natal/stocking stream. — [MDNR Steelhead species page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/steelhead)
- Eggs hatch in 4–7 weeks; juveniles smolt at ~8 inches, usually after 2 years in stream (1–3 years range); adults mature after 2–3 years in the lake. — [MDNR Steelhead species page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/steelhead)

**Little Manistee weir (the DNR's steelhead broodstock and its longest run index)**
- The weir blocks fish in spring and fall; steelhead eggs are taken in spring and Chinook eggs in fall, supplying Michigan hatcheries and several other states. — [MDNR Little Manistee River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/little-manistee-river-weir)
- 2026 operation: weir boards and jump screens installed by mid-March to stop upstream migration; fish "usually ripen... during the month of April"; planned egg-take dates April 14, 15, 16, 21, 22, 2026 (9 a.m.–1 p.m.), continuing until quota is reached, "usually... by the middle of April, but may run later depending on weather." — [MDNR news release, Apr 6 2026](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/about/newsroom/releases/2026/04/06/dnr-to-begin-spring-steelhead-egg-collection-on-little-manistee-river-april-14)
- April 15, 2026: overnight storm knocked out power, backup generator failed, and more than 1,700 adult steelhead (~75% of fish held at the weir) died; DNR had planned to collect 4.6 million steelhead eggs; DNR called it "concerning for the local steelhead fishery in the Little Manistee River" but not catastrophic lake-wide. — [Interlochen Public Radio, Apr 15 2026](https://www.interlochenpublicradio.org/ipr-news/2026-04-15/storm-knocks-out-power-to-little-manistee-river-weir-killing-steelhead)
- Because of that power failure, the 2027 steelhead stocking level (normally ~660,000–700,000 annually) is uncertain. — [Ludington Daily News / Shoreline Media](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/chinook-plants-likely-to-be-reduced-beginning-in-2027/article_20122886-2556-44b6-a4b0-b654a3483117.html)
- Weir steelhead counts: just over 6,000 in 2002 to just under 2,000 in 2020. Lake Michigan steelhead population estimated 3.5 million (2011) to just under 3 million (2018). Biologists describe wild steelhead populations as "consistent" while stocked components are "gradually declining"; stream mortality is 4–6x higher than lake mortality; increasing water clarity (less plankton) cited as a driver. — [Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-environment-watch/steelhead-struggle-some-michigan-waters-will-catch-limit-help/)
- No steelhead are stocked by DNR in the Little Manistee; the run "consists mostly of naturally reproduced fish and strays from other rivers." (snippet; full white paper not retrievable) — [Michigan Sea Grant steelhead natural reproduction white paper](https://www.canr.msu.edu/michigan_sea_grant/uploads/files/2021-files/Steelhead%20Natural%20Reproduction%20White%20Paper.pdf); [Little Manistee Watershed Conservation Council](https://lmwcc.org/mdnr.html)
- A 2021 Michigan Sea Grant analysis reported "no evidence of decline in steelhead natural reproduction" (title only; page blocked). — [Michigan Sea Grant](https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/new-analysis-shows-no-evidence-of-decline-in-steelhead-natural-reproduction)

**Regulatory response indicating priority spring-run rivers**
- Effective Jan 9, 2022, the NRC cut the steelhead daily limit from 3 to 1 between March 15 and May 15 on Bear Creek, the Manistee River, Pere Marquette, Muskegon River, Manistique River, and Carp River, citing weir declines and harsh spawning conditions. — [Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-environment-watch/steelhead-bag-limits-reduced-some-michigan-waters-amid-fish-declines/)

**Stocking scale (Lake Michigan basin)**
- 2025 plan: Michigan ~660,000 steelhead smolts plus ~150,000 fall fingerlings; lakewide 1.75 million (up from <1.5 million the prior year). — [Ludington Daily News, 2025 stocking plan](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/2025-lake-michigan-fish-stocking-plan-mostly-at-same-levels/article_d3b189e6-73e2-44a6-bf2f-4b9b63b9cc5a.html)
- Michigan DNR stocked 18.9 million fish of all species through August 2025; the Platte River State Fish Hatchery stocked 31,722 Skamania steelhead obtained from Indiana DNR in 2025 (snippet). — [MDNR news release, Aug 11 2025](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/about/newsroom/releases/2025/08/11/michigan-dnr-has-stocked-18-million-fish-so-far-in-2025)

**Skamania (summer-run) strain — St. Joseph River (shared with Indiana)**
- Indiana DNR: summer-run Skamania return to tributaries beginning mid-to-late June and continue entering through fall and winter (through March); spawning early February through mid-March, peak February. Winter-run (Michigan strain) return beginning mid-late October, bulk of return in February–March, spawning mid-March to mid-April. Skamania are stocked in the St. Joseph River, Trail Creek and the East Branch Little Calumet; fish "stage" near shore and rush upstream "especially after a heavy rainfall." — [Indiana DNR Lake Michigan Fishing](https://www.in.gov/dnr/fish-and-wildlife/fishing/lake-michigan-fishing/)
- Michigan rivers with summer-run fish (angler-media source, secondary): Big Manistee below Tippy Dam (plus Pine, Bear Creek), St. Joseph ("best summer steelhead fishing in Michigan" due to current stocking), Pere Marquette, Grand, Muskegon; the Rogue gets summer fish though DNR does not stock Skamania there. Runs begin June, peak July–August, fish remain until March; pushes follow larger rains and cooler September conditions. — [Trout and Steelhead: Summer Steelhead Michigan](https://troutandsteelhead.net/summer-steelhead-fishing-michigan/)
- Skamania are a Washougal River (WA) origin strain that runs in mid-to-late summer. — [Great Lakes Angler, Michigan's Summer Run](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/michigan-s-summer-run-by-robert-gwizdz); [Getting Bit Guide Service](https://www.gettingbitguideservice.com/skamania-steelhead)

**Temperature/flow drivers**
- An MSU study using St. Joseph River fishway passage data found steelhead migrations corresponded with increasing water temperature and stream flow and built a temperature-based movement rule transferable to other Great Lakes tributaries (snippet). — [MSU thesis](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/31523/OBJ/download)
- Angler-media temperature guidance (not agency data): optimum migration 45–58°F; spawning 36–44°F depending on strain. — [Great Lakes Angler, Finding Fall Steelhead](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/finding-fall-steelhead); [Swing the Fly, Great Lakes Run Timing](https://swingthefly.com/great-lakes-run-timing/)

**Lake Erie / Lake St. Clair tributaries (SE Michigan)**
- Huron River: 50,000–60,000 yearling smolts stocked annually since early 1990s (largest SE Michigan plant); estimated run 3,000–5,000 fish; fall run begins late September with small fish, larger fish in November around 40°F; spring run stronger; Denil ladder at Flat Rock Dam passes ~20–30% of spring fish up to Belleville Dam; run drivers "rain events, dam draw downs, temp drops and the seiche on Lake Erie"; includes strays from Ohio and Pennsylvania. — [Great Lakes Angler, SE Michigan Steelheading](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/southeast-michigan-steelheading-eric-richard)
- Clinton River: 27,000 smolts (37,000 in 2023); fall run mid-October, spring mid-March; "some natural reproduction"; breached Yates Dam lets steelhead into Paint Creek. Belle River: 7,500 smolts, fall entry late Oct–early Nov, peak late March–early April, little/no wild reproduction, supports a St. Clair River fishery. Mill Creek: 10,000 smolts, flashy. SE Michigan streams do not support a lake fishery because of warm water. — [Great Lakes Angler, SE Michigan Steelheading](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/southeast-michigan-steelheading-eric-richard)
- Steelhead are stocked into Mill Creek, Belle River, Clinton River and Huron River in SE Michigan (snippet). — [MDNR Lake Erie/Lake St. Clair CFAC minutes, Oct 2023](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/Boards/LELSCCFAC/minutes/minutes-oct-16-2023.pdf)

**Lake Huron tributaries**
- Au Sable below Foote Dam: steelhead present October through May, best March–early May; Foote Dam blocks migrants so the lower river concentrates steelhead, Chinook, coho and a newer Atlantic salmon run (secondary source). — [Best Fishing in America, Au Sable](https://www.bestfishinginamerica.com/michigan-au-sable-river-fishing.html)
- DNR is concentrating Au Sable stocking on steelhead and Atlantic salmon and running a paired-planting net-pen acclimation study for steelhead near the river mouth. — [Michiana Outdoors News](https://michianaoutdoorsnews.com/news/michigan/932-there-s-always-something-to-learn-about-the-au-sable-river)
- Boardman (Grand Traverse Bay): steelhead, brown trout, lake trout and Atlantic salmon are passed upstream of the weir; DNR continues to stock brown trout, steelhead, Chinook and coho "as needed." — [MDNR Boardman River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/boardman-river-weir)

**Lake Superior tributaries (Upper Peninsula)**
- Spring run April–May with early May typical peak, later than Lake Michigan rivers because of Superior's colder water and UP snowpack; November is the common fall window (broader Sept–Nov). Rivers: Two Hearted ("premier eastern U.P. steelhead river," annual yearling stocking plus natural reproduction), Huron, Ontonagon (East Branch), Dead and Chocolay (Marquette), Presque Isle, Black, Montreal, Union, Firesteel, Silver, Iron-area streams; "strong wild reproduction in many of these streams" (secondary source). — [Trout and Steelhead: Lake Superior](https://troutandsteelhead.net/lake-superior-steelhead-fishing/)
- Two Hearted peaks "mid-May, usually," later than most Michigan steelhead streams; UP streams fish best April–May and briefly in fall before freeze-up (secondary). — [RiverReports Great Lakes steelhead](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/topics/great-lakes-steelhead-fishing)

### Inferences
- For a month-presence quiz: Lake Michigan LP rivers (St. Joseph, Kalamazoo, Grand/Rogue, Muskegon, White, Pere Marquette, Little/Big Manistee, Betsie, Platte, Boardman) should show steelhead present Oct–May with a fall entry pulse Oct–Dec, holdover Dec–Feb, and spawn Mar–Apr (fall fish first). This follows directly from the MDNR statewide statement plus the Little Manistee weir schedule (boards mid-March, ripe fish mid-April).
- St. Joseph is the only Michigan river with a documented, agency-stocked Skamania program; treat summer steelhead there as present Jun–Sep with holdover to Feb–Mar spawn. Summer fish reported in the Big Manistee, Muskegon, Grand, PM and Rogue rest on angler-media reports plus documented straying; flag as "possible/lower confidence."
- Lake Superior tributaries: shift the spring window later (peak early–mid May, tail into late May on the Two Hearted) and compress the fall window to roughly Oct–Nov; treat these as predominantly wild runs.
- Lake Erie/St. Clair tributaries: fall Oct–Nov and spring Mar–Apr, essentially all hatchery fish plus out-of-state strays; no summer presence.
- Lake Huron (Au Sable, Rifle, Thunder Bay): Oct–May presence with a March–early May peak; hatchery-supported.
- Little Manistee count trend (6,000 → <2,000 over 2002–2020) implies the 2021 bag-limit rivers (Manistee, PM, Muskegon, Bear Creek, Manistique, Carp) are the ones the DNR considers most sensitive; the April 2026 weir kill may depress the Little Manistee run for the 2029–2031 return years and may reduce 2027 stocking statewide.

### Gaps
- I found no MDNR-published spawning water temperature for steelhead; the 36–44°F (spawn) and 45–58°F (migration) figures come from angler media. The requester's "~40–50°F" band could not be verified against an agency source.
- Little Manistee weir annual steelhead counts by year (full time series) were not retrievable; only 2002 and 2020 endpoints were found.
- No river-specific weir/ladder counts were found for the Grand (Sixth Street), Muskegon, Pere Marquette, Betsie, Platte, Kalamazoo, Rogue, Rifle, Two Hearted, Chocolay, Carp, UP Huron, Ontonagon, Presque Isle, Black, Escanaba or Ford rivers. Michigan Sea Grant's fin-clip reports (which give wild-vs-hatchery proportions by river) are behind Incapsula and could not be fetched: [2024 fin-clip update](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/interpreting-steelhead-fin-clips-in-michigan-streams-update-for-the-2024-spring-run-msg23-okeefe23), [2022 unmarked steelhead](https://www.michiganseagrant.org/blog/2022/03/18/how-many-stocked-steelhead-returning-to-michigan-rivers-are-still-unmarked/), [2025 skippers blog](https://www.michiganseagrant.org/blog/2025/03/11/skippers-and-super-skippers-what-do-small-steelhead-tell-us-about-future-runs/).
- The Platte hatchery's 2025 Skamania plant (31,722 from Indiana) — where those fish were released was not found.

## Key Question 2: Chinook (king) salmon — rivers, run timing, spawn temps, wild share, weirs, 2024–2027 stocking changes

### Takeaway
Chinook are in Michigan rivers in catchable numbers by mid-August with DNR weir egg takes from mid-September to late October (Little Manistee: first week of October, Sept 29–Oct 8 in 2025); the Lake Michigan population is now majority wild (72% wild in 2024; record 9.2-million wild 2023 year class, mostly from Michigan tributaries), and Michigan is holding stocking at 1 million in 2025–2026 with a reduction likely in 2027 because the Chinook/alewife predator-prey ratio crossed its threshold in 2025.

### Cited Findings

**Timing and rivers (MDNR)**
- "Chinooks begin their upstream migration in late summer and are usually present in catchable numbers by mid-August." Best inland stream fishing: Manistee, Pere Marquette and St. Joseph; "significant stream fishing occurs all along the Lake Michigan coast." Reproduction "in some places produces the bulk of the fishery," but DNR maintains a large stocking program, "cut in recent years because of concerns about alewife populations." Reared at Platte River, Thompson and Wolf Lake hatcheries. — [MDNR Chinook page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/chinook-salmon)
- "DNR facilities begin operations in mid-September and complete the process in late October for all Pacific salmon egg takes"; staff act only when fish are ready and "conditions can vary day to day." Little Manistee Chinook egg take "typically occurs during the first week of October" (2021 goal: 2.5M eggs for Michigan + 600k Indiana + 800k Illinois). — [MDNR news release, Sept 30 2021](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/about/newsroom/releases/2021/09/30/chinook-and-coho-salmon-runs-begin-as-dnr-gears-up-for-egg-takes)
- 2025 season: Little Manistee Chinook eggs collected Sept 29–Oct 8; "We experienced another strong run of wild Chinook salmon this season" and the run "was robust enough to provide for all of Michigan's egg needs." — [Outdoor News, Dec 16 2025](https://www.outdoornews.com/2025/12/16/michigan-dnr-fisheries-staff-collected-more-than-16-million-trout-and-salmon-eggs-this-season/)
- Swan River weir (Presque Isle Co., south of Rogers City, Lake Huron) is "a backup weir" for Chinook eggs when other weirs miss quota; Medusa Creek weir (Charlevoix) diverts returning salmon into a pond for harvest/roe and the creek is "regularly restocked." — [MDNR Swan River and Medusa Creek Weirs](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/swan-river-and-medusa-creek-weirs)
- Swan River egg takes usually occur the first two weeks of October (snippet); in 2015 the DNR took 2.8 million eggs from 692 pairs in a two-day collection. — [MDNR Weirs article](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/0,4570,7-350-79137_79770_79873_80003-448455--,00.html); [Alpena News, Oct 2015](https://www.thealpenanews.com/news/local-news/2015/10/dnr-collects-salmon-eggs-at-swan-river-weir/)
- Boardman River: Chinook stocked annually since 1985; returning salmon are harvested at the weir "during September and October." — [MDNR Boardman River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/boardman-river-weir)
- Michigan's Lake Huron Chinook egg sources are "principally Swan River... and the Little Manistee River" (snippet). — [MDNR Fisheries Research Report 2086](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR2001-RR2101/RR2086.pdf)
- Nunns Creek (Lake Huron, eastern UP) Chinook stocking entries 2021–2023 ranged ~40,000–247,993 per plant (snippet of third-party mirror of DNR data). — [Outdoor Michigan, Nunns Creek stocking](https://outdoormichigan.org/fishstocks/index/12415/0)

**Wild reproduction share**
- "In 2024, wild chinook made up 72% of the Lake Michigan chinook population"; the 2023 wild year class was estimated at 9.2 million, highest since calculations began in 2006 (Salmonid Working Group / GLFC Lake Michigan Committee). Alewife biomass fell a third straight year to ~175 kilotonnes; 2025 mean weight of age-3 female Chinook 14.67 lb, lowest since 2015 (long-term mean 16.97 lb). — [AOL/Journal Sentinel](https://www.aol.com/news/lake-michigan-fishery-producing-great-105509573.html)
- USFWS mass-marking since 2006 finds 50–66% of age-1 Chinook are wild on average (snippet). — [Wisconsin DNR Chinook research](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/Fishing/lakemichigan/Chinooksalmon.html)
- Michigan tributaries with significant natural Chinook reproduction: Muskegon, White, Pere Marquette, Little Manistee, Manistee and Betsie (snippet). — [Michigan Sea Grant, Fast facts](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/fast_facts_on_lake_michigan_salmon_and_trout_msg16_okeefe16)
- Historic baseline: ~630,000 wild Chinook smolts produced in Michigan LP tributaries of Lake Michigan in 1979 = 23% of Michigan-water smolts that year. — [Carl 1982, NAJFM](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1577/1548-8659(1982)2%3C375:NROCSA%3E2.0.CO;2)
- Lake Huron: Chinook fishery ~80% wild, 20% hatchery (snippet). — [Marklevitz et al., J. Great Lakes Res.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0380133016300272)
- After Lake Huron's 2003 alewife collapse, the share of Lake Huron-origin Chinook recovered in Lake Michigan rose from 13% (1993–97) to 82% (2008–14), yet fish "returned to release sites to spawn regardless of their feeding locations" — equivalent to a 30% increase in Lake Michigan stocking. — [USGS, Chinook movements after alewife collapse](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70194654)

**Stocking policy 2006–2027**
- Lakewide Chinook stocking cut 25% in 2006 and 50% in 2013, a further 900,000 later, then multi-species reductions (Chinook, coho, lake trout, brown, rainbow) beginning 2017; PPR target 0.05, upper limit 0.10, red zone ≥0.08. — [GLFC Lake Michigan Salmonine Stocking Strategy, Apr 2025](https://www.glfc.org/pubs/lake_committees/michigan/LMC%20-%20PPR%20Strategy%20April%202025%20Final.pdf)
- Data through 2023 "indicated increased Chinook salmon biomass and declining alewife biomass relative to 2022." — [GLFC PPR Summary, Sept 2024](https://glfc.org/pubs/lake_committees/michigan/Lake%20Michigan%20PPR%20Summary%20for%20Outreach_9-10-2024.pdf)
- DNR proposed an increase in Lake Michigan Chinook stocking for 2023. — [MDNR release, Sept 12 2022](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/about/newsroom/releases/2022/09/12/dnr-proposes-increase-to-chinook-salmon-stocking-in-lake-michigan/)
- 2025 plan: Michigan 1 million Chinook (Wisconsin 1.3M). — [Ludington Daily News, 2025 plan](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/2025-lake-michigan-fish-stocking-plan-mostly-at-same-levels/article_d3b189e6-73e2-44a6-bf2f-4b9b63b9cc5a.html)
- 2026: 1 million again; 2027 reduction likely (e.g., Ludington 75,000 vs 100,000) because the PPR "passed target thresholds in 2025"; alewife are 99% of Lake Michigan Chinook diet; age-3 female weight dropped >2 lb 2024→2025 to below 15 lb. — [Ludington Daily News, 2027 cuts](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/chinook-plants-likely-to-be-reduced-beginning-in-2027/article_20122886-2556-44b6-a4b0-b654a3483117.html)

**Temperature/flow triggers (non-agency sources, lower confidence)**
- Chinook push upstream when nearshore lake temps drop below ~65°F and fall rains raise rivers; spawning mostly 6–15°C (43–59°F), egg survival optimum 8–12°C (46–54°F); peak mid–late September on most Lake Michigan rivers. — [fishing-reports.ai](https://fishing-reports.ai/blog/best-water-temp-king-salmon/); [Michigan Fishing Guide, Fall Salmon Run](https://www.michiganfishing.guide/guides/fall-salmon-run-michigan/)

### Inferences
- Month windows: Chinook present in Lake Michigan LP rivers Aug (late) – Oct, peak mid-Sept to early Oct (weir egg takes Sept 29–Oct 8 in 2025 mark the ripe-fish peak on the Little Manistee); stragglers/spawned-out fish into early November. Lake Huron (Swan, Au Sable, Nunns Creek, Thunder Bay): same window, egg takes first two weeks of October. Boardman: Sept–Oct.
- Given 72% wild and the named wild-producing rivers, the quiz can label Chinook in the Muskegon, White, Pere Marquette, Little Manistee, Big Manistee (Bear Creek) and Betsie as "mostly wild," while Boardman, Medusa Creek, Swan River, Nunns Creek, St. Joseph, Grand and Platte are stocking-anchored (with wild contribution).
- The 2027 stocking cut will not change river presence in 2026–2028 (fish planted 2024–2026 return through ~2029), so month windows are stable for quiz purposes.

### Gaps
- No agency-published spawning temperature for Great Lakes Chinook was found; the 43–59°F range is from a non-primary aggregator.
- Site-level 2025/2026 Chinook stocking numbers by river were not retrieved (they are in the Fish Stocking Database, see KQ7).
- 2025 Swan River egg-take counts and 2025 Little Manistee egg total were not reported in the sources found.
- Lake Superior Chinook (e.g., in the Chocolay, Carp, Huron) were not covered by any source I could retrieve.

## Key Question 3: Coho salmon — rivers, run timing, Platte weir

### Takeaway
Coho runs in Michigan span early September to November depending on tributary, with the Platte River (site of the state coho hatchery and ~50% of Michigan's coho plants) peaking at the mid/late-October egg take (Oct 16–28 in 2025, ~6 million eggs), a notable late-October fishery on the Manistee, and migrants in the St. Joseph as late as Christmas; the fishery is largely stocking-sustained (1.48–1.57 million/year).

### Cited Findings
- "Depending on the tributary, coho spawning runs occur from early September to November." "Coho typically migrate later than the other salmon and travel longer distances. Although natural reproduction has been documented, the fishery is largely sustained through stocking. The bulk of coho are planted in the Platte River, just downstream from the state's coho hatchery." Fish stage in Platte Bay; "a notable fishery in late October in the Manistee River and migrating fish are caught in the St. Joseph River as late as Christmas"; in the UP, "the best fishing is in the Anna River watershed" (Munising Bay). — [MDNR Coho page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/coho-salmon)
- Platte coho egg take "typically occur[s] during the third week of October" (2021 goal 3M Michigan + 1.1M Indiana + 1M Illinois). — [MDNR release, Sept 30 2021](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/about/newsroom/releases/2021/09/30/chinook-and-coho-salmon-runs-begin-as-dnr-gears-up-for-egg-takes)
- 2025: nearly 6 million coho eggs collected at the Platte weir Oct 16–28, ~3 million for Indiana, Illinois and Wisconsin. — [Outdoor News, Dec 16 2025](https://www.outdoornews.com/2025/12/16/michigan-dnr-fisheries-staff-collected-more-than-16-million-trout-and-salmon-eggs-this-season/)
- Under a court-ordered consent decree the DNR passes 20,000 coho above the Platte weir, then harvests the remainder (snippet). — [MDNR Weirs article](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/0,4570,7-350-79137_79770_79873_80003-448455--,00.html)
- Michigan coho stocking by site (2023 plants; Michigan total 1.48M; "typically... right around 1.5 million"): Platte River 772,000; Grand River 248,000; Boardman 104,000; St. Joseph 90,000; Manistee 82,000; Escanaba 48,000; Big Sable 42,000; Muskegon 37,000; Saugatuck (Kalamazoo) 37,000; Galien River 21,000. — [Great Lakes Angler, Coho Mojo](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/coho-mojo-mike-gnatkowski)
- 2025 plan: Michigan 1.57 million coho (lakewide 2.5M); 2027: 1.5 million for Lake Michigan. — [Ludington Daily News 2025](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/2025-lake-michigan-fish-stocking-plan-mostly-at-same-levels/article_d3b189e6-73e2-44a6-bf2f-4b9b63b9cc5a.html); [Ludington Daily News 2027](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/chinook-plants-likely-to-be-reduced-beginning-in-2027/article_20122886-2556-44b6-a4b0-b654a3483117.html)
- Historic Platte weir technical reports exist for 1984–85 operations. — [MDNR Technical Report 85-2](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/TechnicalReports/TR85-2.pdf); [TR 84-8](https://www.midnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/TechnicalReports/TR84-8.pdf)

### Inferences
- Coho month windows: Platte/Betsie Sept–Oct (peak mid–late Oct); Manistee Oct–Nov (peak late Oct); Grand, Muskegon, Kalamazoo, Boardman, Big Sable Oct–Nov; St. Joseph Oct–Dec (into late December). Anna River (Lake Superior) Sept–Oct. Escanaba (Lake Michigan UP) Sept–Oct, stocking-anchored.
- The stocking list is a reasonable proxy for relative coho abundance by river: Platte >> Grand > Boardman > St. Joseph > Manistee > others.

### Gaps
- No Platte weir annual coho return counts (fish handled) were found, only egg totals and the 20,000 pass-through rule.
- Coho spawning temperatures were not found in any retrieved agency source.
- Wild coho contribution by river is not quantified in the sources found.

## Key Question 4: Pink salmon — UP distribution, odd/even years, timing, size

### Takeaway
Pink salmon (accidentally introduced 1955–56 at Thunder Bay, Ontario) run Michigan's Lake Superior and northern Lake Huron/Michigan tributaries from late August through September/early October; the St. Marys rapids is "the most outstanding pink salmon fishery this side of Alaska," odd-year runs remain largest, but even-year runs have existed since 1976–1980 and both now occur; adults are small (2–3 lb).

### Cited Findings
- Pinks are "caught while ascending streams along Lake Superior and northern areas of lakes Huron and Michigan. Streamers and nymphs account for the bulk of the pinks taken in the St. Marys rapids, the most outstanding pink salmon fishery this side of Alaska." Runs "begin in the summer"; eggs hatch late Dec–late Feb, fry leave gravel late April–early May and mature after ~18 months in the lake. Population "slowly growing in numbers and spreading to much of Lake Superior and the northern areas of lakes Huron and Michigan." — [MDNR Pink Salmon page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/pink-salmon)
- Odd-year spawners found in 53 Lake Superior tributaries, 5 northern Lake Huron and 11 northern Lake Michigan tributaries by 1980; even-year fish first appeared 1976 (single fish), present in 9 streams by 1978 and 23 Lake Superior tributaries by 1980, plus 2 streams each in Lakes Huron and Michigan in 1980; 1979 abundance was "phenomenal" with some runs >10,000 fish; even-year runs came from 2- and 3-year-old fish. — [MDNR Fisheries Research Report 1893 (Wagner & Stauffer 1982)](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1893.pdf); [TAFS 1982 abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1577/1548-8659(1982)111%3C523:DAAOPS%3E2.0.CO;2)
- Origin: ~20,000 fingerlings released into the Current River (Thunder Bay, ON) in 1955; largest concentrations in odd years, "yet good runs can occur during even-numbered years as well"; runs are notoriously unpredictable river to river; adults 2–3 lb; late summer/early fall. — [Jerry Dennis, Little Giants](https://jerrydennis.net/1/post/2017/09/saga-of-the-pink-salmon.html)
- Michigan tourism: pinks run late August through September; largest runs in odd years; best in Lake Huron tributaries, the Carp River (SE UP) and the St. Marys River. — [Michigan.org, 4 Types of Salmon Fishing](https://www.michigan.org/article/trip-idea/types-salmon-fishing-pure-michigan)
- A 2026 review, "The Forgotten Pink Salmon in the Laurentian Great Lakes," exists in Fish and Fisheries (not retrievable; 403). — [Langan et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/faf.70084)
- Angler reports conflict on which year is stronger (one forum says even years), illustrating local variability. — [Michigan Sportsman forum](https://www.michigan-sportsman.com/threads/pink-salmon-in-off-years.763615/)

### Inferences
- Quiz window: pink salmon present late Aug–early Oct (peak September) in the St. Marys River (rapids and Sault area), Carp River (Mackinac Co.), and Lake Superior tributaries including the Two Hearted, Sucker, Chocolay and Huron; note "odd years strong, even years present but variable." The Two Hearted/Sucker/Chocolay presence is inferred from MDNR's "streams along Lake Superior" and the 53-tributary survey rather than a named river list.

### Gaps
- No current (2020s) Michigan agency count or river-by-river list of pink runs was found; the most detailed distribution data are from 1967–1980.
- Spawning water temperature for Great Lakes pinks was not found.
- Whether Lake Michigan UP tributaries (Escanaba, Ford, Manistique) currently host pink runs could not be confirmed.

## Key Question 5: Atlantic salmon — St. Marys, Torch Lake, Au Sable, Thunder Bay, Lexington; run timing; which fisheries exist

### Takeaway
The St. Marys River is Michigan's only established Atlantic salmon river fishery (LSSU-reared fish, ~25,000–40,000/yr since 1987), with adults staging below the Sault hydro plant from late June through August and spawning in the rapids in late October–November; the DNR also stocks Torch Lake, and under an experimental program the Au Sable, Thunder Bay River and Lexington Harbor, each with fall (Oct–Dec) returns.

### Cited Findings
- "The primary fishery is in the St. Mary's River, while the state maintains a fishery in Torch Lake (Antrim Co) with stocked fish, as well as Lexington Harbor, Thunder Bay River and Au Sable River each spring under an experimental stocking program... Fall returns of spawning fish occur at each location, primarily in Oct.-Dec." "The St. Mary's River run typically begins in mid-summer and runs until November when spawning commences"; adults may survive and spawn multiple years. — [MDNR Atlantic Salmon page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/atlantic-salmon)
- St. Marys: adults stage behind the Sault Edison (now Cloverland) hydro plant late June–August; Hexagenia hatch mid-July shifts them to insects; spawning late October in the rapids; average 2–8 lb; ~40,000 released annually with a goal of 25,000; 735,600 stocked since 1987 (angler/outfitter source). — [Rivers North](https://riversnorth.net/atlanticsalmon.html)
- LSSU (with Cloverland Electric and MDNR) marked 40 years of St. Marys Atlantic releases; 2026 public release June 4 at the Barch Center for Freshwater Research and Education (100 Salmon Run Way, Sault Ste. Marie). — [LSSU](https://www.lssu.edu/celebrate-40-years-of-lssus-atlantic-salmon-release-on-the-st-marys-river/)
- LSSU raises ~30,000–40,000 Atlantics per year; the program began 1985 (snippet). — [SooToday](https://www.sootoday.com/local-news/lake-superior-state-university-marking-40-years-of-atlantic-salmon-release-12352441)
- Great Lakes Atlantic salmon stocks are maintained by the St. Marys spawning run, with eggs and milt collected at the LSSU laboratory; DNR also rears Atlantics at Harrietta and Platte River hatcheries; Michigan first stocked ~20,000 Atlantics in the Boyne and Au Sable in 1972 (snippet). — [MDNR Showcasing the DNR](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MIDNR/bulletins/608a41)
- Michigan destroyed 31,000 hatchery Atlantic salmon after a bacterial kidney disease outbreak (headline only). — [CBS Detroit](https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/michigan-kills-31000-atlantic-salmon/)
- Au Sable guides report Atlantic salmon in the lower river. — [Streamside Au Sable](https://michigan-streamside.com/atlantic-salmon-in-michigan/)
- Boardman: Atlantic salmon are among species passed above the weir. — [MDNR Boardman River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/boardman-river-weir)

### Inferences
- St. Marys: Atlantic presence Jun–Nov, peak Jul–Aug (tailrace) and late Oct (rapids spawning); 100% stocked (LSSU). Au Sable below Foote, Thunder Bay River (Alpena), Lexington: Oct–Dec presence, experimental, small numbers, stocked. Torch Lake/Torch River: fall Oct–Dec return, stocked; river fishery poorly documented. Boardman: occasional.

### Gaps
- No return counts or creel estimates for Atlantic salmon at any Michigan site were found.
- Current annual Atlantic stocking numbers for the Au Sable, Thunder Bay and Torch Lake (2024–2026) were not retrieved; the "experimental" status may have changed.
- Spawning temperature not found.

## Key Question 6: Brown trout (resident and lake-run), brook trout (incl. coasters), lake trout river entries

### Takeaway
MDNR lists the Au Sable, Pere Marquette and Manistee as premier resident brown trout rivers, with browns spawning in tributaries in September–October and lake-run fish (avg 8 lb) staging at stream mouths from late summer; brook trout are widespread in UP and northern LP headwaters and spawn October–November, with the Salmon Trout River the only south-shore Lake Superior stream with a significant coaster population (coaster stream entry begins mid-July); lake trout spawn on lake shoals and only occasionally run rivers, creating short fall fisheries in drowned river mouths.

### Cited Findings

**Brown trout**
- "Some of Michigan's best known trout streams — such as the Au Sable, the Pere Marquette and the Manistee Rivers — are noted for their brown trout fisheries." Browns prefer 50–65°F in spring; "Since brown trout spawn in tributary streams in September and October, they begin to take up residence near stream outlets in late summer to early fall." Lake-run browns are silver, "the average lake run adult weighs eight pounds." Introduced 1883; "more tolerant of warmer water temperatures than other trout species." — [MDNR Brown Trout page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brown-trout)
- Baldwin River (PM tributary) was the first U.S. river stocked with brown trout (1884). — [Wikipedia, Pere Marquette River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pere_Marquette_River)
- Lake Michigan brown trout stocking: Michigan 306,000 in 2025 (lakewide ~800,000); Grand Haven added as a port; Ludington plants in Pere Marquette Lake; 2027 stocking continues at Ludington, Manistee, Frankfort, Menominee, Grand Haven. — [Ludington Daily News 2025](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/2025-lake-michigan-fish-stocking-plan-mostly-at-same-levels/article_d3b189e6-73e2-44a6-bf2f-4b9b63b9cc5a.html); [Ludington Daily News 2027](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/chinook-plants-likely-to-be-reduced-beginning-in-2027/article_20122886-2556-44b6-a4b0-b654a3483117.html)
- DNR captive broodstock produced 2.6 million brown trout eggs and 1.5 million rainbow trout eggs in 2025 (resident stream/inland stocking source). — [Outdoor News, Dec 2025](https://www.outdoornews.com/2025/12/16/michigan-dnr-fisheries-staff-collected-more-than-16-million-trout-and-salmon-eggs-this-season/)
- Lodge/guide sources: lake-run browns follow steelhead and salmon up the Pere Marquette to feed on eggs; fall steelhead and lake-run browns "start to show in late October and November." — [Barothy Lodge](https://barothylodge.com/fishing/); [Betts Guide Service](https://bettsguideservice.com/early-salmon-on-the-pere-marquette-manistee/)
- Anglers report lake-run browns are "a lot more rare today" on Lake Michigan tributaries than in the past (forum; low confidence). — [Michigan Sportsman forum](https://www.michigan-sportsman.com/threads/lake-run-brown-trout-lake-michigan.710083/)
- Boardman: brown trout are passed upstream of the weir and stocked in the system. — [MDNR Boardman River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/boardman-river-weir)

**Brook trout and coasters**
- Native state fish; "widespread across the northern portion of the state, especially in the Upper Peninsula, where any stream that runs clear and cold is likely to hold" brook trout; the Black River system (NE Lower Peninsula) is "among the best brook trout streams"; by summer fish move to headwaters seeking colder water; "Spawning generally occurs in the months of October and November" on gravel in spring-fed streams or groundwater seepages; stream fish 7–9 in; coasters up to 25 in/10 lb. — [MDNR Brook Trout page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brook-trout)
- Coaster restoration areas (2015, 10-year regulation, 20-inch minimum, 1 fish): Marquette Co. Iron, Big Huron, Little Huron, Big Garlic rivers; Houghton Co. Pilgrim River and Portage/Torch Lake system; Baraga Co. Silver, Slate, Ravine rivers. "Spawning migrations into streams begin in mid-July." The Salmon Trout River (Marquette Co.) is "often cited as the only remaining stream on the south shore of Lake Superior with a significant population of coasters" (18-inch minimum, 1 fish, shortened season). — [The Mining Journal, Apr 2015](https://www.miningjournal.net/news/front-page-news/2015/04/coaster-brook-trout-restoration-areas-created/)
- Salmon Trout River coasters: 70% increase in adult numbers since 2002, still "relatively small"; species threatened in Michigan; ~$1M habitat work. — [Superior Watershed Partnership](https://superiorwatersheds.org/coaster-brook-trout)
- Coasters once occurred in >100 Lake Superior tributaries; few populations remain in Michigan, Wisconsin and Minnesota. — [Wisconsin DNR coaster page](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/Fishing/lakesuperior/cbrktrout.html); [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70045323)
- Brook trout thermal thresholds: optimal growth 11–16°C, movement threshold 18°C, lethal 25.3°C (Michigan fragmented-stream study). — [TAFS 2025](https://academic.oup.com/tafs/article/154/1/7/8081929)
- Spawning physiology: males mature up to 19°C; ovulation/spawning at 16°C and lower; spawning early Oct–Dec, peak mid-Nov in the study population (lab/general, not Michigan-specific). — [Hokanson et al. 1973](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/f73-158)
- Groundwater withdrawal and land use affect brook charr thermal habitat in two Michigan coldwater tributaries. — [Hydrobiologia 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10750-010-0204-0)
- White River (MI) watershed drone thermal-IR mapping of brook trout refugia (groundwater seeps, cold tributary confluences, headwaters). — [Midwest CASC](https://mwcasc.umn.edu/node/226)
- GVSU thesis on brook trout behavioral thermoregulation in a small Michigan stream. — [GVSU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/theses/865/)
- DNR captive broodstock: 333,500 brook trout eggs in 2025. — [Outdoor News, Dec 2025](https://www.outdoornews.com/2025/12/16/michigan-dnr-fisheries-staff-collected-more-than-16-million-trout-and-salmon-eggs-this-season/)

**Lake trout**
- "Lake trout spawn in the fall, often on shoals and reefs but some migrate upstream creating angling opportunities on piers... Similarly, short-term fisheries are created in the drowned river mouths just inshore of the Great Lakes and in rivers, where lake trout often are caught incidentally by steelhead fishermen." Prefer 40–55°F; shallow (10–30 ft) in fall, winter, spring; mature at 6–7 years. — [MDNR Lake Trout page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/lake-trout)
- Lake trout "seldom enter tributary streams and rivers" (angler source). — [Lake Michigan Angler](https://lakemichiganangler.com/pages/tributary-river-fishing-tips-and-techniques)
- The St. Marys rapids historically were spawning/nursery grounds for lake trout, whitefish, cisco, burbot, walleye and sturgeon (snippet). — [USGS/JGLR sturgeon paper](https://www.usgs.gov/index.php/publications/lake-sturgeon-acipenser-fulvescens-spawn-st-marys-river-rapids-michigan)
- Boardman: lake trout are passed above the weir. — [MDNR Boardman River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/boardman-river-weir)
- Lake Michigan lake trout stocking: Michigan >2 million of 2.11M lakewide in 2025; all plants from Grand Haven south eliminated. — [Ludington Daily News 2025](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/2025-lake-michigan-fish-stocking-plan-mostly-at-same-levels/article_d3b189e6-73e2-44a6-bf2f-4b9b63b9cc5a.html)

### Inferences
- Resident browns: year-round in the Au Sable, Manistee, PM, Boardman, Muskegon (below Croton), Rogue, Rifle, UP rivers (Escanaba, Ford, Ontonagon); spawning behavior/coloration Oct–Nov (MDNR says Sept–Oct for tributary spawning). Lake-run browns: Sept–Nov at Lake Michigan stream mouths and lower rivers (PM, Manistee, Betsie, Boardman, Grand Haven area), present but declining/uncommon; also Lake Huron (Thunder Bay, Au Sable) at low density (inferred from stocking ports; not river-documented).
- Brook trout: year-round in UP streams and northern LP headwaters (Black River NE LP, upper Au Sable/Manistee tribs, Two Hearted, Fox); best May–Sept; spawning Oct–Nov. Coasters: Salmon Trout River plus restoration-area streams, entering from mid-July through fall spawning (Oct–Nov); very low numbers, restrictive regs.
- Lake trout in rivers: treat as incidental Oct–Nov in drowned river mouths and lower rivers (Manistee Lake, Pere Marquette Lake, Betsie, Boardman, St. Marys), not as a target species.

### Gaps
- No agency source quantifying lake-run brown trout runs in any Michigan river; presence is inferred from stocking ports and guide reports.
- No agency-published brown trout spawning temperature for Michigan.
- Coaster run size for the Salmon Trout River (absolute numbers) and current (post-2025) status of the 10-year restoration regulation were not found.
- No documentation of lake trout runs into specific Lake Superior tributaries in Michigan.

## Key Question 8: Documented trends — later/earlier runs, alewife collapse effects, brook trout thermal habitat

### Takeaway
Agency data show a majority-wild Chinook population under alewife-driven stocking cuts and a steelhead decline tied to declining lake productivity; the claim that Great Lakes steelhead now run later in warm falls and spawn earlier in spring comes from angler media and biologists quoted therein rather than a retrieved peer-reviewed Michigan study, and brook trout thermal thresholds (18°C movement, 25°C lethal) are documented in Michigan streams with active thermal-refugia mapping.

### Cited Findings
- Steelhead: Little Manistee weir counts fell ~6,000 (2002) to <2,000 (2020); Lake Michigan steelhead 3.5M (2011) to <3M (2018); drivers cited: increasing water clarity/lower plankton, high in-stream mortality; wild component stable, stocked component declining. — [Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-environment-watch/steelhead-struggle-some-michigan-waters-will-catch-limit-help/)
- Chinook: Lake Huron alewife collapse (2003) shifted Lake Huron-origin Chinook to feed in Lake Michigan (13% to 82%) while still spawning at home rivers. — [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70194654)
- Lake Michigan Chinook 72% wild (2024), alewife down to ~175 kt, Chinook weights falling; stocking cut planned for 2027; multi-species cuts since 2017. — [AOL/Journal Sentinel](https://www.aol.com/news/lake-michigan-fishery-producing-great-105509573.html); [Ludington Daily News 2027](https://www.shorelinemedia.net/ludington_daily_news/news/chinook-plants-likely-to-be-reduced-beginning-in-2027/article_20122886-2556-44b6-a4b0-b654a3483117.html); [GLFC 2025 strategy](https://www.glfc.org/pubs/lake_committees/michigan/LMC%20-%20PPR%20Strategy%20April%202025%20Final.pdf)
- Run timing shift (angler media quoting biologists): steelhead runs "timed differently today than 30 years ago," warm falls delay or halt fall runs, and biologists report "spawning earlier than ever before" in spring over the past decade. — [Great Lakes Angler, That Temperature Thing](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/that-temperature-thing-for-steelhead-matt-straw); [Great Lakes Angler, Finding Fall Steelhead](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/finding-fall-steelhead)
- Bioenergetics: under warming, lake trout and steelhead are projected to fare better than Chinook in Lakes Michigan/Huron. — [Temperature effects on salmonines, Lakes Michigan and Huron](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272946022_Temperature_effects_induced_by_climate_change_on_the_growth_and_consumption_by_salmonines_in_Lakes_Michigan_and_Huron); [Predicted growth of lake trout and Chinook in a warming lake](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0380133024000376)
- Brook trout: Midwest warming threatens brook trout; thresholds 11–16°C optimal, 18°C movement, 25.3°C lethal; thermal refugia (seeps, cold tributaries, headwaters) mapped by drone in Michigan's White River watershed. — [TAFS 2025](https://academic.oup.com/tafs/article/154/1/7/8081929); [Midwest CASC](https://mwcasc.umn.edu/node/226)
- Boardman: Sabin Dam removed 2018; Union Street Dam and its 1987 ladder replaced by FishPass in 2025 (a complete barrier with selective sorting), capping a 20-year reconnection project; anadromous trout/salmon habitat extends upstream. — [MDNR Boardman River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/boardman-river-weir); [Interlochen Public Radio](https://www.interlochenpublicradio.org/news/2020-01-17/traverse-city-will-be-first-in-the-world-to-sort-fish-for-passage-upriver)

### Inferences
- For the quiz, fall steelhead windows on Lake Michigan and Lake Erie tribs should be treated as flow/temperature-conditional (a warm, dry October can shift meaningful entry to November), while spring spawn windows are relatively fixed by the mid-March to mid-April Little Manistee schedule.
- Chinook river presence is unlikely to shrink through 2028 despite stocking cuts, because wild production is now the larger component and 2023 was a record wild year class.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Michigan-specific study quantifying a multi-decade shift in steelhead or salmon run timing was retrieved; the shift is asserted by angler media.
- Whether the 2027 Chinook cut is finalized (and its size) was still described as "likely" as of the latest article found.
