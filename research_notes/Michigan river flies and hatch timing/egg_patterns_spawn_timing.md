# Egg Patterns, Egg-Eating Behavior, and Spawn Timing for Michigan Rivers (with chuck-and-duck and indicator rigs)

Research date: 2026-09-28. Scope: Michigan rivers. Non-Michigan Great Lakes sources are labeled "(non-MI context)". "Documented biology" = agency/peer-reviewed; "angler lore" = guide/shop/forum observation.

---

## Key Question 1: Which species spawn in Michigan rivers, when (by region), with what temperature triggers, and which eggs do trout/steelhead actually key on?

### Takeaway
Michigan rivers carry a near-continuous supply of drifting eggs from late August (pink/Chinook) through late May (suckers, walleye, late steelhead): Pacific salmon and brown/brook/lake trout in fall; Atlantic salmon in late October (St. Marys); steelhead March–April (fall-run fish first, spring-run fish second); walleye in April; white/longnose suckers and redhorse from late April into late May. Guides and DNR-adjacent sources agree steelhead "key on King Salmon eggs big time" behind spawning salmon, and that spring steelhead/trout eat sucker and walleye eggs concurrently with steelhead spawning.

### Cited Findings

**Chinook (king) salmon — fall (late Aug–Oct), documented**
- Michigan DNR: "DNR facilities begin operations in mid-September and complete the process in late October for all Pacific salmon egg takes." Chinook egg takes on the Little Manistee River "typically occur during the first week of October"; coho egg takes at the Platte River "typically occur during the third week of October." — [Michigan DNR press release, Sept 30 2021](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/about/newsroom/releases/2021/09/30/chinook-and-coho-salmon-runs-begin-as-dnr-gears-up-for-egg-takes)
- Little Manistee weir: installed "on, or about August 15th, and removed on, or about November 15th"; "Chinook salmon eggs are taken usually during the first two weeks of October." All salmon (coho, chinook, "rarely pink") reaching the weir are harvested; steelhead and brown trout arriving in fall are passed upstream. — [Michigan DNR, Little Manistee River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/little-manistee-river-weir)
- Run trigger (DNR biologist quoted, Grand River/southern LP): "If temperatures are in the 60s the fish will come upstream," per Jay Wesley, Lake Michigan Basin coordinator; cold fronts and September rain accelerate the run, warm dry falls delay it. — [Experience Grand Rapids (search snippet quoting Wesley)](https://www.experiencegr.com/articles/post/grand-river-coho-salmon-fishing/)
- Pere Marquette guide report: a late-August drop in Lake Michigan surface temperature ("flipped") triggered salmon entering the river; "Steelhead key on King Salmon eggs big time"; steelhead "sit behind the spawning salmon in the deeper pockets... behind the gravel." (angler lore) — [Betts Guide Service PM report](https://bettsguideservice.com/pere-marquette-river-salmon-fishing-report/)

**Coho salmon — fall (early Sept–Nov), documented**
- "Depending on the tributary, coho spawning runs occur from early September to November"; notable late-October fishery in the Manistee River; both adults die after spawning; eggs hatch the next spring. — [Michigan DNR, Coho salmon](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/coho-salmon)

**Pink salmon — late Aug–Sept, mostly UP/northern Lake Huron, documented + lore**
- DNR: "Pink salmon spawning runs begin in the summer in the Great Lakes when they ascend the small streams and rivers of their origin"; eggs hatch "late December to late February"; fish mature after ~18 months in the lake. — [Michigan DNR, Pink salmon](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/pink-salmon)
- Introduced accidentally in 1955 (~20,000 BC fingerlings released into the Current River near Thunder Bay, Ontario); within ten years "observed spawning in the rapids of the St. Mary's River"; "In the Great Lakes it spawns in the largest concentrations in odd-numbered years, yet good runs can occur during even-numbered years as well"; St. Marys hosts the largest runs ("tens of thousands... some years"); spawn "in late summer and early fall." — [Jerry Dennis, "Little Giants"](https://jerrydennis.net/1/post/2017/09/saga-of-the-pink-salmon.html)
- Odd/even nuance is contested in angler sources: one Soo-area article says even years are the "ON" year with a 3–5 day best window in odd years, while most sources say odd years are largest; timing "just after Labor Day." (angler lore; conflicting) — [AA-Fishing, Pink Salmon in the Soo](https://www.aa-fishing.com/mi/mi-fishing-article-salmon-soo.html); contradicted by [Jerry Dennis](https://jerrydennis.net/1/post/2017/09/saga-of-the-pink-salmon.html)
- Best Michigan pink locations: Lake Huron tributaries, the Carp River (SE UP), and the St. Marys River; runs "late August through September." (Pure Michigan tourism, secondary) — [michigan.org](https://www.michigan.org/article/trip-idea/types-salmon-fishing-pure-michigan)

**Atlantic salmon — St. Marys River, spawn late Oct–Nov, documented**
- DNR: "Fall returns of spawning fish occur at each location, primarily in Oct."; the St. Marys run "typically begins in mid-summer and runs until November when spawning commences"; eggs/milt collected at Lake Superior State University; adults may spawn multiple years. — [Michigan DNR, Atlantic salmon](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/atlantic-salmon)
- Guide: adults arrive from Lake Huron "late every June," stage below the Sault Edison hydro plant June–August; "Late October marks the spawning period in the St. Marys rapids." — [Rivers North, St. Marys Atlantic Salmon](https://riversnorth.net/atlanticsalmon.html)
- LSSU/DNR/Edison Sault program began 1985; ~40,000 Atlantics reared/released annually by LSSU's Aquatic Research Laboratory. — [LSSU](https://www.lssu.edu/celebrate-40-years-of-lssus-atlantic-salmon-release-on-the-st-marys-river/)

**Steelhead — enter Oct–May, spawn March–April (fall-run first), documented**
- DNR: "Lake steelhead enter their spawning streams from late October to early May... spawning does not occur until spring. The fall-run fish are typically the first to spawn, often in March, followed by the spring run fish in April." Eggs hatch in 4–7 weeks depending on temperature; steelhead may spawn multiple times. — [Michigan DNR, Steelhead](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/steelhead)
- Little Manistee weir (steelhead egg take, the state's only steelhead egg-take, "Michigan winter-run strain"): weir installed "during the first two weeks of March." — [Michigan DNR, Little Manistee River Weir](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/hatcheries/little-manistee-river-weir)
- Temperature (angler observation, not agency): "Active spawning begins near 42°F"; migrations begin around 36–38°F. — [Great Lakes Angler, Matt Straw (search snippet)](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/finding-veins-of-silver-optimum-conditions-for-steelhead-matt-straw)
- Southern/central LP timing (angler): White River (Hesperia), Rogue (Rockford), Pere Marquette "going strong by late February and early March"; Muskegon fish "remain active well into May." — [RiverReports Great Lakes steelhead (snippet)](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/topics/great-lakes-steelhead-fishing); [Betts Guide Service](https://bettsguideservice.com/michigan-steelhead-fishing-reports-231-519-7348/)
- Northern LP (Manistee): steelhead present "from late September to well into May." (guide) — [Hawkins Outfitters](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/manistee-river-steelhead-fishing/)
- UP: "Spring Steelhead begins in early April, and... usually fish are available until mid-May"; fall begins mid-October, "Rainfall will dictate the Fall fishing." (guide) — [Rivers North, U.P. Steelhead](https://riversnorth.net/steelhead.html). Two Hearted spring run "typically peak[s] in early May"; fall peak November. (angler) — [DIY Fly Fishing, Two Hearted](https://diyflyfishing.com/two-hearted-river-michigan/); [Caddis Shack Guide Service](https://www.caddisshackguideservice.com/blog/steelhead-fly-fishing-in-the-upper-peninsula)

**Skamania (summer-run) steelhead — St. Joseph / Manistee, documented+lore**
- DNR biologist Jay Wesley (via Great Lakes Angler): Manistee below Tippy Dam is "the most dependable summer-run steelhead fishery in the state"; Michigan receives Skamania stock from Indiana for Manistee plantings; St. Joseph (below Berrien Springs) historically productive but "currently unpredictable"; Rogue has an unstocked summer run; typical arrival "first or second week of August" (as early as second week of July in 2019); fish remain through November–December. — [Great Lakes Angler, Gwizdz](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/michigan-s-summer-run-by-robert-gwizdz)
- St. Joe angler sources: Skamania appear May–June and stay to end of September; move into colder tributaries when mainstem exceeds ~67°F; spawning "early February through mid-March, with the peak occurring in February," spent fish return to the lake March–April. (angler/guide; source of the Feb spawn claim is a search-snippet aggregate, treat as lore) — [DIY Fly Fishing, St. Joseph](https://diyflyfishing.com/st-joseph-river-michigan/); [Trout & Steelhead, St. Joseph](https://troutandsteelhead.net/st-joseph-river-steelhead-fishing/); [Indiana DNR Lake Michigan fishing](https://www.in.gov/dnr/fish-and-wildlife/fishing/lake-michigan-fishing/)

**Brown trout — Sept–Oct (lake-run) into Dec, documented**
- DNR: "brown trout spawn in tributary streams in September and October"; lake-run browns stage near stream outlets late summer–early fall; prefer 50–65°F in spring. — [Michigan DNR, Brown trout](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brown-trout)
- Other sources extend the season "October into December" (secondary). — [Yooper Webcam brown trout page (snippet)](https://yooperwebcam.com/fishing-database-mi/brown-trout/)

**Brook trout — Oct–Nov, documented**
- DNR: "Spawning generally occurs in the months of October and November"; up to 5,000 eggs per female; incubation 3–4 months. — [Michigan DNR, Brook trout](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brook-trout)

**Lake trout — fall, mostly shoals but some river-run, documented**
- DNR: "Lake trout spawn in the fall, often on shoals and reefs but some migrate upstream creating angling opportunities on piers"; "fall season broadcast spawning fish"; eggs deposited after dark among cobble; prefer 40–55°F. — [Michigan DNR, Lake trout](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/lake-trout)

**Walleye — April, documented (Detroit River, SE Michigan)**
- USGS/USFWS study: "Walleye spawning peaked during the week of April 12–19" as water rose from 6.5 to 9.0°C; walleye spawned "during a 3-week increase in water temperature from 3.5 to 9°C." — [Manny et al. 2010, J. Great Lakes Res. 36:490–496](https://jamieschmale.ca/walleye/2010_Manny.pdf)

**White sucker / longnose sucker — late April–May, documented**
- Same study: "white sucker spawning peaked on May 10, 3 weeks after peak" walleye; "Suckers spawned during a 4-week increase in water temperature from 6 to 10°C between April 26 and May 17." — [Manny et al. 2010](https://jamieschmale.ca/walleye/2010_Manny.pdf)
- Michigan Sea Grant (West Michigan creeks): "White and longnose suckers are responding to a temperature cue of 43.3°F to initiate their spawning runs, according to research conducted in Michigan." (search snippet; page blocked to fetch; also mirrored at MSU Extension) — [Michigan Sea Grant blog, Mar 20 2025](https://www.michiganseagrant.org/blog/2025/03/20/spring-brings-spawning-fish-into-west-michigan-streams-and-you-can-help-to-monitor-spawning-runs-in-local-creeks/); [MSU Extension mirror](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/spring-brings-spawning-fish-into-west-michigan-streams-and-you-can-help-to-monitor-spawning-runs-in-local-creeks-msg25-okeefe25)
- General: white suckers "breed in the spring, usually from April to early May"; other sources "early April when the water reaches 7°C (45°F)." — [Animal Diversity Web, C. commersonii (snippet)](https://www.animaldiversity.org/accounts/Catostomus_commersonii/); [NatureNorth (snippet)](http://www.naturenorth.com/spring/creature/whsucker/Fwhsuck2.html)
- Pennsylvania (non-MI context): George Daniel says white suckers spawn "when water temperatures reach 50°F," with spawning "beginning in March and extending into late May." — [Hatch Magazine, "The sucker spawn"](https://www.hatchmag.com/articles/sucker-spawn/7715199)

**Redhorse — April–late May, general biology (non-MI specific numbers)**
- Michigan Sea Grant notes different redhorse species arrive at different times in West Michigan tributaries, some still present in late May; silver redhorse may migrate up from Lake Michigan or the Grand River into creeks like Plaster Creek. (snippet) — [Michigan Sea Grant blog](https://www.michiganseagrant.org/blog/2025/03/20/spring-brings-spawning-fish-into-west-michigan-streams-and-you-can-help-to-monitor-spawning-runs-in-local-creeks/)
- Shorthead redhorse spawn March–June at 7–16°C (45–61°F); golden redhorse at 17–22°C in April–May, often at night. (Wikipedia summaries) — [Shorthead redhorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorthead_redhorse); [Golden redhorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_redhorse)
- Michigan regs define "suckers" as longnose, white, northern hog, spotted suckers, all redhorse except protected River Redhorse, buffalo, lake chubsucker, quillback. — [2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations, NOTE 2](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/LED/digests/2026-Michigan-Fishing-Regulations_web_accessible.pdf)

**Which eggs fish key on (guide consensus, angler lore)**
- Fall: steelhead and browns hold behind spawning salmon eating drifting salmon eggs; "Larger eggs drift downstream, attracting steelhead." — [Current Works (Traverse City), Egg Patterns](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/egg-patterns-matching-hatch-steelhead/)
- Spring: during March–April steelhead spawning, "walleyes, resident rainbows, suckers, and carp also utilize rivers, creating abundant egg sources"; sucker spawn patterns in pale yellow imitate sucker and walleye eggs. — [True North Trout / Ted Kraimer](https://truenorthtrout.com/2009/10/egg-flies-matching-the-hatch-for-steelhead/)
- UP fall: "Steelhead are heavily feeding on the large amount of Salmon eggs." — [Rivers North](https://riversnorth.net/steelhead.html)
- Muskegon spring: steelhead "feed heavily on salmon fry, stoneflies, and eggs" late March–April. — [Betts Guide Service, Muskegon (snippet)](https://bettsguideservice.com/best-times-fish-muskegon-river.html)

### Inferences
- Regional windows (synthesized from the above; DNR gives statewide ranges, regional splits come from guide sources): Southern LP (St. Joe, Grand, Kalamazoo): Chinook/coho late Aug–Oct; steelhead fall run Oct–Dec, spawn late Feb–April; Skamania in river June–Nov. Northern LP (PM, Manistee, Betsie, Platte, Muskegon): Chinook peak late Sept–mid Oct, coho to early Nov, steelhead spawn March–April, suckers late April–May. UP (Two Hearted, St. Marys, Carp): pinks late Aug–Sept (odd years biggest), Atlantics late Oct, steelhead fall mid-Oct–Nov and spring early April–mid May (peak early May).
- Temperature ladder: salmon enter when rivers/lake drop into the 60s°F; steelhead spawn ~42°F+; walleye 3.5–9°C (38–48°F); suckers 6–10°C (43–50°F). Sucker spawn thus overlaps the tail of steelhead spawning, which is why "sucker spawn" flies fish for late spring steelhead and drop-back fish.
- Lake trout eggs are a minor river food source in Michigan (mostly shoal spawners); brook trout eggs matter mainly on small inland trout streams, not steelhead rivers.

### Gaps
- No Michigan agency source giving a numeric water temperature range for Chinook or steelhead spawning in Michigan rivers was retrieved (michigan.gov blocked automated fetches for most pages; only species pages and two hatchery pages were parsed). The 42°F steelhead and "60s" Chinook figures are from an outdoor writer and a quoted biologist, respectively.
- Michigan Sea Grant's 43.3°F sucker cue could only be captured from a search snippet; the underlying Michigan study (an MSU thesis at d.lib.msu.edu/etd/25060) was access-restricted.
- Skamania spawn timing in Michigan (Feb–March claim) is from angler aggregators, not DNR.
- Pink salmon odd/even dominance is stated inconsistently between sources; no DNR statement on it was found.

## Key Question 2: Egg sizes and colors by species, and how eggs change after fertilization/death

### Takeaway
Documented sizes: Chinook ~6.4–8.4 mm (mean 7.2 mm in a landlocked population), white sucker ~2–3 mm (3.0–3.1 mm measured in the Detroit River), walleye ~2.0–2.1 mm, Atlantic salmon ~5.6 mm, brown trout ~4–5 mm; coho, pink, steelhead and lake trout sizes were only found in secondary/vendor sources (coho/pink ~6–8 mm; rainbow 3–7 mm). Egg color comes from dietary carotenoids and ranges pale yellow-orange to dark red-orange; unfertilized/dead eggs turn "a milky shade with patches of color," which is the biological basis for "dead egg"/cream/washed-out patterns.

### Cited Findings
**Sizes (documented)**
- Chinook (landlocked fall Chinook, Lake Oahe SD): mean egg diameter 7.22 mm, range 6.36–8.36 mm. — [Krebs et al. 2018 (Allied Academies)](https://www.alliedacademies.org/articles/landlocked-fall-chinook-salmon-egg-size-is-positively-related-to-hatching-time-10971.html)
- Walleye 2.0–2.1 mm; white sucker 3.0–3.1 mm; other (unidentified) 1.4–1.5 mm, all measured in the Detroit River. — [Manny et al. 2010](https://jamieschmale.ca/walleye/2010_Manny.pdf)
- White sucker "20,000 and 50,000 eggs, about 2 to 3 mm in diameter"; eggs sticky, sink, attach to gravel; hatch in 5–7 days. — [Animal Diversity Web (snippet)](https://www.animaldiversity.org/accounts/Catostomus_commersonii/)
- Atlantic salmon (Miramichi, NB): one-sea-winter females' eggs averaged 5.6 mm. — [ICES J. Mar. Sci. 69:1678 (snippet)](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/69/9/1678/639366)
- Brown trout eggs "large (4–5 mm diameter)." — [MDB fish fact sheet (snippet)](https://www.mdb.fish/fish-fact-sheets/brown-trout); a second source gives dry brown trout eggs 3.7–5.2 mm — [Guide Recommended (snippet)](https://guiderecommended.com/color-of-brown-trout-eggs/)
- Rainbow trout eggs "relatively large in diameter (3–7 mm)" (aquaculture reference). — [The Fish Site](https://thefishsite.com/articles/cultured-aquaculture-species-rainbow-trout)
- Pacific salmon eggs range "from ¼ inch (Sockeye Salmon) to ½ inch (Chum"; "The red color in eggs come from carotenoids"; Chinook eggs hatch around 12 weeks; eggs are "an important food source for other fish." — [USFWS Pacific Salmon Life Stage Cheat Sheet](https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/06_Life%20Stage%20Cheat%20Sheet.pdf)
- Brook trout eggs "typically falling between 4.1 and 4.6 millimeters" (secondary, unverified). — [Biology Insights (snippet)](https://biologyinsights.com/what-do-trout-eggs-look-like-size-color-and-more/)

**Sizes (vendor/angler)**
- Coho roe "6–8mm"; pink roe "6–8mm" (bead vendor sizing guidance). — [Stone Cold Beads, Bead Fishing Basics (snippet)](https://stonecoldbeads.com/bead-fishing-basics/)
- Guide egg-fly sizing: "Yarn Egg/Glo Bug 6mm–14mm"; large 10–14 mm in murky water, small 6–8 mm in gin-clear water. — [Trout & Steelhead, Best Egg Flies (Graham Bristow)](https://troutandsteelhead.net/best-egg-flies-for-steelhead/)

**Color (documented)**
- "The red color of eggs comes from carotenoids (antioxidant pigments) that the salmon get from their diet"; colors vary by species "from pale yellowish-orange to dark reddish-orange"; unfertilized eggs "lose their bright appearance and turn a milky shade with patches of color." — [USGS FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/why-do-salmon-eggs-come-different-colors)
- Sucker eggs: "a light golden color to a pale yellow," laid in clusters (PA observation, non-MI). — [Hatch Magazine, George Daniel](https://www.hatchmag.com/articles/sucker-spawn/7715199)

**Color change and "dead egg" logic (Michigan guides, angler lore)**
- After drifting or settling in gravel, "eggs become less colorful, a bit translucent and eventually opaque," so pattern choice depends on time since spawning; guides advise examining real salmon eggs at access sites. — [Current Works, Egg Patterns](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/egg-patterns-matching-hatch-steelhead/)
- Natural references: spawning brown trout eggs "bright orange"; steelhead eggs "translucent yellow to yellow-orange"; dead eggs "cream and light pink (fade over time)." — [MidCurrent, How to Tie Egg Flies](https://midcurrent.com/v2/how-to-tie-egg-flies-salmon-steelhead/)
- "Most fish eggs are bright yellow, orange, or pinkish red when laid, and generally turn either milky white or translucent yellow/white over time." — [FlyDeal Flies, Sucker Spawn category (snippet)](https://www.flydealflies.com/product-category/half-buck-fly/steelhead-flies/egg-flies/)

### Inferences
- The angler shorthand "Chinook 7–8 mm orange, coho 6–7 mm, steelhead 4–5 mm, brown 4–5 mm, sucker 2–3 mm" is consistent with the documented values found (Chinook 7.2 mm mean; brown 4–5; sucker 2–3/3.0), but coho and steelhead figures rest on vendor/aquaculture ranges rather than a Great Lakes measurement.
- Because dead/unfertilized eggs go milky with residual color patches, veiled patterns (Nuke Egg, Otter's milking egg) and cream/"dead egg" yarn colors imitate the majority of drifting eggs, which are non-viable ones washed out of redds.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve Beacham & Murray 1993 (Pacific salmon egg size by species) or Jastrebski 2009 (lake trout egg size) abstracts (publisher 403). No peer-reviewed Great Lakes steelhead egg-diameter value was captured; the 1989 Lake Superior steelhead fecundity paper reports egg counts, not diameters.
- No documented lake trout or pink salmon egg diameter beyond vendor ranges.
- Hareline/Wapsi official color lists (Glo Bug yarn, McFly Foam) could not be fetched before the search budget ran out; color names below come from guide articles and a retailer listing.

## Key Question 3: Egg pattern families used in Michigan, with hooks, colors, materials, and the most-recommended patterns per river

### Takeaway
Michigan shops and guides converge on a short list: Nuke Egg (McFly Foam or Glo Bug yarn core with a yarn/Egg Veil shroud) and Glo Bug on #8–#12 short-shank scud/egg hooks (Gamakatsu C14S, TMC 2457, TMC 105, Daiichi 1130), Estaz eggs, Sucker Spawn/Crystal Meth/Clown "rag" eggs, plus Otter's Soft Milking Eggs and beads (beads only where legal). Signature Michigan color combos are Oregon Cheese over Steelhead Orange, Apricot Supreme over Steelhead Orange, Egg over Sockeye, Pink Lady over Shrimp Pink, and attractors Chartreuse over Flame / Flame over Cerise; natural apricot/cream/pale pink in clear water, chartreuse/orange/hot pink/clown in high or stained water.

### Cited Findings
**Nuke Egg (Michigan recipes)**
- Current Works (Traverse City) recipe: Hook TMC 105 #6–10, TMC 2457 #8–12, or Gamakatsu C14S #8–12; thread Uni 6/0 Lt. Cahill or to match veil; nucleus Steelhead Orange egg yarn (or dubbing, chenille, braid, McFly Foam); veil Apricot Supreme egg yarn; combos Chartreuse over Flame, Flame over Cerise, Oregon Cheese over Steelhead Orange; fished for steelhead and salmon in northern Michigan spring and fall. — [Current Works, Nuke Egg](https://www.current-works.com/how-to-tie-fly/nuke-egg-fly-pattern/)
- Nomad Anglers (Michigan shop): Hook Gamakatsu C14S; body McFly Foam; veil Glo Bug Yarn; "Change up the colors and size for different water levels and times of the year"; for salmon, steelhead and trout in the Great Lakes. Tying note: use ~3/8 inch diameter of McFly Foam. — [Nomad Anglers, Simple Nuke Egg](https://nomadanglers.com/blogs/fly-tying-patterns/simple-nuke-egg-pattern)
- Nuke Egg described as an improvement over the GloBug: "a veil or shroud of yarn that covers the nucleus... giving it a natural, transparent look when wet"; natural combos Egg over Sockeye, Pink Lady over Shrimp Pink, Apricot Supreme over Steelhead Orange, Salmon Egg over Chartreuse, Oregon Cheese over Steelhead Orange; attractors Chartreuse over Flame, Flame over Cerise. — [Current Works, Egg Patterns](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/egg-patterns-matching-hatch-steelhead/)

**Glo Bug / yarn egg, McFly Foam, Estaz**
- General Great Lakes egg-fly guide: all-around size 12 short-shank scud hook; 14–16 in low clear water; trout hooks Daiichi 1130 or TMC 2457; steelhead/salmon Gamakatsu C14S; builds: Glo Bug (McFly Foam or egg yarn), Sucker Spawn (yarn/angora loops), Estaz egg (single flashy strand wound into a ball); variations Nuke Egg ("milky-white veil over a colored core") and Blood Dot; core palette orange, yellow, light pink, cream; alternates pale blue, chartreuse, red; seal only head thread wraps. — [MidCurrent](https://midcurrent.com/v2/how-to-tie-egg-flies-salmon-steelhead/)
- McFly Foam favored over Glo Bug yarn by a Great Lakes guide, in "Apricot and Oregon Cheese"; requires strong thread (Kevlar/GSP) for tight wraps; sizes 6–14 mm; Blood Dot Egg designed by Great Lakes guide Jeff Blood (Pennsylvania) to resemble a spawn-bag cluster; Crystal Meth and Estaz eggs best in dirty water; Nuke Egg works clear or stained; Micro Spawn 6–8 mm for low clear water. (non-MI author) — [Trout & Steelhead, Best Egg Flies](https://troutandsteelhead.net/best-egg-flies-for-steelhead/)
- McFly Foam egg fly retail colors: pink, light pink, orange, light orange, fire orange, white, chartreuse, yellow. — [Frosty Fly (snippet)](https://frostyfly.com/products/mcfly-foam-egg-fly)
- Estaz egg on the Pere Marquette: "a very simple egg pattern that should be in every steelheader's fly box if you're fishing the PM," tied in chartreuse, pink, peach. (PM Lodge page now 404; snippet only) — [Pere Marquette River Lodge, Estaz Egg video](https://www.pmlodge.com/estaz-egg-fly-tying-video/)

**Sucker Spawn / Crystal Meth / Clown / Y2K / Otter's / Egg-sucking leech**
- Sucker Spawn: "two or three strands of regular knitting yarn, tied in at the bend... creating a series of small bumps" along the shank; Clown egg = same construction with multiple colors; Crystal Meth = same as Clown but with Dyed Pearl Diamond Braid; Y2K tied "semi-Glo Bug style." ## Key Question 4: Which egg colors/sizes match which species and water conditions, with seasonal logic

### Takeaway
Guides pair pattern to the egg source of the moment: fresh, realistic salmon-egg colors and larger sizes (Steelhead Orange, Sockeye, Salmon Egg cores) in Sept–Oct behind spawning kings/coho; washed-out veiled eggs (Oregon Cheese, Apricot Supreme, cream, pale pink) as eggs age through Nov–Feb; pale yellow/cream sucker spawn plus small natural steelhead-egg colors in March–May; sizes go up and colors get brighter (chartreuse, flame, cerise, clown) when water is high/stained or very cold, and down/natural when low and clear.

### Cited Findings
- Fall salmon: "Fresh eggs have realistic coloring when salmon actively spawn"; steelhead spring spawn March–April, imitated with pale yellow; suckers and walleye "spawn concurrently with steelhead in spring," imitated with pale yellow sucker spawn; high/stained water use dime-sized patterns; clear water demands "smaller, realistic color patterns." — [Current Works](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/egg-patterns-matching-hatch-steelhead/)
- Two-fly logic for fall steelhead behind salmon: larger bright attractor plus smaller realistic egg. — [True North Trout](https://truenorthtrout.com/2009/10/egg-flies-matching-the-hatch-for-steelhead/)
- Clarity rules: low clear water apricot, cream, pale pink in smaller sizes; high/off-color chartreuse, orange, hot pink, clown. — [MidCurrent](https://midcurrent.com/v2/how-to-tie-egg-flies-salmon-steelhead/)
- Size vs clarity: 10–14 mm bright in murky water; 6–8 mm natural in gin-clear; Crystal Meth/Estaz for dirty water. (non-MI author) — [Trout & Steelhead](https://troutandsteelhead.net/best-egg-flies-for-steelhead/)
- Cold-water behavior (winter): in 30°F water strikes are subtle; wait "two or three head throbs" before setting; egg patterns (Glo-bugs, beads, Krystal Meth, Sucker Spawn, Otter Eggs) remain staples. — [Fly Fisherman, Supinski](https://www.flyfisherman.com/editorial/guide-strategies-for-great-lakes-winter-steelhead/370579)
- Dead eggs are cream/light pink; brown trout eggs bright orange; steelhead eggs translucent yellow to yellow-orange. — [MidCurrent](https://midcurrent.com/v2/how-to-tie-egg-flies-salmon-steelhead/)
- Biological basis: unfertilized eggs go "milky... with patches of color." — [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/why-do-salmon-eggs-come-different-colors)
- Sucker eggs pale yellow/light golden; suckers spawn on the same gravel trout use. (PA) — [Hatch Magazine](https://www.hatchmag.com/articles/sucker-spawn/7715199)

### Inferences
- Seasonal matrix (synthesis): Late Aug–Oct: Chinook/coho eggs, 7–8 mm/6–8 mm, orange to red-orange fresh; flies #8–10 Steelhead Orange, Sockeye, Salmon Egg, Flame; add chartreuse/clown in stained water. Nov–Feb: aging salmon eggs plus brown/brook/laker eggs (4–6 mm); flies #10–12 Oregon Cheese, Apricot Supreme, Pink Lady, cream "dead egg" Nuke Eggs. March–April: steelhead eggs (~4–5 mm, translucent yellow-orange) and walleye eggs (2 mm); flies #10–14 apricot, peach, light pink, pale yellow. Late April–May: sucker and redhorse eggs (2–3 mm, pale yellow/cream); flies #12–14 Sucker Spawn, Crystal Meth, cream/yellow Glo Bugs, Otter's #14.
- Since UP steelhead spawn later (early May peak) and suckers spawn in the same window, UP spring boxes should emphasize sucker/steelhead colors together.

### Gaps
- No source quantified a color shift timeline (days) for dead eggs in Michigan water; the "fresh, then translucent, then opaque" sequence is qualitative guide observation.

## Key Question 5: The chuck-and-duck rig, its Michigan origin, casting, and the modern alternatives (indicator, Euro/tight-line)

### Takeaway
Michigan chuck-and-duck = backing, ~100 ft of level running/shooting line (or Amnesia mono), a 7–12 ft butt of 12–20 lb Maxima, a sliding snap swivel carrying a slinky or pencil lead buffered by a bead against a barrel swivel, then 3–4 ft of lighter tippet to the first fly with a second fly 24–36 in below; it is lobbed, not false-cast. Guides tie its origin to Michigan's flies-only water where clients could not fly-cast, on the Pere Marquette and Manistee; indicator (float) rigs on 10–11 ft single-hand or switch rods have since become the dominant method, with swing and Euro/tight-line as further alternatives.

### Cited Findings
**Components (Michigan sources)**
- Baldwin Bait & Tackle (Pere Marquette, Steve Fraley): backing; running line "Amnesia or thin mono" joined with an Albright; butt 10–12 ft of 12–15 lb mono (Maxima); "A tapered leader is not actually needed"; #10 black sliding snap swivel with a bead ahead of a #10 barrel swivel (sliding weight lets fish take without resistance); tippet via improved clinch; two-fly ~3 ft between flies, single fly ~4 ft; cast "straight across stream or slightly quartered down," not upstream. Indicator rig: 9.5–11 ft rod (switch/spey preferred), floating line overlined 1–2 weights, ~9 ft leader ("Pere Marquette standard"), adjustable sliding indicator sized to the lead, 3.5–4 ft tippet single fly or 3 ft to first fly and 1.5–2 ft to second, shot "rides just above the bottom only touching occasionally," fished "just as you would most dry flies." — [Baldwin Bait & Tackle](https://fishbaldwin.com/pere-marquette-travel-fishing-tips/steves-explanation-of-chuck-n-duck-indicator-rigs-for-migratory-fish/)
- Current Works (Ted Kraimer): butt Maxima 12# Ultragreen or 12# fluoro; small rivers (Betsie, Bear Creek, Pere Marquette) 7–8 ft butt, split shot on the barrel swivel's tag end, 5–8 lb leader, first fly ~30 in from swivel, second fly 24 in below first, dropper tied off the bend; large rivers (Big Manistee, Muskegon, Au Sable) 8–12 ft butt, pencil lead (1-lb coils cut to length) on a snap swivel with a 4 mm bead buffer. Indicator rig: floating line (SA Magnum Taper), 8 ft bass/striper tapered leader ≥10 lb, barrel swivel with 2–3 in tag for shot, 30 in to first fly, 24 in between flies; indicators Thing-a-ma-bobber, Thill Ice 'n Fly, Drennan Piker; best under ~5 ft deep. — [Current Works, Rigging](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/rigging-for-steelhead-salmon/)
- Nomad Anglers: chuck and duck "best used for fishing deep spots" in winter; running line 30 lb Amnesia (fluorescent green), main line 20 lb Maxima Ultragreen, micro swivels, glass spacer bead, split shot, 10 and 8 lb fluoro tippet. Indicator rig ("arguably the most effective"): 10 ft 7–8 wt, SA Amplitude/Mastery floating line, 9 ft 0X–1X leader, Blackbird Phantom float, Loon Camo Drops/Blackbird shot, micro swivels, Seaguar STS/Maxima 10 and 8 lb tippet. Swing: monocore shooting line, SA Freightliner Skagit, Third Coast tips, 1.5–3 ft 16 lb fluoro. — [Nomad Anglers, Michigan Steelhead Set Up](https://nomadanglers.com/pages/michigan-steelhead-set-up)
- Trails to Trout "River Guide" formula: 100+ yd 30 lb backing, 100 ft shooting line, 20 ft 15 lb Amnesia, 20 ft 12 lb Maxima, swivels and weight, 3–6 ft of 6–8 lb Maxima; weight 2–4 pea-sized split shot; leave ~5 ft between rod tip and weight; "one cast to the fish, not several false casts," weight leading the fly. — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/salmon-fishing-tips/)
- Hawkins (Manistee): float rig uses 8–20 g float, 12 ft of 12–15 lb fluoro with shot decreasing in size down the line, 18 in tippet to egg or bead; float fishing "expands the fishable water tenfold" versus chuck-and-duck. — [Hawkins Outfitters](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/manistee-river-steelhead-fishing/)
- Generic description (non-MI author): thin ~100 ft PVC running line; 11 ft of 20 lb Maxima butt; slinky (lead or steel shot in parachute cord) on snap swivel with bead; barrel swivel to tippet; optional 1–2 ft hi-vis mono "indicator"; cast by "lobbing the weight into a basket"; dead-drift with weight ticking bottom at ~45°. New York (non-MI) rule: 15 ft max leader with max 4 ft between weight and fly. — [Global FlyFisher, Bob Petti](https://globalflyfisher.com/fish-better/chuck-n-duck)
- Weight options (non-MI author): slinky, pencil lead, or split shot on a light breakaway tag; inline barrel swivel plus snap swivel for slinkies/pencil lead; fluoro 12–14 lb bottom leader; "check your area to determine if multiple flies are permitted." — [Trout & Steelhead, Chuck and Duck](https://troutandsteelhead.net/chuck-and-duck-fishing/)
- Amnesia "has long been the standard" running/butt material for chuck-and-duck in the Great Lakes. — [Guide Recommended (snippet)](https://guiderecommended.com/how-to-rig-and-fish-nymphs-chuck-and-duck-style/)

**Origin (angler lore; no primary documentation found)**
- Hawkins: on the Manistee "chuck and duck was born, essentially using lead and a thinner line to get egg and nymph flies to the bottom—lobbing lead versus fly casting," and it became "the number one method for Manistee River steelhead fishing," though "very limiting" in woody rivers. — [Hawkins Outfitters](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/manistee-river-steelhead-fishing/)
- A search-result summary attributes the name and technique to Mike Gnatkowski, "the first and only importer of Slinky Drifters to Michigan," who guided mainly on the Pere Marquette and adopted shooting line + Slinky Drifter + fly so non-casting clients could fish the flies-only stretch. The underlying page (a Michigan Sportsman forum thread) could not be loaded (paywall), so this is unverified forum lore. — [Michigan Sportsman forum thread (snippet)](https://www.michigan-sportsman.com/threads/the-martin-72-multiplier-a-michigan-legend.123000/)
- Context: eastern/midwestern anglers found West Coast swing techniques ineffective in near-freezing Great Lakes tributaries, motivating bottom-bouncing with fly tackle. — [Global FlyFisher](https://globalflyfisher.com/fish-better/chuck-n-duck)

**Modern alternatives**
- Supinski: switch rods for indicator nymphing on smaller rivers; "chuck-and-swing" with clear intermediate line and two weighted flies "(where legal)"; Euro-nymphing with sighters and Cortland Liquid Crystal lines in deeper runs. — [Fly Fisherman](https://www.flyfisherman.com/editorial/guide-strategies-for-great-lakes-winter-steelhead/370579)
- Hawkins: Skagit swing (SA Freightliner 480–560 gr on 12–14 ft spey rods); float nymphing preferred over chuck-and-duck in wood. — [Hawkins Outfitters](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/manistee-river-steelhead-fishing/)

### Inferences
- A representative Michigan chuck-and-duck build from these sources: 30 lb backing → 100 ft level running line or 20–30 lb Amnesia → 8–12 ft 12–20 lb Maxima butt → sliding #10 snap swivel + slinky/pencil lead + bead → #10 barrel swivel → 30–48 in of 6–10 lb tippet to fly #1 → 24–36 in to fly #2 (tied off the bend). Indicator version keeps the same terminal spacing under an 8–9 ft leader and a float sized to the shot.
- The "1980s Pere Marquette/Manistee" origin is plausible from the guide statements but rests on lore, not a dated primary source.

### Gaps
- No dated primary source (magazine article, book) confirming who coined "chuck and duck" or when; Michigan Sportsman forum threads that discuss it are paywalled to automated fetching.

## Key Question 6: Michigan regulations affecting egg flies, beads, and rigs (2026–27 rules), including any 2025–2026 changes

### Takeaway
The 2026–27 Michigan regulations define an artificial fly as up to two in-line single-pointed hooks dressed with thread-attached materials, ½-inch max gap, weighted fly or leader allowed but no weight suspended below the hook, nothing attached above the eye; a bead "is not a fly and is not allowed in flies only gear restricted waters" but is a legal "lure" on other waters when pegged within 4 inches of a ½-inch-gap single hook (newly codified for 2026); on all streams Aug 1–May 31 single hooks over ½-inch gap and trebles over 3/8-inch are illegal and lures over 1 oz are banned; listed salmon rivers (Betsie, Bear Cr., Manistee below Tippy, Big Sable, PM, Little Manistee, White, N. Br. White, Muskegon below Croton, Pentwater branches) restrict terminal gear Aug 1–Nov 15 to ½-inch single hooks/jigs or 3/8-inch trebles only on body baits/plugs/spinners/spoons, and lead-bodied devices don't count as lures; drop-shotting is illegal on rivers; snagging and chumming (including with fish eggs) are illegal; live/dead/preserved bait is illegal on gear-restricted streams and scented material on flies-only streams. Michigan has no numeric weight-to-fly distance rule for flies.

### Cited Findings (all from the 2026 digest unless noted; digest URL: [2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations, rules Apr 1 2026–Mar 31 2027](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/LED/digests/2026-Michigan-Fishing-Regulations_web_accessible.pdf))
- Artificial Flies (Definitions, p. 8): "Known as wet and dry flies, streamers, or nymphs, is defined as a single-pointed hook, or not more than two single-pointed hooks connected in-line (the second hook commonly known as a stinger hook), crafted with natural, artificial and/or synthetic materials attached to the hook with thread. An artificial fly may not include a spinner, spoon, scoop, lip or any other fishing lure or bait attached. Material of any type cannot be attached above the eye of the hook. The fly or leader may be weighted, but no weight shall be attached to the terminal tackle in a manner that allows the weight to be suspended from or below the hook. Single-pointed hooks are restricted to measuring 1/2-inch or less from point to shank. A bead is not a fly and is not allowed in flies only gear restricted waters." — [2026 digest p. 8](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/LED/digests/2026-Michigan-Fishing-Regulations_web_accessible.pdf); same text mirrored at [eRegulations Michigan definitions](https://www.eregulations.com/michigan/fishing/definitions)
- Artificial Lure/Bait (p. 8): "A man-made lure manufactured to imitate natural bait... They also include plastic products made to resemble worms, eggs, fish and other aquatic organisms. Use of a bead (bead fishing) is considered to be a lure. NOTE: On the following waters from Aug. 1 – Nov. 15, terminal fishing gear is restricted to single-pointed hooks or jigs, measuring 1/2-inch or less from point to shank or treble hooks 3/8-inch or less from point to shank ONLY when attached to a body bait, plug, spinner or spoon. An artificial lure is not a device primarily constructed of lead: all waters of the Betsie R. (Benzie and Manistee Cos.), all waters of Bear Cr. ### Inferences
- Chuck-and-duck is legal on Michigan flies-only water provided the flies meet the artificial-fly definition and the weight is on the leader above the fly (not suspended below it); the "no weight suspended from or below the hook" clause and the stream ban on drop-shotting are the relevant constraints, not a distance rule. The New York 4-ft weight-to-fly limit does not apply in Michigan.
- Egg flies tied with yarn/foam on a ≤½-inch-gap single hook qualify as artificial flies; a plastic egg/bead (even on the hook) is a "lure," so it is legal on Type 1–4 water and non-flies-only gear-restricted water but never on flies-only reaches. Scented eggs/yarn are illegal on flies-only reaches.
- Sizes #8–#14 scud/egg hooks fall well within the ½-inch gap limit; large salmon hooks (#2 and up) risk exceeding it and are the target of the Aug 1–May 31 stream rule.
- Bead pegging: the 4-inch rule covers the common 1–2 in peg distance; a bead pegged higher is illegal statewide.
