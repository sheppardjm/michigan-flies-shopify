# Michigan River-by-River Hatch Calendars, Regulations, Run Timing and Regional Offsets

Research date: 2026-09-28. Scope: Michigan only. All regulation citations below were read from the **2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations digest (rules apply April 1, 2026 – March 31, 2027)**, downloaded from the Michigan DNR and text-extracted; they must still be re-verified against the live DNR guide/FO-200/FO-210 before publishing, because the two-column PDF layout can interleave entries and because DNR revises Fisheries Orders annually.

Working notes on source quality (applies to every section):
- The most authoritative *local* hatch charts (Gates Au Sable Lodge, Current Works' Upper Manistee & Boardman chart, True North Trout's Au Sable/Manistee/Jordan charts, Pere Marquette River Lodge) are published only as **images/PDFs**, so their cell-by-cell contents could not be machine-read here; their URLs are recorded so they can be transcribed by hand. Text-based charts that *were* fully captured are: Superior Flies (UP), Trails to Trout (statewide, northern-LP baseline), Motor City Anglers (statewide), Big Y Fly Co (per-river date tables for Au Sable, Manistee, Little Manistee, Pere Marquette, Muskegon), The Fly Shack (per-river month grids), Gray Drake Lodge (Muskegon, 2013), FlyFishFinder (2026 statewide + Au Sable/PM pages), Current Works Upper Manistee text calendar, michiganfishing.guide PM calendar (2026).
- Aggregator charts (Big Y, Fly Shack, FlyFishFinder, DIY Fly Fishing, michiganfishing.guide, michiganflyfishinghub) are secondary sources; several Big Y date windows look generic/copied between rivers (e.g., identical Trico windows for Manistee and Little Manistee; a "Hendrickson March 1" start on the Au Sable that no local shop supports). Treat them as fill-in data, and prefer the local shop/guide statements where the two conflict.

## Key Question 1: What does each river's published hatch chart show by month, and which charts are most authoritative?

### Takeaway
Michigan's trout-hatch sequence is consistent statewide (early black stones → Hendrickson + black caddis → sulphur/March brown/gray drake → brown drake → Hex → Isonychia/Trico/terrestrials → White Fly → fall BWO), and the best per-river data comes from a small number of local charts. On the Au Sable/Manistee "northern rivers" baseline, Hendricksons run roughly Apr 20–May 20 (peak May 1–14), Brown Drakes Jun 1–Jul 4 (peak Jun 1–14), Hex Jun 10–Jul 10 (peak Jun 21–Jul 7), Tricos Jul 20–Oct 20 and White Fly Aug 14–Sep 30; the UP chart (Superior Flies) shifts each of these 2–3 weeks later, and mid-state rivers (PM, Muskegon) run 1–2 weeks earlier.

#### Northern Lower Peninsula

**Au Sable River (Main Stream "Holy Waters", North Branch, South Branch/Mason Tract, below Mio)**

**Manistee River (upper trout water; Big Manistee below Tippy Dam)**

**Boardman River (Traverse City)**

**Pigeon, Sturgeon, Black (Pigeon River Country, "Tip of the Mitt")**
- Pigeon: brown/rainbow with brook trout in headwaters; 2015 dam removal cooled the river; "plenty of smaller mayflies, caddis and even tricos, but doesn't have the big-bug hatches like drakes and Hex"; "Tricos grace the water most morning in mid-to-late August"; fall hoppers, beetles, flying ants. — [DIY Fly Fishing Pigeon](https://diyflyfishing.com/pigeon-river-michigan/)
- Sturgeon: 15 ft/mile gradient, "well oxygenated... quite cold"; stoneflies year-round; caddis and mayflies (Hendricksons, gray/brown drakes, Hex) April–May per DIY; terrestrials summer; fall "by far the best time" (steelhead/migratory browns from Burt Lake from mid-September). — [DIY Fly Fishing Sturgeon](https://diyflyfishing.com/sturgeon-river-michigan/)
- Pigeon/Sturgeon/Black share "similar hatches of drakes, hexes, caddis, and sulphurs" (search summary of DIY Pigeon page). — [DIY Fly Fishing Pigeon](https://diyflyfishing.com/pigeon-river-michigan/)
- Trails to Trout instructs "Add 1-2 weeks" to northern-river dates for Sturgeon, Pigeon and Black. — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)

**Little Manistee River**
- Wild brook, brown, rainbow, steelhead and salmon; hatches named: Hex, brown drakes, caddis, smaller mayflies (no dates). — [DIY Fly Fishing Little Manistee](https://diyflyfishing.com/little-manistee-river-michigan/)

**Pine River (Manistee tributary)**
- "The coldest, fastest river in Lower Michigan," groundwater keeps it "always 69 degrees or colder"; July–August water in "high 50's or low 60's"; more resident rainbows than any other Lower Michigan stream; "isn't primarily a surface-feeding river" — key foods are Pteronarcys stoneflies (#2-6), baitfish, sculpins; best mid-May–September. — [Hawkins Outfitters Pine River](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/pine-river/)
- Gray drakes, light cahills, sulphurs and yellow stoneflies present; wild McCloud-strain rainbows. — [DIY Fly Fishing Pine](https://diyflyfishing.com/pine-river-michigan/)
- Note: Hawkins states the Pine is "flies-only, catch-and-release"; this is **not** reflected in the 2026 DNR gear-restricted list (see KQ2) — flag as conflicting/unverified.

**Rifle River**
- Brown-trout river with "sparse runs of Chinook salmon and a few steelhead, along with lake-run brown trout" in fall; hatches: BWO, Hendricksons, cahills, sulphurs, white millers, "occasional Hex"; very clear water. — [DIY Fly Fishing Rifle](https://diyflyfishing.com/rifle-river-michigan/)
- Hex peak "Late June–Early July," "one of Michigan's underfished Hex events" (2026). — [Michigan Fly Fishing Hub – Hex](https://michiganflyfishinghub.com/michigan-hex-hatch.html)

### Inferences
- A defensible per-river, per-half-month seed can be built from the Trails to Trout northern-LP baseline, then shifted with the printed regional rules (−1 to −2 wk mid-state; +1 to +2 wk Tip of the Mitt; +2 to +4 wk UP), and then overridden with the river-specific peak statements captured above (e.g., PM Hex Jun 10–25, Au Sable Jun 20–28, Manistee Jun 25–Jul 5, UP Hex peak 7/5–7/21).
- Tailwaters (Muskegon below Croton, Manistee below Tippy) should be modeled with a longer shoulder season for BWO/midges (winter activity) rather than with a latitude shift alone.
- Rivers described as lacking big-bug hatches (Pigeon, Chocolay, Pine) should down-weight Hex/Brown Drake recommendations and up-weight caddis, small mayflies, Tricos (Pigeon) or stonefly/sculpin streamers (Pine).

## Key Question 2: What are the flies-only, no-kill and Type 1–4 / gear-restricted designations and dates, and where is the official list?

### Takeaway
The official list is the "Inland Trout & Salmon Stream Regulations" section of the annual Michigan Fishing Regulations digest (2026 edition: Type table p. 42–43, Gear Restricted Streams pp. 44–46, county listings pp. 48–65), backed by Fisheries Orders FO-200/FO-210 and the DNR FishingMaps/ArcGIS layers. The 2026 digest lists 20 gear-restricted reaches statewide; the flies-only/no-kill reaches relevant here are the Au Sable Holy Water (Burton's–Wakeley), South Branch Chase Bridge–Lower High Banks, and PM M-37–Gleason's Landing, with additional flies-only (2-fish) reaches on the North Branch, lower South Branch, Manistee M-72–CCC Bridge and Little Manistee Spencer's–Johnson bridges.

## Key Question 3: What steelhead/salmon run months apply on rivers with lake-run fish?

### Takeaway
Statewide pattern from the sources: Chinook Labor Day–October (earliest on the Betsie, mid/late August), coho September–October (into November on the Grand/St. Joe/St. Marys), fall steelhead October–December (November peak on the Betsie, Grand, Clinton, Two Hearted, Thunder Bay), winter holdovers, spring steelhead late February–April/early May (later in the UP: April–early May), summer Skamania on the St. Joseph (mid-May–September, now unreliable) and Manistee below Tippy ("most dependable summer-run fishery in the state"), pinks late August–September in the St. Marys/Carp/Lake Superior tributaries (odd years strongest), and Atlantics in the St. Marys mid/late June–August with late-October spawning.

## Key Question 4: What regional offsets do sources suggest, and do they explain them (latitude, groundwater, tailwater)?

### Takeaway
Two independently published charts give explicit, near-identical offset rules: relative to the northern-LP (Au Sable/Manistee) baseline, mid-state rivers run 1–2 weeks early, Tip-of-the-Mitt rivers 1–2 weeks late, and the UP 2–4 weeks late (Superior Flies' UP-based chart says northern rivers are 2 weeks earlier and mid-state 3 weeks earlier than the UP). Sources attribute the shift to water temperature and latitude, with tailwaters (Muskegon warmer/earlier; Manistee-below-Tippy extended) and strong groundwater (Jordan, South Branch Au Sable, Pine) modifying the pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Trails to Trout chart rule: Northern rivers (Au Sable, Manistee) "Use Emergence Dates"; Mid-state (Muskegon, Pere Marquette) "Subtract 1-2 weeks"; Tip of the Mitt (Sturgeon, Pigeon, Black) "Add 1-2 weeks"; Upper Peninsula (Fox, Carp, Ontonagon) "Add 2-4 weeks." — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)
- Superior Flies UP chart rule: "Tip-Of-The-Mitt - Subtract 1 Week; Northern Rivers - Subtract 2 Weeks; Mid-State Rivers - Subtract 3 Weeks." — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/)
- Norrik (search summary): UP rivers (Fox, Whitefish, Ontonagon) hatch "2-3 weeks later than the northern Lower Peninsula rivers." (Page returned HTTP 500 when fetched.) — [Norrik Michigan hatch chart](https://norrik.com/fly-hatches/michigan-fly-hatch-chart/)
- Hex (2026): Muskegon peak Jun 8–20 ("tailwater runs warmer than northern rivers putting it among the first"); PM Jun 10–25 ("often the first week of June"); Au Sable Holy Waters Jun 20–28; Boardman late June; Manistee Jun 25–Jul 5 (tailwater extends window); Rifle late June–early July; Jordan, Betsie, Fox, Two Hearted "2–3 weeks behind the Au Sable and Manistee." Explanation: "water temperature and latitude. Southern/mid-state rivers warm first; northern rivers follow 7–10 days later." Also notes 2026's cold spring ("water temperatures were below normal well into late May") could delay hatches. — [Michigan Fly Fishing Hub – Hex](https://michiganflyfishinghub.com/michigan-hex-hatch.html)
- Statewide: "The last week of June through the Fourth of July weekend is historically the most consistent window for heavy Hex activity statewide." — [Michigan Fly Fishing Hub – Hex](https://michiganflyfishinghub.com/michigan-hex-hatch.html)
- UP Hex chart window: 6/24–7/24, peak 7/5–7/21, vs northern-LP baseline 6/10–7/10, peak 6/21–7/7 (a ~2-week lag in both start and peak). — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/); [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)
- Hendrickson: UP chart 5/4–6/3 (peak 5/14–21) vs northern-LP 4/20–5/20 (peak 5/1–14) — again ~2 weeks; PM Hendricksons (Big Y) May 1–31 and michiganfishing.guide "May" (i.e., PM Hendricksons listed later than the Au Sable's late-April start, which contradicts the "mid-state earlier" rule — flag as an aggregator artifact rather than a documented offset). — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/); [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); [Big Y PM](https://bigyflyco.com/pages/pere-marquette-river-michigan-hatch-chart)
- Tailwater effect: Muskegon "Midges and BWOs active through winter"; Manistee below Tippy "BWOs fish well even through winter" (2026). — [FlyFishFinder MI](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/fly-hatches-michigan/); "The Muskegon is a tailwater below Croton Dam, giving it year-round cold temperatures and extended hatch seasons at both ends." (search summary) — [FlyFishFinder MI](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/fly-hatches-michigan/)
- Groundwater effect: Jordan "rarely exceed[s] 60 degrees F" — [DIY Jordan](https://diyflyfishing.com/jordan-river-michigan/); Pine "always 69 degrees or colder," high 50s/low 60s in July–August — [Hawkins Pine](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/pine-river/); South Branch Au Sable 50°F in late September — [Michigan Fly Fishing Hub – South Branch](https://michiganflyfishinghub.com/rivers/au-sable-river-south-branch/); Platte "groundwater-fed, stable flows" vs Betsie "easily affected by rain and run-off" — [Jon Zickert](https://jonzickert.com/blog/when-the-salmon-come-back-a-locals-guide-to-fall-on-the-betsie-and-platte); Thunder Bay "too warm during summer for trout" — [DIY Thunder Bay](https://diyflyfishing.com/thunder-bay-river-michigan/)
- Surface-feeding transition on the Muskegon occurs "when water temperatures reach 50–60°F" in May–June (2013). — [Gray Drake Lodge](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)

### Inferences
- Recommended engine defaults: region offset in days relative to northern-LP baseline: SW/central LP (PM, Muskegon, White, Rogue, Dowagiac, Huron) −7 to −14; northern LP (Au Sable, Manistee, Boardman, Pine, Little Manistee, Rifle) 0; Tip of the Mitt (Pigeon, Sturgeon, Black, Jordan, Maple) +7 to +14; UP (all) +14 to +28 (use +14 to +21 for the eastern UP/Two Hearted/Fox Hex per the 2–3 week statements, up to +28 for western-UP/Lake Superior tribs).
- Add a per-river "thermal class" modifier: tailwater (Muskegon: earlier + longer shoulders; Big Manistee: longer shoulders), strong-groundwater (Jordan, South Branch, Pine, Platte: later/longer Hex and a longer summer dry-fly season), runoff/warm (Betsie, Thunder Bay, Rifle lower: earlier warm-water cutoff).
- No source gave an *elevation* rule; Michigan sources uniformly frame offsets as latitude + water temperature.

### Gaps
- No source quantified Hendrickson timing differences between the PM and Au Sable from local shops; the only PM-vs-Au Sable numbers are aggregator tables.
- No explicit North Branch vs South Branch vs Main Stream Hex dates were published in text form (Gates' chart likely has them; image only).
