# Non-insect foods, species diets, and matching flies for Michigan river salmonids

Scope note: This file separates (a) documented science (MDNR Fisheries Research Reports, peer-reviewed diet studies, agency species pages) from (b) angler/guide recommendation. Anything drawn from outside Michigan (Lake Erie, Minnesota North Shore, West Virginia, Pacific Northwest) is labelled REGIONAL or OUTSIDE-REGION. Research date: 2026-09-28. Web search budget was exhausted near the end; several primary pages (michigan.gov species pages, MSU Extension) blocked automated fetching and were read via curl where possible.

## KQ1. What do Michigan diet studies say brown trout, brook trout, and rainbow/steelhead eat in rivers?

### Takeaway
The best Michigan-specific science is old MDNR work: large Au Sable North Branch brown trout (1962–71 stomachs) were 70–80% piscivorous (mostly small brook trout plus "coarse fish"), and Anna River (UP, Lake Superior tributary) brown trout under ~12 in ate mostly caddis while fish over ~12 in ate fish (stocked coho smolts in May, slimy sculpin June–October, selecting salmonids over sculpins). No Michigan river study quantifying Hexagenia nymph, crayfish, scud, or terrestrial share of trout diet was located; the terrestrial-importance numbers available are from West Virginia brook trout (38–47% of annual biomass).

### Cited Findings

**Brown trout (science)**
- MDNR Fisheries Research Report 1855 (Alexander, 1977), North Branch Au Sable: stomachs of large wild brown trout collected 1962–1971; "The real diet of large brown trout, in the normal regulation water, for the summer period, was composed of 75% fish. Small brook trout made up 30% of the diet; no brown trout were found... The winter diet consisted of 80% fish. Small brook trout comprised 37% and small brown trout 2%." In the flies-only special-regulation water, summer diet was 82% fish (61% small brook trout) and winter 77% fish (42% brook trout, 5% brown trout). "The diet composition of North Branch Au Sable trout is 70 to 80% fish." Brown trout are "the most significant natural predator yet identified on the Au Sable in terms of total trout killed" and "the predation of the brown trout on coarse fish populations is beneficial to all trout." — [MDNR RR1855 PDF](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1855.pdf); catalog entry [UM Fisheries Division Library](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/f/fishery/AAG2862.1855.001/?view=toc)
- MDNR Institute for Fisheries Research Report 1759 (Hannuksela, 1969), Anna River, Alger County (Lake Superior tributary, UP), May–Oct 1968, 135 brown trout stomachs: "Brown trout 1.3 to 11.8 inches (33-300 mm) long and 0, I, II and III years of age, fed primarily on Trichoptera. Larger brown trout, 11.8-20.6 inches (300-522 mm) long (ages IV and V) ate mostly fish... Stocked coho salmon were the most frequently ingested fish during May, and slimy sculpins were eaten most frequently during June-October. Brown trout selected salmonids over sculpins to eat, even though sculpins were nine times more abundant than salmonids in the stream." Fish were 62.3% of food weight (salmonids 54.8%, slimy sculpin 7.4%); invertebrates 37.7% by weight with Trichoptera most numerous. Ingested coho were 3.6–5.7 in (91–145 mm); sculpins 1.8–3.3 in (46–85 mm). The report notes "brown trout have nocturnal feeding habits (Brynildson, Hacker, and Klick, 1963)." Slimy sculpins composed ~90% of available prey June–October. — [MDNR RR1759 PDF](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1759.pdf)
- Michigan DNR brown trout species page: "The diet of adult brown trout includes insects and their larvae, crustaceans, mollusks, amphibians, small rodents and other fish." Lake-run browns average eight pounds; brown trout spawn in tributaries in September–October and "begin to take up residence near stream outlets in late summer to early fall." — [Michigan DNR Brown trout](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brown-trout)
- Troutnut (Hexagenia limbata page) on Hex and browns: "In the cold trout rivers of Wisconsin and Michigan, huge nocturnal brown trout whose usual menu consists of smaller brown trout become, for a week or so, prime dry fly quarry." Hex nymphs ("Michigan Wigglers") were once prized live bait for ice fishermen; Hex is a two-year nymph in rivers (angler/naturalist source, not a diet study). — [Troutnut Hexagenia limbata](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/32/Mayfly-Hexagenia-limbata-Hex); [Troutnut Michigan Wigglers](https://www.troutnut.com/common-name/308/Michigan-Wigglers); [Hawkins Outfitters Hexagenia](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/hexagenia-limbata/)
- GVSU thesis, Stegman Creek (west Michigan coldwater stream), 2016–17: PIT-tagged 352 mottled sculpin and 329 brown trout; describes mottled sculpin as "a small-bodied benthic fish" and brown trout as "a larger-bodied, drift-feeding fish"; sculpin movement declined in winter while trout condition/movement were stable across seasons. (Movement/condition study, not a diet study; confirms sculpin–brown trout co-occurrence in Michigan streams.) — [CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/outputs/130170630/); [GVSU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1856&context=theses)
- OUTSIDE-REGION context on sculpin predation: Logan River, Utah — fish in 0% of age-1, 10% of age-2, 33% of age-3+ brown trout diets, ~81% of fish eaten were sculpin. — [Meredith et al. 2014 (USU)](https://www.usu.edu/fel/publications/pdf/Meredithetal2014EFF.pdf)

**Brook trout (science)**
- Michigan DNR: "Brook trout have been described as voracious feeders with the potential to consume large numbers seasonally available mayflies, stoneflies and other aquatic insects as well as terrestrial insects. However, they will often feed on whatever is most readily available like zooplankton, crustaceans, worms and fish." — [Michigan DNR Brook trout](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brook-trout)
- Alexander 1977 (above) documents small brook trout as the principal fish prey of large Au Sable browns (30–61% of summer diet) — i.e., brook trout are themselves a key "baitfish" in Michigan trout rivers. — [MDNR RR1855](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1855.pdf)
- OUTSIDE-REGION (West Virginia, Sweka & Hartman): terrestrial invertebrates were "38–47% of the yearly biomass consumed" and "51–63% of the yearly energy consumed" by stream brook trout; lowest in fall/winter, higher spring/summer; removing terrestrial input would require a 130% increase in aquatic prey consumption to maintain growth. — [TAFS 137(1):224](https://academic.oup.com/tafs/article/137/1/224/7888240)
- OUTSIDE-REGION synthesis: terrestrial Coleoptera and Lepidoptera "consistently proved to be important prey during warmer seasons"; terrestrial use declines with increasing benthic invertebrate biomass. — [EBTJV summary](https://easternbrooktrout.org/science-data/science-publications/what-predicts-the-use-by-brook-trout-salvelinus-fontinalis-of-terrestrial-invertebrate-subsidies-in-headwater-streams)

**Rainbow trout / juvenile steelhead (science)**
- Godby, Rutherford & Mason 2007, NAJFM 27(2):578–592, "Diet, Feeding Rate, Growth, Mortality, and Production of Juvenile Steelhead in a Lake Michigan Tributary": Muskegon River main stem and Bigelow Creek, 1998–2001; parr diet categories and gut contents analyzed; "remarkable seasonal dietary patterns in age-0 steelhead between the two neighbouring riverine systems." (Abstract text was paywalled; specific prey percentages not retrieved.) — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1577/M06-077.1); [UM Deep Blue](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/141847?show=full)
- Michigan DNR steelhead page: "During the lake-dwelling part of their life cycle, they primarily feed on baitfish like alewives or rainbow smelt, although at times they will feed on insects and other aquatic life... Although they feed primarily in mid-depths, they do occasionally take surface insects." — [Michigan DNR Steelhead](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/steelhead)
- Michigan Sea Grant lesson: young steelhead feed on zooplankton and aquatic insects; adults in the lake eat small fish and insects. — [Michigan Sea Grant: Searching for Steelhead](https://www.michiganseagrant.org/lessons/lessons/by-broad-concept/life-science/searching-for-steelhead/)
- Lake diet context (science, Lake Michigan): MSU/Sea Grant diet study dissected 4,538 trout/salmon/walleye stomachs in 2018; Lake Michigan steelhead diets >90% alewife in 2017–2019, with more insects in spring and a shift to alewife in summer; "terrestrial insects were abundant in steelhead stomachs in the spring" near thermal fronts. — [Michigan Sea Grant diet study](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/fish-diet-study-reaches-another-milestone-msg19-okeefe19); [Sea Grant steelhead declines](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/steelhead-declines-spur-discussion-of-research-and-management-options-msg21-okeefe21)
- REGIONAL (Lake Erie, 2004, 349 stomachs, lake-caught): 72% contained food; diets 74–94% fish by occurrence; emerald shiners and rainbow smelt in 82–91% of stomachs; round gobies important in East Basin; terrestrial "moths, lady bugs, and terrestrial beetles" frequent in June. — [GLFC Lake Erie steelhead diet report](https://www.glfc.org/pubs/lake_committees/erie/CWTG_docs/other_reports_and_docs/fin_rep_2004_steelhead_diet_project.pdf)

**Guide observations on resident trout forage in Michigan rivers (angler sources)**
- Kevin Feenstra (Muskegon): resident brown and rainbow trout consume "varieties of types of fry," stoneflies, caddis, sulphurs, gray drakes, Isonychia; "Nymphs and small streamers" in winter. — [Feenstra Gamefish](https://feenstraoutdoors.com/wordpress/gamefish-2/)
- Betts Guide Service (Muskegon/Big Manistee), sucker spawn May–June: "Over 100,000 Red Horse Suckers ascend the Muskegon River"; big trout feed behind sucker redds on "sucker eggs, nymphs, and steelhead fry"; "Salmon parr, steelhead fry, and sucker fry are making up the bulk of the trout diet"; "King salmon fry (salmon smolt) are the main food source in the river at this time." — [Betts sucker spawn](https://bettsguideservice.com/michigan-trout-guides-sucker-spawn/); [Betts spring steelhead report](https://bettsguideservice.com/muskegon-river-fishing-report-spring-steelhead/)
- Outfish Au Sable guide (angler source): crayfish described as "one of the main staples of a brown trout's diet." — [Outfish Au Sable](https://www.outfish.in/au_sable/fishing-guide)

### Inferences
- For a recommendation engine: brown trout ≥12 in should be modelled as primarily piscivorous in Michigan rivers year-round (Au Sable 70–80% fish; Anna River age IV+ "primarily piscivorous"), with prey = small trout, sculpin, coho/salmon/steelhead smolts and fry, plus "coarse fish" (dace/shiners/darters). Sub-12-in browns and brook trout are insect-dominant (caddis in the Anna River), with terrestrials likely a large summer share (by analogy with WV data).
- Sculpins being ~90% of available prey in a UP stream but under-selected relative to salmonids implies sculpin patterns are "always available" imitations, while smolt/fry/parr patterns are seasonally very high-value (May smolt outmigration; spring fry).

### Gaps
- No located Michigan study quantifying Hexagenia nymphs, crayfish, scuds, sowbugs, leeches, or terrestrials as a percentage of resident brown/brook/rainbow diet in the Au Sable, Manistee, Pere Marquette, or Muskegon. Alexander 1977 reports only the fish fraction; the invertebrate breakdown (Tables 1–2) was not extracted.
- No Michigan resident rainbow trout (non-steelhead) river diet study located.
- Godby et al. 2007 prey percentages (juvenile steelhead, Muskegon) not accessible (paywall).

## KQ2. Do steelhead feed actively in Michigan rivers (fall, winter, spring), on what, and what are the standard Michigan steelhead nymph and swing flies?

### Takeaway
Michigan agencies and guides agree steelhead feed in rivers — on eggs (salmon in fall, suckers/steelhead in spring), stonefly and Hex nymphs, caddis larvae, salmon/sucker/steelhead fry, and baitfish (shiners in fall, sculpins/gobies/darters in winter) — but I found no Michigan-specific adult in-river stomach-content study; the "~10% of winter steelhead have food, 13% had eggs" figures come from an angler article that does not cite a Michigan study. Standard Michigan flies: Hex nymph #6–10, black stone #6–12, green caddis #6–12, eggs #6–10, Steelhead Bugger #6, black leeches for high water, and swung Feenstra leeches/Intruders #2–4.

### Cited Findings

**Feeding behavior (agency and guide sources)**
- Michigan DNR: "Lake steelhead enter their spawning streams from late October to early May. Although many steelhead enter the rivers in the fall and overwinter there, spawning does not occur until spring. The fall-run fish are typically the first to spawn, often in March, followed by the spring run fish in April... Steelhead don't necessarily die after spawning." — [Michigan DNR Steelhead](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/steelhead)
- Feenstra (Muskegon): steelhead "enter West Michigan rivers as early as late September," peak fall by mid-October, peak spring "late March and April"; they "feed heavily on salmon fry, stoneflies, and eggs"; swinging streamers/spey flies fall–winter, nymphing in spring. — [Feenstra Gamefish](https://feenstraoutdoors.com/wordpress/gamefish-2/)
- Feenstra seasonal baitfish (Anchored Outdoors): Fall — migratory fish switch from lake alewife to river shiners ("Shiners are small, silvery baitfish that school up in three to six feet of water"; flashy patterns effective). Winter — sculpins ("They don't have swim bladders, so they can't hover. Instead, they dart a short distance and then sink back down"), round gobies, darters; olive/tan earth tones. Spring — "Salmon fry hatch in the spring and quickly become a significant part of the food chain"; sucker fry have a "purplish hue" so lavender/pink tones work; trout and steelhead feed aggressively. — [Anchored Outdoors / Feenstra](https://anchoredoutdoors.com/guaranteed-to-catch-more-fish-understanding-baitfish-seasons-with-kevin-feenstra/)
- Betts (Muskegon spring, March–early June): "Both larger Trout and Steelhead have been feeding heavily on Chinook Salmon fry along the shoreline"; salmon fry patterns, eggs/beads, green caddis; "Brighter colored flies, beads, and spawn have absolutely been pummeling Steelhead" in tea-stained 3–5 ft visibility. — [Betts spring steelhead](https://bettsguideservice.com/muskegon-river-fishing-report-spring-steelhead/)
- Caddis Shack (UP): fall steelhead enter "when salmon and browns are spawning" seeking an "easy egg buffet"; winter fish "hunker down" in larger rivers; "Late April and early May is prime time in the Upper Peninsula"; fish "consume nymphs and sculpin-like patterns year-round." — [Caddis Shack UP steelhead](https://www.caddisshackguideservice.com/blog/steelhead-fly-fishing-in-the-upper-peninsula)
- Michigan Sportsman forum (anecdotal): Michigan steelhead stomachs reported to contain salmon eggs, bugs, crayfish, rocks, sticks, leaves, baitfish, and artificial flies. — [Michigan Sportsman: Steelhead Facts](https://www.michigan-sportsman.com/threads/steelhead-facts.80757/)
- MidCurrent (angler article, unattributed studies): "90 percent of winter steelhead have empty stomachs" while "the remaining 10 percent contain salmon eggs, stonefly nymphs, and caddis larvae"; 13% of returning steelhead had eggs, some consuming up to 14 eggs daily; Great Lakes fish feed more than Pacific Northwest fish because eggs drift all winter; spring little black stoneflies (Allocapnia) in March with steelhead taking nymphs more than adults. Recommended: Glo-bug eggs #8–12 (orange, apricot, pale yellow), 10 mm beads, black stonefly nymphs #8–12, Hare's Ear #10–14; for aggression: Intruders 2–4 in (pink/orange, black/blue), bunny leeches with hot-spot heads #2–6, Egg-Sucking Leech #4–8, marabou speys in flame orange or chartreuse. — [MidCurrent: What do winter steelhead eat](https://midcurrent.com/v2/what-do-winter-steelhead-eat-a-guide-to-fly-selection/)
- Perfect Fly (St. Marys): steelhead "show up in the rapids in the middle of May with the run continuing into the middle of June"; steelhead flies there imitate "the Hexigenia mayfly nymph, certain caddisfly nymphs, stonefly nymphs and fish roe or eggs." — [Perfect Fly St. Marys](https://perfectflystore.com/your-streams/fly-fishing-the-st-marys-river-michigan/)

**Standard Michigan steelhead flies (guide sources)**
- Current Works "Top 5 Steelhead Flies for Michigan": Hex nymph #6–10 (Bear's Hex Nymph, Schultzy's Rabbit Hexum, Ted's Edible Hex, Fox's Shuck Hex) — "Hexagenia mayfly nymphs or 'wigglers' are significant, since many of our rivers with steelhead also have this silt burrowing mayfly"; Caddis #6–12 in green/chartreuse (bright) and olive/cream (natural) — Disco Caddis, Boogie Woogie Caddis, PM Caddis, Flashy Brassie Grub, naturals for "low, clear water"; Steelhead Bugger #6 (Umpqua); Eggs #6–10 — Glo Bugs (Oregon Cheese, Golden, Chartreuse), Crystal Eggs (Peach, Chartreuse), Nuke Egg, Rag Eggs ("Tandem fly rigs are the norm... one of the flies is an egg pattern"); Stonefly #6–12 — Mercer's Biot Epoxy Stone (dark), Trout Retriever, Ted's Early Black Stone, 20 Incher ("The first insect of the year to hatch in significant numbers is the small black stonefly"); Leeches (black) — String Leech, Silvey's Tail Light, Miles Davis, Larimer's Reverse Marabou for "High, dirty water conditions." — [Current Works Top 5](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/top-5-steelhead-flies/)
- Chuck Hawkins (Orvis) spring Great Lakes steelhead top five: Clown Egg (pre-runoff pinks/oranges dime-sized; runoff chartreuse/orange/bright yellow quarter-sized; post-runoff muted natural dime-sized); Rabbit Wiggle Nymph (turbid water); Black Stone tied large during runoff ("Black Stones begin to hatch in spring, with lots of them moving around the bottom"); Ed's Salmon Alevin (dead-drifted or swung; also taken as caddis larva because spawning steelhead dislodge insects); Feenstra's Shrew post-runoff for drop-backs, matching "gobies, sculpins, and chubs." — [Orvis: Top five spring GL steelhead flies](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/pro-tips-top-five-flies-for-spring-great-lakes-steelhead)
- Chuck Hawkins Top 10 Michigan flies: Clown Egg ("will fool most salmon and steelhead"); Parr None — "Great pattern for early spring in the Midwest... any river with a migration of salmon parr" for salmon, steelhead, brown trout, pike. — [Orvis: Hawkins Top 10](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/tuesday-tip-chuck-hawkinss-top-10-michigan-flies)
- Caddis Shack UP nymph rig: double-bead stoneflies #8–10 black/brown, Pat's Rubberleg #8–10, beadhead Woolly Bugger #8 black/green, Hex nymph #8, eggs #8–10; spring adds white Zonkers and White Deaths #8; trailer flies: 8 mm trout beads, beadhead pheasant tail #10, caddis larva #10, black stone #10. Swing: "Feenstra's Grapefruit Leech (#size 2-4 3 X long streamer hook)," Halloween Leech, Spey Buggers, Intruder-style #2–4. — [Caddis Shack](https://www.caddisshackguideservice.com/blog/steelhead-fly-fishing-in-the-upper-peninsula)
- Feenstra swing patterns: Grapefruit Leech — "bright pink and chartreuse head... a top choice for winter steelhead or cold water lethargy"; "one of the most reliable Midwest swung fly patterns and is exceptional in cold winter temperatures." — [MCA Grapefruit Leech](https://mca.fish/products/feenstras-grapefuit-leech). Halloween Leech — black marabou/schlappen, peacock and orange Ice Dub, pink and copper Flashabou, 25 mm Senyo shank with trailer or Daiichi 2461; "Originating from the Muskegon River, this is a great swing fly for Great Lakes Steelhead"; "great through fall." — [Nomad Anglers](https://nomadanglers.com/blogs/fly-tying-patterns/tying-kevin-feenstras-halloween-leech); [Trident](https://www.tridentflyfishing.com/products/feenstras-halloween-leech-fly). Emulator — "a versatile searching pattern that works year-round for multiple species." — [Feenstra fly gallery](https://www.feenstraguideservice.com/flygallery.html). Aqua Nuisance — "primarily imitates a sculpin or a large baitfish," size 1, rainbow/copper, "shines during the fall and spring months when fish are aggressive," low light and stained water, for "steelhead, salmon, and large trout." — [Trident Aqua Nuisance](https://www.tridentflyfishing.com/products/feenstras-aqua-nuisance-fly). Feenstra's listed patterns: Australian Possum Hex, Aquatic Nuisance, BTS, Emulator, Grapefruit Leech, Grease Stain, Halloween Leech, Psycho Sculpin, Reflector, River Chicken. — [Feenstra fly gallery](https://www.feenstraguideservice.com/flygallery.html)
- Feenstra's book "Matching Baitfish: Patterns and Techniques for Great Lakes Steelhead and Lake Run Browns" (Stackpole). — [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Matching-Baitfish-Patterns-Techniques-Steelhead/dp/0811737152)
- Egg-Sucking Leech: popular steelhead fly; described (Current Works) as "less intrusive than most patterns and helps target the selective steelhead which tend to be more common than aggressive steelhead." — [Wikipedia ESL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_sucking_leech); [Current Works steelhead articles](http://www.current-works.com/category/related-articles/fly-fishing-steelhead-northern-michigan/page/2/)
- Mangled Fly (Ed McCoy, March 2024): lamprey patterns with "long and 'leechy' appearance with lots of undulating movement," 4–7 in, for spring streamer fishing; Jon Ray: "Fall Steelhead fishing is truly fantastic" with strong predatory behavior. — [Mangled Fly brown trout/steelhead posts](https://mangledfly.com/category/brown-trout/)

### Inferences
- Seasonal steelhead forage model for Michigan: Fall (late Sep–Nov) = salmon eggs + shiners + aggression (swing bright/dark leeches, ESL, eggs). Winter (Dec–Feb) = sculpins/gobies/darters, stonefly nymphs, lingering eggs (slow swing, olive/tan or Grapefruit Leech; nymph small stones, Hex nymph, eggs). Spring (Mar–May) = black stoneflies, Hex nymphs, caddis larvae, steelhead/sucker eggs, salmon fry/alevin, sucker fry; drop-backs eat fry and sculpins (Shrew, Parr None, alevin patterns).
- Hex nymph patterns should be tagged for steelhead on silt-bottomed rivers (Muskegon, Manistee, Pere Marquette, Au Sable, St. Marys) — Michigan-specific, not generic Great Lakes.

### Gaps
- No Michigan adult steelhead in-river stomach-content study found; the 10%/13% statistics are uncited in the MidCurrent article and may be Pacific Northwest data.
- Michigan Sea Grant/MSU steelhead diet program is lake-caught fish only.

## KQ3. Do Chinook, coho, and pink salmon take flies in Michigan rivers, why, and what patterns/colors?

### Takeaway
Michigan DNR states Pacific salmon "do not feed but will often strike out of aggression" on the spawning run (stated on the Atlantic salmon page for salmon generally); FISHBIO and popular accounts attribute river strikes to territorial/redd defense, while an MSU Extension article (blocked from fetching) reportedly claims "some Pacific salmon DO feed during spawning run." Guides use large, bright or dark flies to trigger aggression: black/olive/purple Woolly Buggers, Egg-Sucking Leeches with chartreuse/orange/red/pink heads, Intruders/temple dogs #4–1/0 in chartreuse, red, orange, black, blue, flash flies and Clousers for coho, and eggs/beads 8–14 mm; pinks (St. Marys, Carp, Chocolay; heaviest odd years) take small pink/chartreuse flies (#8 buggers, #12–20 nymphs, eggs).

### Cited Findings

**Feeding physiology / why they strike**
- Michigan DNR (Atlantic salmon page, generalizing): "Salmon in the lake eat crustaceans, but seek out smelt, alewives and other available forage. While on their spawning run, they do not feed but will often strike out of aggression." — [Michigan DNR Atlantic salmon](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/atlantic-salmon)
- Bridge Michigan: "During spawning season, adult salmon stop eating and dedicate all of their energy to swimming upstream, where they often die after spawning." — [Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-environment-watch/michigans-fall-salmon-migration-run-has-begun-anglers-are-salivating/)
- FISHBIO (fisheries consultancy, general Pacific salmon): "Once salmon enter streams and rivers they typically stop feeding and go into defensive mode"; spawning salmon "will defend their spawning area from spawning salmon or other fish species"; females "aggressively defend their nest (i.e. redd)"; they "bite shiny or brightly colored objects like lures" through territorial aggression (cites Baxter 1991). — [FISHBIO: The bite's on](https://fishbio.com/the-bites-on/)
- Popular account: salmon "stomach is no longer needed and it begins to disintegrate internally — leaving more room for the developing eggs and sperm." — [Biology Insights](https://biologyinsights.com/when-and-where-do-salmon-spawn-in-michigan/)
- MSU Extension article titled "Amazing revelation: Some Pacific salmon DO feed during spawning run" exists but could not be fetched (Incapsula block). Search snippet from a secondary page: "salmon were believed to stop feeding in rivers, but recent research suggests this isn't always the case... Anglers in Michigan rivers have long known that Chinook and coho salmon will bite on eggs and egg imitations." — [MSU Extension (URL only)](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/amazing_revelation_some_pacific_salmon_do_feed_during_spawning_run); snippet via [Trout and Steelhead: Why do salmon run](https://troutandsteelhead.net/why-do-salmon-run-and-when/)
- Michigan DNR Chinook page: "Primary food source is alewives, but other prey species such as rainbow smelt and bloaters are also common"; river anglers use "spoons, spinners and plugs — as well as with spawn"; best inland stream fishing "in the Manistee, Pere Marquette and St. [Joseph]." — [Michigan DNR Chinook](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/chinook-salmon)
- Michigan DNR Coho page: "larger coho feed primarily on smelt and alewives; however, they are opportunistic feeders"; "Depending on the tributary, coho spawning runs occur from early September to November. Both male and female adults die soon after spawning." — [Michigan DNR Coho](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/coho-salmon)
- Michigan DNR Pink salmon page: "Great Lakes pink salmon eat a variety of fish and other aquatic animals. Pink salmon spawning runs begin in the summer in the Great Lakes"; introduced unintentionally mid-1950s; adults after ~18 months in the lake. — [Michigan DNR Pink salmon](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/pink-salmon)

**Run timing**
- Chinook enter "as early as mid-August on the Betsie River and the season extends into October on the Big Manistee River." — [Current Works salmon](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-seasons/salmon-betsie-manistee-northern-michigan/)
- Muskegon: kings and coho "enter the Muskegon in August and September," peak October; kings average 12–13 lb. — [Feenstra Gamefish](https://feenstraoutdoors.com/wordpress/gamefish-2/)
- Coho: "tributary runs typically begin in mid-September and peak in October, slightly later than chinook runs"; rivers: Platte (premier coho stream), Pere Marquette, Manistee below Tippy, Betsie, Little Manistee, Muskegon, St. Joseph (fish into December). — [Michigan Fishing Guide coho](https://www.michiganfishing.guide/species/coho-salmon/)
- UP: Chinook/coho run "late September" through "early November"; 6–20 lb; surf casting at river mouths with Spey rods early, then nymphing single-hand in rivers; "Salmon will chase properly presented streamers quite aggressively." — [Rivers North Chinook & Coho](https://www.riversnorth.net/chinookcohosalmon.html)
- Great Lakes generally: Chinooks arrive "early September or sometimes even late August," cohos "consistent fishing into October." — [Flylords: Salmon of the Great Lakes](https://flylordsmag.com/salmon-of-the-great-lakes/)

**Fly patterns and colors (guide/angler sources)**
- Flylords: "Popular patterns are woolly buggers in black, olive, and purple. Egg-sucking leeches with the same body colors but with a chart, orange, red and pink hotspot." Swing Intruders and temple dogs on shanks/tubes, hook "size 4 to 1/0... In chartreuse, red, orange, black, and blue being the top producers." Beads 8–14 mm, natural and bright. Techniques: Spey, single-hand, indicator, free-drift, streamer stripping. — [Flylords](https://flylordsmag.com/salmon-of-the-great-lakes/)
- Into Fly Fishing (Michigan guide): "Egg Sucking Leeches, Intruders, and Chicken Littles... bright flies that bring out angry strikes from the salmon"; salmon "strike out of aggression, which is why most people recommend big and bright flies"; swing or dead-drift, strike at the head of the pool. — [Into Fly Fishing Michigan](https://intoflyfishing.com/fly-fishing-michigan/)
- Coho: "fly anglers swing egg-sucking leeches, woolly buggers, and bright streamer patterns through runs and tailouts on 7- to 8-weight rods." — [Michigan Fishing Guide coho](https://www.michiganfishing.guide/species/coho-salmon/). Flash Flies (Pope's, California Neon, Pink Pop Cycle) with Krystal Flash/Flashabou wings; chartreuse/white, pink/white, all-pink Clousers; black/olive/chartreuse Woolly Buggers "especially in off-color water"; black-chartreuse or pink-chartreuse Intruder combos; "Chartreuse is great for low light or slightly murky water." (General coho sources, not Michigan-specific.) — [Big Y coho flies](https://bigyflyco.com/collections/coho-silver-salmon); [coho fly patterns guide](https://cohoflypatterns.pages.dev/posts/coho-fly-patterns/)
- Betsie: "Dark leech patterns with flash, egg patterns, and Egg Sucking Leeches work on the Betsie... dead drift or swing those streamers." — [Into Fly Fishing Michigan](https://intoflyfishing.com/fly-fishing-michigan/)
- Pere Marquette salmon pattern recipe: black schlappen collar palmered over "UV Polar Chenille Chartreuse" body; woolly buggers essential; steelhead flies "black and brown to shades of pink and chartreuse." — [Outfitters North PM report](https://outfittersnorth.com/pere-marquette-river-fishing-report/); [DIY Fly Fishing PM](https://diyflyfishing.com/pere-marquette-river-michigan/)
- Current Works salmon technique: nymphing with floating lines or "duck and chuck," streamers on sink tips with 9–10 wt for fresh fish; patterns referenced: Sparrow Nymph, Rabbit Strip Leech, Flashtail Clouser. — [Current Works salmon](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-seasons/salmon-betsie-manistee-northern-michigan/)

**Pink salmon (UP)**
- "The two rivers in Michigan that see Pink Salmon runs each September are the Saint Mary's River and the Carp River of the Upper Peninsula, with runs occurring every year and the heaviest numbers returning on odd-numbered years." Michigan record 8.56 lb / 28 in from the Carp River (1987). — [Yooper Webcam pink salmon](https://yooperwebcam.com/fishing-database-mi/pink-salmon/); [DIY Carp River](https://diyflyfishing.com/carp-river-michigan/)
- Chocolay River (Marquette Co.): "diminishing runs of Chinook and Coho salmon, and strong runs of pink salmon." — [DIY Chocolay](https://diyflyfishing.com/chocolay-river-michigan/)
- St. Marys: "Pink Salmon enter the river in late Summer" and remain "in the rapids until mid October." — [Perfect Fly St. Marys](https://perfectflystore.com/your-streams/fly-fishing-the-st-marys-river-michigan/)
- "The pinks and king salmon spawn starts in late Aug to early Sep and peaks the later part of Sep." — [Yooper Webcam](https://yooperwebcam.com/fishing-database-mi/pink-salmon/)
- REGIONAL (Minnesota North Shore, Lake Superior): run "usually begins in September but can start as early as August or as late as October"; originally even-year spawners, now annual; nymphs #12–20 (Frenchies, pheasant tails, copper johns, prince nymphs, scuds, zebra midges) in clear water; bright eggs (glo-bugs/beads) in faster water; "size 8 bead-headed wooly bugger in black, olive or pink" or Muddler, weighted, short quick strips. — [Minnesota DNR pink salmon](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/fishing/trout/pink-salmon.html); [JS-Outdoors](https://js-outdoors.com/superior-pink-salmon/)
- General: "Pink salmon generally favor pink colored flies, and smaller bead-head flies in bright pink or chartreuse with ample amounts of flash are preferred." — [Flylords pink salmon](https://flylordsmag.com/catching-humpbacks-on-a-fly-rod-get-to-know-the-pink-salmon/)

### Inferences
- Engine rule: for Chinook/coho/pink in rivers, do not match "diet"; match triggers — size/contrast (black, purple, chartreuse, hot orange/pink heads), flash (coho especially), and eggs/beads presented to fish holding below spawning fish. Fresh chrome fish near the mouth (late Aug–Sep) respond to swung/stripped streamers; staged/spawning fish (Oct) to dead-drifted eggs, ESLs, and nymph-style flies.
- Pinks: scale everything down (#8 buggers, #10–14 eggs, #12–20 nymphs) in pink/chartreuse; window late Aug–early Oct, odd years strongest.

- No Michigan-specific published pink salmon fly list from a UP shop was found; regional Lake Superior (MN) recommendations were used.

## KQ4. Atlantic salmon in Michigan: where, run timing, do they feed, which flies?

### Takeaway
Michigan's main Atlantic fishery is the St. Marys River rapids at Sault Ste. Marie (LSSU Aquatic Research Lab stocks ~30,000–40,000/yr since 1987); fish enter in late June, eat smelt/juvenile salmonids early (streamers), then switch to Hex nymphs, caddis, and midges by mid-July (indicator nymphing), spawn late Oct–Nov and can be caught nearly year-round. DNR also stocks Torch Lake, Lexington Harbor, Thunder Bay River, and the lower Au Sable (resumed 2013; Foote Dam fish caught fall through April on streamers, nymphs, plugs, spawn). Despite DNR's general statement that salmon "do not feed" on the run, St. Marys guides and DNR-quoted biologists describe Atlantics as opportunistic in-river feeders.

### Cited Findings
- Michigan DNR: "The primary fishery is in the St. Mary's River, while the state maintains a fishery in Torch Lake (Antrim Co) with stocked fish, as well as Lexington Harbor, Thunder Bay River and Au Sable River each spring under an experimental stocking program. Fall returns of spawning fish occur at each location, primarily in Oct.-Dec."; "the St. Mary's River run typically begins in mid-summer and runs until November when spawning commences. When spawning is finished the adults may rest in the river for some time and then return to the lake. Atlantic salmon can potentially spawn multiple years." Also: "While on their spawning run, they do not feed but will often strike out of aggression." — [Michigan DNR Atlantic salmon](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/atlantic-salmon)
- Torch Lake: 18,770-acre oligotrophic lake with rainbow smelt and cisco prey base; average 31,000 yearlings stocked annually 1998–2006; "Stocking has established a popular fishery in Torch Lake." — [MDNR Fisheries Report 17 (review of Atlantic salmon attributes)](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/FisheriesReports/FR017.pdf); [Shoreline Scout Torch Lake](https://shorelinescout.com/michigan/antrim/torch-lake/fishing/)
- Au Sable: "Beginning in 2013, the Michigan DNR resumed stocking Atlantic Salmon in the lower Au Sable, with over 40,000 juvenile Atlantics released in 2023 at the Whirlpool Launch. Returns have been increasing each year." — [Streamside Au Sable guides](https://michigan-streamside.com/atlantic-salmon-in-michigan/)
- Au Sable (Gwizdz/Great Lakes Angler): fish caught below Foote Dam (~8 miles above Lake Huron); DNR's Tim Cwalinski: "I know people who fish there and catch fish all winter – these fish are being caught in January, February, March and April"; anglers use jerk baits, "fly rods with streamers and nymphs, and spawn"; "One day last week we moved about 15 fish on streamers"; 2-year-olds 20–24 in, 3-year-olds 25–30 in, largest low-30s; DNR began rearing at a second hatchery to increase production. — [Great Lakes Angler: Au Sable Atlantics](https://www.glangler.com/blogs/articles/au-sable-atlantics-robert-gwizdz)
- Lake Huron: in 2010 DNR expanded Atlantic stocking in Lake Huron; Atlantics caught in Lake Huron, St. Marys, and St. Clair River near Port Huron. — [Michigan DNR Showcasing: Atlantic salmon](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MIDNR/bulletins/608a41)
- LSSU: Aquatic Research Laboratory "annually rears and releases 40,000 Atlantic Salmon inside the Sault Edison hydro plant"; average 33,437/yr over 22 years, 735,600 total since 1987; ~6–8% end up in creels vs 2% considered good. — [Rivers North Atlantic salmon](https://riversnorth.net/atlanticsalmon.html); [FishSens](https://www.fishsens.com/st-marys-rivers-atlantic-salmon-among-the-hardest-fighting-fish-in-the-great-lakes/)
- Run timing and feeding (Rivers North): "Late every June, the 2-5 year old Atlantic Salmon make the voyage upstream from Lake Huron"; average 2–8 lb; early season "Stripping large baitfish imitation patterns on sink tips can provide for some exciting strikes!"; "Shortly after the baitfish schools leave the river system, the Hexagenia hatch occurs in mid-July causing the Atlantics to switch their diet from baitfish to insects"; "Indicator nymphing is very productive." — [Rivers North Atlantic salmon](https://riversnorth.net/atlanticsalmon.html)
- FishSens (quoting Brad Petzke, Rivers North): early fish encounter smelt, juvenile salmon and steelhead; later "midges, caddis flies, and the region's prolific Hexagenia mayfly hatches"; "a very opportunistic fish"; "Essentially, you can catch Atlantics in this river 365 days a year"; fish spawn through November, overwinter, drop back May–June. — [FishSens](https://www.fishsens.com/st-marys-rivers-atlantic-salmon-among-the-hardest-fighting-fish-in-the-great-lakes/)
- Betts: guided St. Marys Atlantic trips "late July and early August"; "Atlantics feed on caddis, rainbow smelt and Hexagenia nymphs in the St. Mary's during the summers months"; spawn "in late October"; three-fish daily harvest limit. — [Betts Atlantic salmon](https://bettsguideservice.com/michigan-atlantic-salmon.html)
- Perfect Fly: "In late June, two to five year-old, five to eight pound Atlantic Salmon make their way upstream"; hold "below the Edison plant from June through August," spawn in the rapids late October; "Hexigenia nymphs... very popular flies for the St. Marys River during July"; also wet flies, egg flies, Spey flies. — [Perfect Fly St. Marys](https://perfectflystore.com/your-streams/fly-fishing-the-st-marys-river-michigan/)
- Creel/regional context: GLFC synthesis of St. Marys recreational fishing (2022 creel). — [GLFC St. Marys creel](https://www.glfc.org/pubs/lake_committees/huron/SMR%20Creel%202022%20report%20-%20final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Atlantic salmon are the one "salmon" in Michigan for which the engine should match diet: smelt/smolt streamers (white/silver, 3–5 in) late June–early July; Hex nymphs #6–8, caddis larvae/pupae, midges under indicators mid-July–August; swung wets/speys and eggs in fall; streamers and nymphs all winter at Foote Dam.
- The DNR's "do not feed" line is generic across salmon species; the St. Marys evidence from guides and the DNR-quoted Au Sable biologist indicates Atlantics do feed in-river, but no stomach-content study was found.

### Gaps
- No sourced Michigan-specific list of classic Atlantic dry/wet patterns (Bomber, Green Machine, Blue Charm, Sugerman Shrimp) in use on the St. Marys; Ontario Out of Doors and The New Fly Fisher articles (Canadian side) were blocked/empty. Treat those pattern names as unverified for Michigan.
- Au Sable Atlantic run months are inconsistent between sources (DNR: Oct–Dec spawning returns; Gwizdz: caught Jan–Apr) — both may be true (fall run, winter holding).

## KQ5. Streamers: Michigan tradition, patterns, sizes, colors by water/light, and seasonal calendar

### Takeaway
The modern articulated-streamer movement began in Michigan: Kelly Galloup's Zoo Cougar (mid-1990s, Manistee, Troutsman shop) and "Modern Streamers for Trophy Trout" (with Bob Linsenman, 1999), Russ Maddin's Circus Peanut (~2000), and Tommy Lynch's Drunk & Disorderly (Pere Marquette/Upper Manistee). Guides fish streamers hardest April–May and late September–November (plus winter on the extended-season Manistee/Boardman), rotating white/black/tan/olive/yellow/chartreuse by light and clarity — light (white/yellow) on sunny clear days, olive/gray on cloudy, black/dark or bright in dirty water — with 4–7 in flies for trophy browns and downsizing when fish are not committing.

### Cited Findings

**Michigan streamer tradition**
- "Kelly Galloup developed the Zoo Cougar fly in the mid-1990s for the Manistee River in Michigan where his first fly shop, the Troutsman fly shop was located. He purchased the Slide Inn on the Madison River in 2001 and moved to Montana in 2002." Zoo Cougar colors: black, olive, white, tan, gray, golden brown, yellow; sizes 2–6. — [RiverKeeper Flies: Zoo Cougar](https://www.johnkreft.com/galloups-zoo-cougar/)
- Zoo Cougar original is "Yellow, but mellow. A nice sulphur color deer hair and a dyed mallard flank feather also in yellow"; Galloup's palette runs "from pure white to almost totally black"; tied on "4's, 2's and even 1/0 hooks"; a Muddler-derived deer-hair head design fished on floating or sink-tip/sinking lines; documented in "Modern Streamers for Trophy Trout" (Galloup & Linsenman) and the "Streamer Flies for Trophy Trout" DVD. — [Global FlyFisher: Zoo Cougar](https://globalflyfisher.com/patterns-streamers/zoo-cougar)
- Wet Fly Swing (Galloup podcast): Galloup "spent a couple of years fishing nothing but streamers, and Russ Maddin joined in soon after, starting the whole era of modern streamer fishing. The Zoo Cougar and the Woolly Sculpin came early"; in 1999 Galloup and Bob Linsenman published "Modern Streamer Tactics for Trophy Trout." Galloup's origin story: watching bass pro Larry Nixon's "walk the dog" retrieve, then diving the Boardman River and finding big trout in unexpected lies; he saw a brown "with a five or six inch rainbow in its mouth halfway down his throat." — [Wet Fly Swing: Galloup](https://www.wetflyswing.com/kelly-galloup-on-the-best-streamer-strategies-for-giant-trout/)
- Articulated streamers "popularized by Kelly Galloup and the Michigan folks (for example, Russ Maddin, Mike Schmidt, and Tommy Lynch)." — [North American Fly Fishing Forum](https://www.theflyfishingforum.com/forums/index.php?threads/your-take-on-the-articulated-streamer-craze/502569/)
- Circus Peanut: "developed around 2000 by Russ Madden who worked for Kelly at his Troutsman fly shop located in Traverse City, MI"; "one of the patterns that ushered in the age of articulated streamers"; used "on the Au Sable and Manistee Rivers for big browns"; sold in Root Beer by Streamside. — [RiverKeeper Flies: Circus Peanut](https://www.johnkreft.com/circus-peanut-articulated-streamer/); [MidCurrent: Circus Peanut story](https://midcurrent.com/2025/07/13/video-the-story-behind-russ-maddins-circus-peanut/); [Streamside: Circus Peanut Root Beer](https://michigan-streamside.com/product/maddins-circus-peanut-root-beer/)
- Tommy Lynch (Fish Whisperer, Baldwin; 20+ years guiding the Pere Marquette) on the Drunk & Disorderly: "a fly that swims differently with every strip to mimic an injured baitfish"; "large but easy to cast, with a profile that matched natural baitfish—largest near the head and slender towards the rear"; "door wedge" deer-hair head; flash because "You only see minnows and fish when they are injured and flashing"; retrieve "strip very sharply but short and with no rhythm. One pop, followed by three short strips, and then let it swim and 'die out'"; on the PM and Upper Manistee "Several browns over 24 inches have fallen for the big fly, along with hundreds over 20 inches." — [Orvis: Drunk & Disorderly](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/big-and-meaty-tommy-lynchs-drunk-disorderly-streamer); [Hyde: Tommy Lynch](https://hydeoutdoors.com/pages/pro_staff/tommy-lynch); [Wet Fly Swing 347](https://www.wetflyswing.com/streamers-for-trout-with-tommy-lynch-brown-trout-drunk-and-disorderly-pere-marquette/)
- Chuck Hawkins: "Streamer fishing is a staple for our big, difficult trout" — Nutcracker (natural), Hat Trick (olive), Little Rascal (ginger); Wet Skunk "weighted... fished on a floating line near the structure can produce at times when nothing else will." — [Orvis: Hawkins Top 10](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/tuesday-tip-chuck-hawkinss-top-10-michigan-flies)

**Color, size, light, clarity (guide advice)**
- Galloup's rotation: "White, Black, Tan, Olive, Yellow, Chartreuse"; "when the river turns yellow in late afternoon, tan becomes the best option"; "when in doubt, don't go big. You go small" — big flies trigger aggression, smaller flies feed selective fish; "animate the fly with the rod, not long strips"; "if that fly doesn't initiate a move immediately, I think you've missed eighty five percent of your fish"; stall "about one or two seconds" over structure. — [Wet Fly Swing: Galloup](https://www.wetflyswing.com/kelly-galloup-on-the-best-streamer-strategies-for-giant-trout/)
- MeatEater (Kubie Brown), fall streamers: high/muddy — dark black/brown/olive for contrast, "big profile, a lot of flash, and a heavy weighted head" (Dolly Llama, Galloup's Nancy P, Mike's Meal Ticket); average flows — "lighter colors such as white or yellow on sunny days or darker colors like olive or gray on cloudy days" (Dally's Twerking Minnow, Circus Peanut, Galloup's Sex Dungeon, Cheech Leech); low/clear — "neutrally buoyant flies... sleek natural profile, and a small amount of flash" (Schmidt's Double Deceiver, Junk Yard Dog, Sluggo); fall is when "big trout become more active in search of calories before winter"; 8–10 in flies for trophy hunting. — [MeatEater](https://www.themeateater.com/fish/freshwater/how-to-pick-the-best-streamer-for-big-fall-trout)
- Galloup pattern color notes (retail): Sex Dungeon natural "mimics everything from sculpins to juvenile trout and chub minnows... go-to pattern in clear water or high-pressure conditions"; olive for "clear to slightly stained water"; "Crawdad Orange is used in off-colored or stained water." — [Flies and Guides: Sex Dungeon Olive](https://flysandguides.com/product/galloups-sex-dungeon-olive/); [Murray's: Dungeon](https://www.murraysflyshop.com/products/galloups-dungeon-fly)
- Mangled Fly (Ed McCoy, Sept 2026): "Beefing up the fly size seemed to be what the fish wanted lately"; recommended 4–7 in to match prey; "The streamer fishing should be the main focus" in fall. — [Mangled Fly brown trout](https://mangledfly.com/category/brown-trout/)
- Muskegon lake-run browns: "meat eaters," "the most productive flies to catch them imitate sculpins and other bait fishes"; most abundant "late September through March." — [Feenstra Gamefish](https://feenstraoutdoors.com/wordpress/gamefish-2/)
- Michigan TU Fall 2021 "Streamer Fishing: How to Decide What Flies to Fish" (issuu; body text not retrievable): snippet — in spring on rivers with a previous fall salmon run, "casting an alevin or smelt pattern can be effective, as there are huge numbers of these available and trout gorge on them"; imitate "sculpins, gobies, darters, shiners, lamprey, or a bunch of other things" depending on reach. — [Michigan TU issuu](https://issuu.com/www.michigantu.org/docs/michigan_trout_fall_2021-web/s/14447231)

**Seasonal streamer calendar (guide sources)**
- Current Works (northern Michigan): April–May "These are the months to throw streamers for some trophy fish" (sink tips, 20+ in fish); September–November "Water temps have dropped and streamer fishing gets going again," peak below Tippy Dam late Oct–Nov; winter/early spring extended season on Manistee and Boardman yields "some of the largest fish of the year" on streamers. — [Current Works trout seasons](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-seasons/trout-traverse-city-northern-michigan/)
- Mangled Fly: Jon Ray (March 2022) — spring streamer fishing as escape from steelhead crowds; Ed McCoy (Sept 2026) — fall streamer focus. — [Mangled Fly](https://mangledfly.com/category/brown-trout/)
- DNR: brown trout spawn September–October (pre-spawn aggression window inferred). — [Michigan DNR Brown trout](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brown-trout)

### Inferences
- Engine defaults for brown trout streamers in Michigan: spring (Apr–May, high/stained) — dark or bright, heavy heads, 4–6 in (Sex Dungeon black/olive, Circus Peanut, Nancy P, D&D); summer — smaller sculpin/baitfish (Zoo Cougar #2–6, Woolly Sculpin, Muddler) at dawn/dusk; fall (late Sep–Nov, pre-spawn) — largest flies 5–7 in, yellow/white on bright days, olive/gray cloudy, black low light; winter — slow, natural olive/tan sculpin imitations on the Manistee/Boardman/Muskegon.
- Resident rainbows and brook trout: same colors, downsized (#4–8 Woolly Buggers, Muddlers, small Zoo Cougars).

### Gaps
- Galloup's own written "color by clarity" rules (from the Slide Inn / book) were not directly retrieved; the rotation quote is from a podcast summary.
- Russ Maddin's original Circus Peanut colors/size and intended imitation not confirmed from a primary source.

## KQ6. Terrestrial season, mousing, and Hex night fishing in Michigan

### Takeaway
Michigan terrestrial fishing runs mid-May to October with a July–August peak (hoppers, ants incl. flying ants, beetles, crickets after rain, cicadas); Hex hatches mid-June to mid-July after dark (10 pm–midnight) on the Au Sable/Manistee system with #6–8 nymph/dun/spinner patterns; mousing is a July–September night game (late Aug–Sep prime) with deer-hair/foam mice (Morrish Mouse #4–6, Master Splinter, McCoy's Mouse, Amphibious Assault, gurglers, frogs) fished on a slow wake in shallow flats.

### Cited Findings

**Terrestrials**
- Current Works: terrestrial activity begins mid-May, peaks July–August, extends through September–October; more active "Mid-day as air temperatures rise and dew-fall burns off"; types: grasshoppers, ants (incl. flying ants), crickets, beetles, cicadas; patterns: Sweetgrass Hopper, Galloup's Ant Acid, XO Beetle, Tar Baby, Chernobyl Ants, Skunks, Fuzzy Wuzzies, Club Sandwiches; technique "Hit the bank and plop, twitch and dead-drift"; sunken ants; crickets "after a good rainfall as they often get washed into the river"; beetles as dropper behind a visible pattern; rivers: Upper and Lower Manistee, Betsie, Boardman. — [Current Works terrestrials](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/terrestrial-fishing-grasshopper/)
- Current Works post-Hex: after the Hex, Isonychia, Light Cahills, BWOs, and terrestrials carry July–August. — [Current Works after Hex](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/fishing-after-the-hex-hatch/)
- RiverReports (Au Sable): "August is prime terrestrial season"; patterns Fat Albert, Parachute Hopper, Charlie Boy Hopper, Moorish Hopper; black beetles "around overhanging trees, log jams, and undercut banks"; black and cinnamon flying ants "during late-summer mating flights"; fish them "close to grassy banks, overhanging tag alder and cedar limbs." — [RiverReports Au Sable](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/rivers/au-sable-river-michigan-fly-fishing)
- Hawkins: "Hex and Sulphur hatches followed by great terrestrial action with grasshoppers and ants in July and August." — [Hawkins trout](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/species/trout/night_fishing_for_trout/)
- Current Works seasons: July–August "Grasshoppers and other terrestrial patterns come out of the box." — [Current Works trout seasons](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-seasons/trout-traverse-city-northern-michigan/)

**Hex night fishing**
- Hawkins: "Look for hexes to appear on lakes in northern Michigan in early to mid-June. They will start on the rivers 7 to 10 days after they hatch on the lakes. Timing is usually mid-June to mid-July"; "Hexes hatch, mate and die primarily in the dark in late June"; nymphs on "Size 6 or 8 hooks"; "These big mayflies bring the largest fish in the river up to the surface"; patterns: Robert's Yellow Drake tied large, Ed McCoy's All Day Dun, Boondoggle Hex. — [Hawkins Hexagenia](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/hexagenia-limbata/)
- Gates Au Sable Lodge (June 2017): "major hatches of the Hexagenia Limbata are spreading through the entire Au Sable river system"; "a dark river filled with nocturnal brown trout"; size 6 Hex patterns; concurrent Isonychia, sulphurs, yellow/golden stones, caddis. — [Gates: Hexin'](https://gateslodge.com/2017/06/hexin/); late-June variability: [Gates: Late hex](https://gateslodge.com/2019/06/late-hex/)
- RiverReports: Hex "occurs from late June through July... begins after dark — typically 10pm to midnight." — [RiverReports Au Sable](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/rivers/au-sable-river-michigan-fly-fishing)
- Current Works: "Hex fishing is the time of the year that 2-1/2″ – plus mayflies hatch in the last minutes of light" (June). — [Current Works trout seasons](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-seasons/trout-traverse-city-northern-michigan/)
- Streamside: "Hex Hatch Is On - All Night Long." — [Streamside](https://michigan-streamside.com/hex-hatch-is-on-all-night-long/)

**Mousing**
- Mangled Fly (Ed McCoy): "Peak times to target trout while Mousing range from July to September"; patterns McCoy's Mouse, Amphibious Assault, waking flies, gurglers, frogs; cast tight to banks with popping strips; "the larger fish in our rivers still prefer to hunt at night"; late summer "food is limiting making for hungry trout hunting at night"; "Unfortunately in Michigan we need the darkness to make it work with conviction"; rivers Au Sable, Pere Marquette, Upper Manistee. — [Mangled Fly night fishing](https://mangledfly.com/night-fishing/)
- Current Works: "June through mid-September is the best time for night fishing in Northern Michigan"; "'Mousing' is the casting of big surface flies resembling mice and other surface disruptions to bring up those nocturnal brown trout which are out in the shallow flats." — [Current Works trout seasons](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-seasons/trout-traverse-city-northern-michigan/)
- Mid-summer Michigan darkness: "you have to wait until after 10:30pm for darkness." — [Trout Chasers (cites Michigan)](https://www.troutchasers.com/night-mousing-info)
- REGIONAL (Minnesota TU, Aug 2026): "Late August and September are prime times for night mousing"; big browns "come out from those deep snags and undercut banks to feed in surprisingly shallow water"; patterns Morrish Mouse, Master Splinter, brown Dahlberg Diver; red headlamp; slow steady strip with pauses. — [MNTU mousing](https://mntu.org/2026/08/mousing-at-night-for-fall-browns/)
- Morrish Mouse: foam + deer/elk hair body + rabbit strip tail, wakes across surface; sizes 4 and 6. — [Trident mouse flies](https://www.tridentflyfishing.com/fly-fishing-flies/mouse-flies.html); [Fly Fish Food Morrish Mouse](https://www.flyfishfood.com/blogs/dry-fly-tutorials/morrish-mouse)
- Master Splinter (Joe Cermele): "essentially a mousified gurgler with good wake"; other named patterns: Mr. Hankey, Mousey McMouseface, Mad Mouse, Natural Deer Hair Mouse, Trap Mouse, Midknight Express; tiers use "size 1/0 and 1 B10S hooks." — [Blue Stream Fly mouse guide](https://bluestreamfly.com/flies/mouse-patterns); [Michigan Sportsman mouse thread (redirect-blocked)](https://www.michigan-sportsman.com/threads/mouse-patterns.736716/)
- Sipping Mayflies: "Just buy a Master Splinter mouse pattern and be done with it"; best nights "pitch dark"; water 52–68°F; at night "Just let the mouse swing around towards the bank." — [Sipping Mayflies](https://sippingmayflies.com/mousing-for-brown-trout/)
- Science note: DNR lists "small rodents" and "amphibians" in adult brown trout diet. — [Michigan DNR Brown trout](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/brown-trout)

### Inferences
- Night-fishing calendar for the engine: Hex window ~June 15–July 20 (rivers), 10 pm–1 am; mousing July 1–Sept 30 with a late-Aug/Sept peak; both target brown trout (and large brook trout in UP rivers, inferred), not migratory species.
- Terrestrial calendar: ants/beetles from late May; hoppers late July–September; flying ants late Aug–Sept; cicadas sporadic; crickets after rain.

### Gaps
- No Michigan diet study quantifying mice/voles or frogs in brown trout stomachs; DNR statement is qualitative.
- No sourced Michigan cicada emergence-year data (periodical broods) found in this research pass.

## KQ7. Non-insect forage catalogue and imitating flies (for the recommendation engine)

### Takeaway
Michigan river forage that matters to salmonids, with documented (science) or guide-attested presence, and the patterns Michigan guides use to imitate them, are listed below. Items with science backing: sculpins (slimy/mottled), juvenile salmonids (brook trout, coho/steelhead smolts and fry), coarse fish, crustaceans, small rodents, amphibians; guide-attested: shiners, darters, gobies, dace/chubs, alewife/smelt in lower rivers and the St. Marys, lamprey, crayfish, leeches, sucker eggs/fry, salmon eggs, worms after rain.

### Cited Findings (forage → evidence → flies)
- Sculpins (slimy Cottus cognatus; mottled C. bairdii): slimy sculpin ~90% of available prey and most-eaten fish June–Oct in the Anna River (UP) [RR1759](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1759.pdf); mottled sculpin co-occur with brown trout in west Michigan (Stegman Creek) [GVSU](https://core.ac.uk/outputs/130170630/); Feenstra: winter forage for steelhead, bottom-hugging, olive/tan [Anchored](https://anchoredoutdoors.com/guaranteed-to-catch-more-fish-understanding-baitfish-seasons-with-kevin-feenstra/); lake-run browns eat sculpin imitations [Feenstra Gamefish](https://feenstraoutdoors.com/wordpress/gamefish-2/). Flies: Muddler Minnow (deer-hair head sculpin archetype) [Wikipedia Muddler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muddler_Minnow); Zoo Cougar #2–6 in olive/tan/yellow/white/black [RiverKeeper](https://www.johnkreft.com/galloups-zoo-cougar/); Woolly Sculpin (Galloup) [Wet Fly Swing](https://www.wetflyswing.com/kelly-galloup-on-the-best-streamer-strategies-for-giant-trout/); Sex Dungeon natural/olive [Flies and Guides](https://flysandguides.com/product/galloups-sex-dungeon-olive/); Feenstra Aqua Nuisance size 1 and Psycho Sculpin [Trident](https://www.tridentflyfishing.com/products/feenstras-aqua-nuisance-fly), [Feenstra gallery](https://www.feenstraguideservice.com/flygallery.html); Feenstra Shrew for drop-back steelhead (gobies, sculpins, chubs) [Orvis](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/pro-tips-top-five-flies-for-spring-great-lakes-steelhead).
- Juvenile salmonids (brook trout, brown trout, coho/Chinook/steelhead fry, parr, smolts): small brook trout 30–61% of large Au Sable brown trout summer diet [RR1855](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1855.pdf); stocked coho smolts (3.6–5.7 in) the main May prey in the Anna River [RR1759](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1759.pdf); spring salmon fry/steelhead fry/sucker fry are "the bulk of the trout diet" on the Muskegon [Betts](https://bettsguideservice.com/michigan-trout-guides-sucker-spawn/), [Betts spring](https://bettsguideservice.com/muskegon-river-fishing-report-spring-steelhead/); St. Marys Atlantics eat juvenile salmon/steelhead early [FishSens](https://www.fishsens.com/st-marys-rivers-atlantic-salmon-among-the-hardest-fighting-fish-in-the-great-lakes/). Flies: Parr None (early spring, rivers with salmon parr) [Orvis Hawkins](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/tuesday-tip-chuck-hawkinss-top-10-michigan-flies); Ed's Salmon Alevin [Orvis spring](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/pro-tips-top-five-flies-for-spring-great-lakes-steelhead); "salmon fry patterns" and sucker-fry patterns with lavender/pink tones [Anchored](https://anchoredoutdoors.com/guaranteed-to-catch-more-fish-understanding-baitfish-seasons-with-kevin-feenstra/); alevin or smelt patterns in spring [Michigan TU](https://issuu.com/www.michigantu.org/docs/michigan_trout_fall_2021-web/s/14447231); Drunk & Disorderly as injured-baitfish [Orvis D&D](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/big-and-meaty-tommy-lynchs-drunk-disorderly-streamer).
- Shiners (emerald/spottail) and "coarse fish": Feenstra — first thing migratory fish key on in fall; flashy patterns [Anchored](https://anchoredoutdoors.com/guaranteed-to-catch-more-fish-understanding-baitfish-seasons-with-kevin-feenstra/); Alexander notes brown trout predation on "coarse fish" [RR1855](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1855.pdf); emerald shiners a top lake prey for Lake Erie steelhead (REGIONAL) [GLFC](https://www.glfc.org/pubs/lake_committees/erie/CWTG_docs/other_reports_and_docs/fin_rep_2004_steelhead_diet_project.pdf). Flies: Feenstra Emulator, Reflector, Grease Stain (flash-bodied) [Feenstra gallery](https://www.feenstraguideservice.com/flygallery.html); white Zonkers/White Death #8 in spring [Caddis Shack](https://www.caddisshackguideservice.com/blog/steelhead-fly-fishing-in-the-upper-peninsula); Flashtail Clouser [Current Works](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-seasons/salmon-betsie-manistee-northern-michigan/); Schmidt's Double Deceiver for clear water [MeatEater](https://www.themeateater.com/fish/freshwater/how-to-pick-the-best-streamer-for-big-fall-trout).
- Darters, round gobies, dace/chubs: Feenstra winter forage (darters thin/quick; gobies "sit more visibly on rocks") [Anchored](https://anchoredoutdoors.com/guaranteed-to-catch-more-fish-understanding-baitfish-seasons-with-kevin-feenstra/); Shrew imitates gobies/sculpins/chubs [Orvis spring](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/pro-tips-top-five-flies-for-spring-great-lakes-steelhead); Sex Dungeon natural covers "chub minnows" [Flies and Guides](https://flysandguides.com/product/galloups-sex-dungeon-olive/). Anna River: cyprinids and mudminnows rare (UP small stream) [RR1759](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1759.pdf).
- Alewife and rainbow smelt (lower rivers, St. Marys): lake diet staple for steelhead, Chinook, coho, Atlantics [DNR steelhead](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/steelhead), [DNR Chinook](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/chinook-salmon), [DNR coho](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/coho-salmon); smelt present in the St. Marys when Atlantics arrive in late June (streamers on sink tips) [Rivers North](https://riversnorth.net/atlanticsalmon.html), [Betts Atlantic](https://bettsguideservice.com/michigan-atlantic-salmon.html); Torch Lake Atlantics on a smelt/cisco prey base [FR017](https://www.michigandnr.com/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/FisheriesReports/FR017.pdf); "smelt pattern" in spring on salmon rivers [Michigan TU](https://issuu.com/www.michigantu.org/docs/michigan_trout_fall_2021-web/s/14447231). - Lamprey: Ed McCoy fishes 4–7 in "leechy" lamprey patterns in spring [Mangled Fly](https://mangledfly.com/category/brown-trout/); listed among things to imitate [Michigan TU](https://issuu.com/www.michigantu.org/docs/michigan_trout_fall_2021-web/s/14447231).

### Inferences — species feeding model for the engine
- Brook trout: insect/terrestrial-dominant, opportunistic on small fish and crustaceans; flies: small Muddlers/Woolly Buggers #8–12, ants/beetles/hoppers, Hex where present (UP rivers); avoid large articulated flies except for trophy UP/Lake Superior coasters (inferred).
- Brown trout (resident): ≥12 in piscivorous; sculpin/smolt/fry/shiner streamers year-round; crayfish colors in summer/stained water; terrestrials July–Sep; Hex nymph/dun June–July; mice July–Sep at night.
- Resident rainbow trout: same as brown but smaller streamers and more nymph/fry emphasis (Feenstra: fry, stoneflies, caddis) — limited Michigan-specific data.
- Steelhead: eggs + nymphs (stone, Hex, caddis) + fry + sculpin/shiner swing flies; feeding is real but intermittent; aggression flies (Intruders, bright leeches) in fall/high water.
- Chinook/coho/pink: aggression-only model; ESL/buggers/Intruders/flash flies/eggs; pinks downsized in pink/chartreuse.
- Atlantic salmon: true in-river feeders (smelt/smolt streamers → Hex nymph/caddis/midge → fall wets/eggs).

### Gaps
- Michigan-specific fly recommendations for smelt/alewife imitations in lower rivers, scuds/sowbugs, San Juan Worm/Squirmy Wormy after rain, Pat's Rubber Legs for trout (found only in UP steelhead rig), Circus Peanut original spec, Sculpzilla, Chubby Chernobyl, and Ray Charles were not found in Michigan guide sources during this pass; they should be treated as generic recommendations, not Michigan-sourced.
- No Michigan quantitative data on crayfish, leech, or scud share of any salmonid diet.
