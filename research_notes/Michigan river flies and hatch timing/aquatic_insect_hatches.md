# Michigan River Aquatic Insect Hatches: Species-Level Reference for Fly Anglers

Scope note: Michigan rivers only. Where a Wisconsin or general Upper-Midwest/Eastern source was used to fill a gap (Troutnut species pages, wiflyfisher.com, Fox Valley TU) it is labeled "[non-MI source]" or "[WI]". Angler hatch charts and shop/guide pages are labeled "[angler]"; university, museum, and peer-reviewed sources are labeled "[scientific]". Dates from angler charts are calendar approximations that the charts themselves say vary year to year.

Baseline convention used below: unless stated otherwise, dates are for the northern Lower Peninsula (Au Sable/Manistee), which is the baseline region of the most detailed Michigan chart (Trails to Trout). Regional offsets are given in Key Question 5.

---

## Key Question 1: Major mayfly hatches on Michigan trout rivers, sequence and Michigan timing

### Takeaway
The Michigan mayfly season runs roughly: Little Mahogany/Blue Quill and Hendrickson (late April to mid May) -> Black Quill/Great Mahogany and first BWO peak (early May) -> Sulphurs, March Brown, Gray Drake and Great Speckled Olive (mid May to June) -> Brown Drake (early to mid June, 3-7 days per reach) -> Hex (mid June to mid July, peak roughly June 21-July 7 on Au Sable/Manistee, later in the U.P.) -> Light Cahill, Isonychia and Pale Evening Dun (June to August) -> Trico (late July to October, mornings) -> White Fly (mid August to September, dusk) -> fall Isonychia, BWO and Mahogany duns (September-October). All angler charts agree on this order; they disagree by 1-3 weeks on start dates depending on region and year.

### Cited Findings

#### Michigan species checklist context [scientific]
- Ethan Bright's "Aquatic Insects of Michigan" (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology Insect Division / School of Natural Resources and Environment) documents "Michigan records for at least 127 species in 42 genera" of mayflies, "with perhaps 40 additional species" known nearby; it records Hexagenia limbata as "widespread throughout the state, in many collections," plus Ephemera, Litobrancha, Isonychia bicolor, Siphlonurus rapidus and S. quebecensis, Stenonema/Stenacron/Maccaffertium, Tricorythodes, Leptophlebia, Ephoron album and E. leukon, and Ephemerellidae genera Ephemerella, Drunella, Eurylophella and Serratella — [Aquatic Insects of Michigan, Ephemeroptera species list](https://www.aquaticinsects.org/sp/Ephemeroptera/sp_eom.html)

#### Hendrickson / Red Quill — Ephemerella subvaria
- Michigan timing [angler]: Motor City Anglers chart lists Ephemerella subvaria "Hendrickson" late April-May, #10-14 — [Motor City Anglers Michigan Hatch Chart](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart)
- Trails to Trout (Au Sable/Manistee baseline) lists "Dark Hendrickson" #12-14, 4/20-5/20, peak 5/1-14, 10am-4pm — [Trails to Trout Michigan Hatch Chart](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)
- Superior Flies U.P. chart lists "Dark Hendrickson/Red Quill" #12-14, May 4-June 3, peak May 14-21, 10 AM-4 PM — [Superior Flies U.P. Hatch Chart](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/)
- Au Sable-specific [angler]: "About the last week of April the Hendrickson hatch usually starts on the Au Sable River" — [Perfect Fly, Au Sable River](https://perfectflystore.com/your-streams/fly-fishing-on-the-ausable-river-in-michigan/); an Au Sable guide report (May 10, 2024) says the Dark Hendrickson begins in late April with the Light Hendrickson "shortly after," spinner falls occurring late afternoon/evening over riffles and suppressed by cool evenings; recommended patterns Regan's Hendrickson Parachute #14, Regan's Hendrickson Spinner #14, and a Hendrickson Emerger — [Streamside Au Sable Guides](https://michigan-streamside.com/hendrickson-hatch-dark-light-rising-trout/)
- Old Au Sable Fly Shop calls Hendricksons "the first of the Big Five" on the Au Sable, #12-14, colors "creamy tan to a dark reddish brown depending on species and sex," with a Pheasant Tail nymph #12-14, emergers and a floating nymph recommended; note the shop lumps subvaria, invaria and rotunda under "Hendricksons" — [Old Au Sable Fly Shop, The Hatches](https://www.oldausable.com/the-hatches)
- Water temperature [angler entomology, non-MI]: Troutnut says most sources cite 50-55 F for emergence (Fred Arbona: 55-65 F); emergence "afternoon, usually mid-afternoon, for about 1-2 hours"; spinner falls late afternoon to evening (delayed to morning after cold evenings), often compressed into about 30 minutes near dusk beginning around 4-5 PM; nymphs in fast to slow current over gravel, small boulders and vegetation; male dun = Red Quill (deep brown thorax with reddish tinge, light reddish-brown abdomen), female dun = Light Hendrickson (lighter); Red Quill pattern usually one hook size smaller than the Hendrickson; Troutnut states southern Michigan timing aligns with the Catskills and northern regions peak in May — [Troutnut, Ephemerella subvaria](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/7/Mayfly-Ephemerella-subvaria-Hendrickson/)
- Additional temperature thresholds [angler entomology, non-MI]: sporadic emergence when water reaches the mid-40s F; "prolific emergence" needs 50-55 F; nymphs become active near 50 F; peak activity mid-April to early May lasting 2-3 weeks, starting early afternoon — [Flyfishing Entomology, The Hendrickson Hatch](http://flyfishingentomology.com/Article%20The%20Hendrickson%20Hatch.htm) (page could not be fetched directly because of an expired certificate; figures come from the search index of that page)
- Au Sable water temperatures in April run about 48-54 F, and several angler sources state that consistent, fishable dun emergence requires sustained 52-55 F, with the Au Sable hatch typically 2-5 PM — [Current, Au Sable River](https://currentfishing.com/locations/au_sable); [MidCurrent, How to Fish the Hendrickson Hatch](https://midcurrent.com/v2/how-to-fish-the-hendrickson-hatch-timing-flies-and-techniques/)
- Life cycle: univoltine, nymphs grow slowly through winter and emerge in a tight April/early-May window; the hatch "often follows the first warm days after snowmelt, with water temperatures in the mid-40s to low 50s F sparking emergence" — [Wikipedia, Ephemerella subvaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephemerella_subvaria)
- Stages that matter: nymph (dark wing cases pre-hatch), emerger, dun (mid-afternoon), spinner (evening) — synthesized from the Troutnut and Streamside sources above.

#### Sulphurs — Ephemerella invaria (incl. former E. rotunda) and E. dorothea dorothea
- Michigan timing [angler]: Motor City Anglers lists "Ephemerella invaria — Sulphur" mid-May-June, #12-14 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Trails to Trout lists "Sulphur Dun Mayfly" #12-16, 5/15-7/8, peak 6/7-30, noon-5pm; "Little Sulphur" #16-18, 5/15-6/7, evening; and "Pale Evening Dun" #16-18, 6/1-7/15, 8pm-midnight — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)
- Au Sable [angler]: Old Au Sable Fly Shop describes Sulphurs (E. dorothea, "miniature eastern sulphur") late May through July, afternoon emergence in a 1-2 hour window, #14-20, creamy yellow bodies with olive to orange shades and pale gray wings; spinner fall over riffles — [Old Au Sable](https://www.oldausable.com/the-hatches)
- Muskegon [angler]: Supinski's Muskegon chart: E. invaria #16 and E. dorothea #18-20, third week of May through June; "emergers preferred; rusty spinners at dark" — [Gray Drake Lodge, Muskegon River Hatch Cycle Chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Michigan rivers named for Sulphurs: Au Sable, Pere Marquette, Sturgeon; season May-July "extending into fall on tailwaters"; hatches sporadic through the day with evening peak, spinner falls at sunset; colors yellowish-orange, creamy yellow, yellowish-olive, and rusty (spinner); emergers and spinners are the critical stages because trout often refuse duns; patterns: Henry Ramsay emerger, Marinaro Thorax, emergers with brown Z-Lon or pheasant-tail shucks — [Hallowed Waters, The Ultimate Sulphur Primer](https://hallowedwaters.com/2021/05/the-ultimate-sulphur-primer/)
- Pere Marquette: "Sulphurs through July" — [FlyFishFinder, Pere Marquette](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/hatches/pere-marquette-river/)
- Species biology [angler entomology, non-MI]: E. invaria hatches May-June, peaking mid-June in the Upper Midwest; emergence is slow through the film in mid/late afternoon or evening; water 52-60 F; hook #14-16; nymphs brown to olive; duns pale yellowish; duns molt to spinners within a day and spinners fall at dusk over riffles; "invaria and rotunda are a single species" — [Troutnut, Ephemerella invaria](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/11/Mayfly-Ephemerella-invaria-Sulphur-Dun)
- E. dorothea is generally a size smaller (about #18 versus #14-16 for invaria) and follows or overlaps invaria later in the season — [Troutnut, Ephemerella dorothea](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/458/mayfly-ephemerella-dorothea-sulphur); [wiflyfisher Sulphur page — WI](https://wiflyfisher.com/sulphur-mayfly-hatch.asp)

#### Blue-Winged Olives — Baetis tricaudatus (and other Baetidae, Drunella, Pseudocloeon/Plauditus)
- Michigan timing [angler]: Motor City Anglers: Baetis tricaudatus April-October, #16-20 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Trails to Trout: BWO #16-20, 4/15-10/24, peaks 5/1-7 and 6/7-21, noon-3pm; "Tiny Blue-winged Olive" #20-22, 5/15-8/30; "Slate Winged Olive" #14-18, 6/21-8/14; "Great Speckled Olive Mayfly" #8-12, 5/15-7/15, peak 5/14-31, noon-5pm (probably a Drunella; chart gives no Latin name) — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); U.P.: BWO #16-20 April 29-September 7 with multiple peaks — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/)
- Muskegon: Drunella BWO #14 mid-May, Baetis #14-18 June; tiny "Pseudocloeon" BWOs #24-26 summer through November, evening emergers and spinners, trout require 7X; BWOs #24 continue late afternoons through first snow — [Gray Drake Lodge Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Species biology [angler entomology, non-MI]: Baetis tricaudatus "the most widespread and abundant baetid on the continent"; good populations "usually produce three broods with distinct peaks: Late March to April, late June, and September"; spring brood largest, later broods progressively smaller; East sizes #16-20 — [Troutnut, Baetis tricaudatus](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/240/mayfly-baetis-tricaudatus-blue-winged-olive)

#### Blue Quill / Little Mahogany — Neoleptophlebia (formerly Paraleptophlebia) adoptiva; Black Quill / Great Mahogany — Leptophlebia cupida
- Michigan timing [angler]: Motor City Anglers: Paraleptophlebia adoptiva "Little Mahogany" May-June #16-18; Leptophlebia "Great Mahogany" late April-June #10-14 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Trails to Trout: "Little Mahogany" #16-18, 4/15-7/8, peak 5/1-14, 10am-4pm; "Great Mahogany" #10-14, 4/20-6/15, 10am-4pm — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); U.P.: "Slate Wing Mahogany/Little Red Quill" #16-18, April 29-July 22, peaks May 14-28 — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/)
- Muskegon: "Early Quills" Leptophlebia cupida and Epeorus pleuralis (Black Quill, Quill Gordon) #10-12 around May 10 for about two weeks, warm afternoons into early evening — [Gray Drake Lodge Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Pere Marquette: "Fall Mahogany Duns drawing feeding activity from mid-September through October" — [FlyFishFinder, Pere Marquette](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/hatches/pere-marquette-river/)
- Species biology [non-MI]: N. adoptiva "emerges around midday after the water temperature has been above 50 degrees for a few days" and is the most important early-spring species of its genus — [Troutnut, Neoleptophlebia adoptiva](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/51/Mayfly-Paraleptophlebia-adoptiva-Blue-Quill); L. cupida is "most important in the East and Midwest," emerges by crawling out on shore or on the surface during midday/afternoon, GBIF adult records mostly April (32%), May (23%), June (22%), March (13%); low-floating emergers recommended — [Troutnut, Leptophlebia cupida](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/79/Mayfly-Leptophlebia-cupida-Black-Quill)

#### March Brown / Gray Fox — Maccaffertium (Stenonema) vicarium; Light Cahill — Stenacron / Maccaffertium spp.
- Michigan timing [angler]: Trails to Trout: "March Brown" #8-12, 5/15-7/20, noon-4pm; "Light Cahill" #10-12, 6/5-8/14, 2pm-10pm; "Golden Dun" #10-14, 7/11-9/15 — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); Fly Shack Au Sable chart: March Brown #10-12 Mar-May and Light Cahill #14 May-Jul (this aggregator's March dates for March Brown are not corroborated by any Michigan-specific source and should be treated as unreliable) — [Fly Shack Au Sable chart](https://www.flyshack.com/HatchChart.aspx?RiverID=1406)
- Muskegon: Stenonema "presence huge"; S. ithaca/canadense (Light Cahill #14), S. vicarium (American March Brown #12), S. fuscum (Grey Fox #12), June through September, with Cottonwood Flats specifically noted — [Gray Drake Lodge Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Species biology [non-MI]: M. vicarium is "by far the most important species" of the genus; the March Brown and Gray Fox are the same species in two forms; hatch mid-May through early July, late morning through evening peaking mid-afternoon; begins when water temperature reaches the mid-50s F; dun pale amber-yellow to reddish-brown, 12-16 mm — [Troutnut, Maccaffertium vicarium](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/601/Mayfly-Maccaffertium-vicarium-March-Brown)

#### Gray Drake — Siphlonurus quebecensis (also S. alternatus, S. rapidus)
- Michigan [angler]: Gray Drakes are most important on the Pere Marquette and Muskegon but "occur in most of our trout streams"; "starting as early as mid May," #10-12, gray body with a visible white stripe around the head; nymphs crawl out to emerge "so typically the dun stage is not imitated"; the spinner fall at dusk over riffly current is the fished event; patterns: Ed McCoy's Real McCoy Gray Drake Spinner, or an Adams #10-12 tied slim — [Hawkins Outfitters, Gray Drake](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/gray-drake/)
- Muskegon: Siphlonurus gray drakes #10-12, third week of May to third week of July, spinner flights at dusk (sometimes on cloudy/cold days), trout target mating doubles/triples — [Gray Drake Lodge Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html); Muskegon guide: big Gray Drake spinner falls "every evening from 7pm until dark" in season — [Betts Guide Service](https://bettsguideservice.com/muskegon-river-trout-hatch-season/)
- Other Michigan charts: Motor City Anglers: Siphlonurus quebecensis June #10-14 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Trails to Trout: Gray Drake #8-10, 5/20-7/15, peak 6/1-7, "dark" — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)
- Species biology [scientific-leaning angler source, non-MI]: S. quebecensis records mid-May through late July, 78% in June; emerges "by crawling out on shore or on the water's surface, during early night"; lays eggs "a few at a time in repeated landings on the surface, during late evening, in fast or moderately fast water"; nymphs in slow/still water near wood or rocks; the author notes good Gray Drake spinner fishing is reported "particularly in Michigan" — [Troutnut, Siphlonurus quebecensis](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/171/Mayfly-Siphlonurus-quebecensis-Gray-Drakes)

#### Brown Drake — Ephemera simulans
- Michigan [angler]: Motor City Anglers: mid-May-June #10-12 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Trails to Trout: "Brown Drake Mayfly" #8-10, 6/1-7/4, peak 6/1-14, 8pm-midnight — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); U.P.: #8-12 June 15-July 18, evening — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/); Au Sable: "Brown Drakes in May" as an evening spectacle — [RiverReports, Au Sable](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/rivers/au-sable-river-michigan-fly-fishing); Old Au Sable: late May through mid-June, #8-12, brownish-tan bodies with mottled brown/tan wings, emergence lasts 3-7 days — [Old Au Sable](https://www.oldausable.com/the-hatches)
- Species biology [non-MI]: "legendary for short-lived periods of blizzard-like hatches," peaking early June in the Midwest, typically "three to five days in one location before moving upriver" (some Midwestern spring creeks sustain it for weeks); nocturnal or very late dusk; hatching begins about 50 F; #8-10 2XL; dark brown with blotched wings; nymphs burrow in sand, fine gravel and silt in slow to moderate current and mature in one to two years; spinner falls at dusk into darkness are the best fishing — [Troutnut, Ephemera simulans](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/35/mayfly-ephemera-simulans-brown-drake)

#### Hex / Giant Michigan Mayfly — Hexagenia limbata
- Michigan timing [angler]: Trails to Trout: #4-8, 6/10-7/10, peak 6/21-7/7, 10pm-4am — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); U.P.: #4-8, June 24-July 24, peak July 5-21, 10 PM-4 AM — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/); Motor City Anglers: mid-June-July #6-10 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart)
- Michigan rivers/progression [angler]: northern Michigan lakes see Hex in early to mid June and rivers begin "7 to 10 days after they hatch on the lakes," typically mid-June to mid-July; Manistee, Au Sable and Pere Marquette named; hatches "primarily in the dark in late June" on "muggy warm nights"; nymph hooks #6-8; patterns: Robert's Yellow Drake (dun and spinner), Ed McCoy's All Day Dun and Boondoggle Hex; shorten leaders to about 6 ft 2X — [Hawkins Outfitters, Hexagenia limbata](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/hexagenia-limbata/); Au Sable Hex "in late June"; Pere Marquette's "defining event is the Hex hatch in late June" — [RiverReports, Au Sable](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/rivers/au-sable-river-michigan-fly-fishing); [FlyFishFinder, Pere Marquette](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/hatches/pere-marquette-river/)
- Species biology [scientific]: "Subimago emergence does not occur until water temperatures have reached 20 degrees Celsius" (68 F); peak adult emergence June-July but can occur late spring to early fall; typical two-year nymphal life (14-22 months), 3-4 years in cold regions; nymphs dig U-shaped burrows and can bury 10 cm deep; adults live 1-2 days; ADW states subimagos emerge "during morning and afternoon hours" with mating swarms "just before sunset" and oviposition "after sunset" — [Animal Diversity Web, Hexagenia limbata](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Hexagenia_limbata/) — NOTE: the morning/afternoon subimago statement conflicts with every Michigan angler source and with Troutnut, which describe dun emergence at dusk/after dark on trout streams; treat ADW's time-of-day claim as not applicable to Michigan rivers.
- [angler entomology]: emergence "usually dusk, sometimes late at night, occasionally midday," beginning at twilight and often continuing past midnight; spinner falls 1-3 days after emergence shortly after dark; nymphs need silt "soft enough to burrow into but rigid enough to prevent collapse"; first gained fame on Michigan and Wisconsin rivers, peaking late June/early July; males 12-24 mm, females similar or larger with yellow-orange egg masses; body pale yellow to whitish with reddish-brown markings; Caucci & Nastasi estimate trout in prime habitat may see "up to 2,000 nymphs per day during non-hatch periods" (argument for year-round Hex nymph fishing) — [Troutnut, Hexagenia limbata](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/32/mayfly-hexagenia-limbata-hex)
- Weather: angler sources describe ideal Hex weather as clear to partly cloudy, 78-82 F daytime with humid nights in the mid 60s-70s F — [Gink and Gasoline, Demystifying the Hex Hatch](https://www.ginkandgasoline.com/trout-fishing/demystifying-the-hex-hatch/); anglers discuss the water-temperature range at [Troutnut forum, Hex hatch water temperature range](https://www.troutnut.com/topic/8508/Hex-hatch-water-temperature-range)
- Degree-days [scientific, Manitoba lake data]: Flannagan & Heise (J. N. Am. Benthol. Soc. 6(4), 1987) distinguished seven H. limbata cohorts in Dauphin Lake representing three life-history patterns (2-year with immediate egg hatch; 2-year with 9 months as overwintering eggs and 15 months as nymphs; and 14-15 months); "the degree days above 10 C accumulated by each life history type were 1848, 2030 and 1806, respectively"; the same study covered Ephemera simulans — [Flannagan & Heise 1987 abstract](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/1467310) (full text returned 403; numbers from indexed abstract)
- A USGS/Journal of Great Lakes Research paper covers the H. limbata life cycle in the St. Marys River between Lakes Superior and Huron (Michigan/Ontario border) — [USGS publication record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1000336) (not retrieved; existence only)
- Stages: nymph (Hex wiggle nymph/Spring Wiggler, year-round and for steelhead, see KQ4), emerger, dun (after dark), spinner (after dark, 1-3 nights later) — synthesized from Troutnut and Hawkins above.

#### Isonychia — Isonychia bicolor ("Iso," Slate Drake, Mahogany Dun, Lead-wing Coachman)
- Michigan [angler]: Motor City Anglers: June and August-September, #10-12/#12-14 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Trails to Trout: #8-10, 5/15-9/30, evenings — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); Muskegon: "lead-winged coachman bicolor and sadleri start around the first week in June and emerge sporadically until September" — [Gray Drake Lodge blog](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html); Old Au Sable lists Isonychia among its Au Sable "Big Five" — [Old Au Sable](https://www.oldausable.com/the-hatches); U.P. guide service lists Isonychia among its hatches — [Rivers North Guide Service](https://riversnorth.net/brookandbrowntrout.html)
- Species biology [non-MI]: two peaks: late May-early July (reaching the Upper Midwest late June to mid-July) and September; emergence "usually concentrated in evening with a peak near dusk... may be sporadic all day," fall emergences more often midday; emerges by crawling out on shore leaving shucks on rocks; females "lay eggs all at once after landing on the surface, during late evening, in moderately fast water"; 9-16 mm; males intensely red with pale "white-gloved" fore tarsi; nymphs in cobble/fast water and strong swimmers — [Troutnut, Isonychia bicolor](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/649/Mayfly-Isonychia-bicolor-Mahogany-Dun); wiflyfisher [WI]: first hatches mid-May through July, second brood mid-to-late September — [wiflyfisher Isonychia](https://wiflyfisher.com/isonychia-bicolor-mayfly-hatch.asp)

#### Trico — Tricorythodes spp.
- Michigan [angler]: Trails to Trout: #22-26, 7/20-10/20, 6am-10am — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); U.P.: #22-26 August 3-November 3, 6-10 AM — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/); Motor City Anglers: mid-July-September #18-24 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Muskegon: #24, early mornings, "fish double patterns," timing correlated with bottom-draw dam releases — [Gray Drake Lodge Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Biology [non-MI angler sources]: hatch July-October, #20-26; duns pale gray/olive with light gray wings hatching late evening or early morning; the defining event is the mid-morning (about 9-11 AM) spinner fall; one source ties the spinner fall to air temperature reaching 68 F — [Troutnut, Tricorythodes genus](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/669/Mayfly-Tricorythodes-Tricos/); [wiflyfisher Trico — WI](https://www.wiflyfisher.com/Trico-Hatch.asp); [Fly Guys N Lies, Trico](https://flyguysnlies.com/insects/mayfly/trico/)

#### White Fly — Ephoron leukon (and E. album)
- Michigan [angler]: Motor City Anglers: Ephoron leukon mid-August-September #12-14 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); Trails to Trout: #10-14, 8/14-9/30, evening/night — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); Au Sable: "White Flies in August" at dusk — [RiverReports, Au Sable](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/rivers/au-sable-river-michigan-fly-fishing); Michigan White Fly hatch (leukon and album) "begins around August 14," patterns #14-16 White Wulff, White Parachute, White Comparadun — [FlyFishFinder, Michigan](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/fly-hatches-michigan/); an Au Sable guide page titled "Dark Hex & White Flies" exists — [Streamside](https://michigan-streamside.com/dark-hex-white-flies-dry-fly-time/); U.P. guide lists "Ephron Luekons" (sic) — [Rivers North](https://riversnorth.net/brookandbrowntrout.html)
- Biology [non-MI]: E. leukon (East) and E. album (West) overlap in the Midwest; August-October, peaking late August-September, at dusk; water 65-70 F; females never molt to spinners and mate/die as duns; males molt on vegetation or in flight; entire event about two hours; true duns have light gray wings over creamy white bodies (bright white patterns are inaccurate); nymphs resemble small Brown Drake nymphs; favor alkaline, warmer water — [Troutnut, Ephoron](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/820/Mayfly-Ephoron-White-Flies); [WI] burrowing nymph in sand/silt, August, 2-3 weeks, strongly phototactic, spinner tied sparse on #12-14 — [wiflyfisher Ephoron leukon](https://wiflyfisher.com/ephoron-leukon-mayfly-hatch.asp)

#### Other Michigan mayflies on charts
- Baetisca ("Bat Fly," armored mayfly) June #16-18 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart); biology: emerges by crawling out on shore late morning/midday, spinners mid-evening (8-9 PM), extremely fat-bodied so thin spinner patterns fail — [Troutnut Baetisca page](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/38/)
- "Hebe" (Leucrocuta hebe, Little Yellow Quill) #16-18, 6/15-9/30, peak July; "Quill Gordon" #12-14, 5/24-7/12; "Golden Dun" #10-14, 7/11-9/15 — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)
- Yellow Drake (Ephemera varia) is mentioned as following Sulphurs on the Au Sable — [Streamside](https://michigan-streamside.com/hendrickson-hatch-dark-light-rising-trout/)

### Inferences
- The Trails to Trout and Superior Flies charts share the same row structure and species list and appear to derive from a common Michigan source (both cite regional offsets in the same framework); they should be treated as one chart with two regional calibrations rather than two independent confirmations.
- Because Hendrickson emergence is temperature-gated near 50-55 F and April Au Sable water runs 48-54 F, "last week of April" is a median, and cold springs can push the Au Sable peak into the second week of May (consistent with the Trails to Trout peak window 5/1-14).
- For Gray Drake and Isonychia, Michigan sources consistently say to skip dun imitations and fish spinners (Gray Drake) or nymphs/spinners (Isonychia).

### Gaps
- No Michigan-specific peer-reviewed emergence-date dataset was found for any river mayfly except the Hex life-cycle study on the St. Marys River (not retrieved). Michigan DNR Fisheries Research Reports and UM Biological Station papers on Au Sable insect phenology were not located in this search.
- Ann Miller's "Hatch Guide for Upper Midwest Streams" and "Pocketguide to Upper Midwest Hatches" (author is an aquatic biologist with a cabin on a northern Michigan trout stream; the pocket guide includes pattern recipes and is sold by Gates Lodge) are the closest thing to a definitive Michigan species-by-species hatch guide, but their contents are not online — [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Hatch-Guide-Upper-Midwest-Streams/dp/1571884815); [Outdoor Journal review](https://howardmeyerson.com/2023/03/18/updated-guide-to-midwest-fly-hatches-released/); [Gates Lodge listing](https://www.gatesflyshop.com/the-pocketguide-to-upper-midwest-hatches-by-ann-mi.html)
- Gates Au Sable Lodge publishes its hatch chart inside a PDF catalog distributed by email; it was not retrievable online — [Gates Lodge catalog page](https://www.gatesflyshop.com/catalog/)
- Exact species identity of Michigan "Light Cahill" (Stenacron interpunctatum vs Maccaffertium spp.) and "Great Speckled Olive" (Drunella sp.) was not confirmed by any Michigan source.

## Key Question 2: Major caddis hatches and Michigan timing

### Takeaway
Michigan's angler-relevant caddis are the early-season Little Black Caddis (Chimarra aterrima, late April-June, midday emergence, afternoon egg-laying), the Grannom/Mother's Day caddis (Brachycentrus spp., late April into summer, afternoon), the season-long net-spinners (Hydropsyche "Spotted/Cinnamon Sedge" and Cheumatopsyche "Little Sister/Green Sedge," May-October, afternoon to dark), the summer "White Miller" (Nectopsyche, July-September), and the fall Great Autumn Brown Sedge / October Caddis (Pycnopsyche, August-October). Peer-reviewed work from Hillsdale College (Houghton) shows Michigan caddis flight peaks in early-to-mid July on the Manistee system and that calendar date predicts flight timing better than water temperature in small streams.

### Cited Findings
- Checklist [scientific]: Michigan has "about 76 genera and 299 species" of caddisflies, including Brachycentrus americanus, fuliginosus, incanus, lateralis, numerosus; Micrasema rusticum, wataga; 20+ Hydropsyche spp.; Cheumatopsyche analis, aphanta, campyla, gracilis, minuscula, oxa, pasella, sordida, speciosa; Glossosoma intermedium, lividum, nigrior; Rhyacophila brunnea, fenestra, fuscula, glaberrima, invaria, ledra, lobifera, mainensis, manistee; Helicopsyche borealis; Neophylax aniqua, concinnus, fuscus, oligius; Pycnopsyche aglona, antica, circularis, guttifer, indiana, lepida, subfasciata; Chimarra aterrima, feria, obscura, socia — [Aquatic Insects of Michigan, Trichoptera species list](https://www.aquaticinsects.org/sp/Trichoptera/sp_tom.html)
- Michigan chart timing [angler] (Trails to Trout, Au Sable/Manistee baseline): Little Black Caddis #16-20, 4/20-6/30, peak 5/1-14, noon-10pm; Grannom Caddis #12-16, 4/20-7/31, 2pm-dark; Green Caddis #10-16, 5/1-7/31, 2pm-dark; Spotted Sedge #12-16, 5/1-9/30, noon-dark; Cinnamon Caddis #14-18, 5/20-9/30, 4pm-dark; Great Orange Sedge #8-10, 6/1-7/31, evenings; White Miller Caddis #12-16, 7/1-9/30, afternoons; October Caddis #8-10, 7/20-1/30, evening/morning — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); U.P.: Little Black Caddis #16-18 May 4-August 14, peak May 14-21 — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/); statewide: Chimarra aterrima "Black Caddis" late April-June #14-18; "Tan Caddis" May-October #12-16; "October Caddis" late September-October #10-14 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart)
- Muskegon (southern LP tailwater) [angler]: Hydropsyche bifida "cinnamon caddis" #16 and Brachycentridae "black caddis" #20 from the second week of May, black caddis "extremely heavy mid-May through June" at dusk; summer/fall: Cheumatopsyche speciosa (green caddis #18-20), Hydropsyche, Macrostemum zebratum (zebra caddis), Limnephilidae (autumn sedge), Nectopsyche albida (white miller #14 at dark), mid-July through October; the Muskegon is called "world renowned" for caddis and requires 6X-7X; trout feed heavily on "quad-wing" spent egg-layers at dusk — [Gray Drake Lodge Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Chimarra aterrima biology [non-MI angler entomology]: "better known in the Midwest"; produces heavy hatches; adults mostly May-June; "emerges slowly through the surface film, during late morning or midday"; "lays eggs by sitting on the surface, during late afternoon"; larvae in fast cobble/gravel, "drift extensively prior to emerging"; low-floating emergers recommended; egg-laying behavior is disputed (LaFontaine: rides the surface; Swisher & Richards: dives) — [Troutnut, Chimarra aterrima](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/2952/Caddisfly-Chimarra-aterrima-Little-Black-Sedge); [Troutnut, Chimarra genus](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/2947/Caddisfly-Chimarra-Little-Black-Sedges)
- Brachycentrus biology [non-MI]: adults #14-16 emerge in late afternoon once water reaches the low 50s F — [MidCurrent, Caddisfly Hatches February to April](https://midcurrent.com/v2/caddisfly-hatches-from-february-through-april-flies-timing-and-presentation-for-every-stage/); genus emerges "during early midday or afternoon" with intense brief hatches, most records May-July; freshly emerged adults have pale blonde wings and bright green bodies, egg-layers light brownish-gray wings and medium green bodies; females ride the water to oviposit; larvae build tapered square-section wood cases in fast rocky water and hang on silk lines (LaFontaine's white-leader trick) — [Troutnut, Brachycentrus](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/1751/Caddisfly-Brachycentrus-Grannoms); [WI] northern Wisconsin Grannom emerges mid-April with "Michigan's Upper Peninsula... a week or two later" — [Fox Valley TU, American Grannom](https://foxvalleytu.org/hatch-chart/bugs-american-grannom/)
- Hydropsyche/Cheumatopsyche biology [non-MI]: Hydropsyche "emerges slowly through the surface film during early or late morning, evening, or night," some species emerge underwater and rise as winged adults; pupal wingpads darken to near black a day or so before emergence; Cheumatopsyche occurs lower in watersheds than Hydropsyche/Ceratopsyche and includes lighter-winged species — [Troutnut, Hydropsyche](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/1916/Caddisfly-Hydropsyche-Spotted-Sedges); [Troutnut, Cheumatopsyche](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/1913/Caddisfly-Cheumatopsyche-Little-Sister-Sedges)
- Glossosoma [non-MI]: sporadic emergence limits the importance of adults/pupae, but larvae (day) and diving egg-layers (dusk) matter; emerges by crawling out or on the surface late morning or evening; females dive and swim to the bottom in fast water late evening; larvae dead-drift — [Troutnut, Glossosoma](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/1800/Caddisfly-Glossosoma-Little-Black-Short-Horned-Sedges)
- Pycnopsyche [non-MI]: P. lepida is "the most abundant midwestern species"; adults mostly August (31%), September (28%), July (24%), October (10%); about 20 mm, uniformly reddish-brown; larval cases of wood or pebbles — [Troutnut, Pycnopsyche lepida](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/2909/Caddisfly-Pycnopsyche-lepida-Great-Autumn-Brown-Sedge)
- Rhyacophila for steelhead [angler, MI]: a green Rhyacophila caddis larva pattern is "effective on the Muskegon River for steelhead" — [Schrems West Michigan TU, Nymph Fishing for Steelhead](https://swmtu.org/fishing/nymph-fishing-for-steelhead)
- Michigan peer-reviewed phenology [scientific]: Houghton (Hillsdale College), Environ. Entomol. 44(6):1472-87, 2015: 27 caddis species tracked weekly 2010-2014 at forest and meadow sites on a first-order northern Lower Michigan stream; at the forest site 10 of 11 abundant species were univoltine and 1 bivoltine; at the meadow site 5 univoltine, 5 bivoltine, 3 enigmatic; "Date was a better predictor of flight periodicity than water temperature" for nearly all species, so "warming water temperatures... may not always be primary life cycle synchronizers in small streams" — [PubMed 26339996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26339996/)
- Houghton, J. Freshwater Ecology 2013: over 18,000 specimens of 44 species collected sunset-to-sunrise on six nights in early and mid-July 2010 on a large northern Lower Michigan river, described as "the seasonal peak of adult caddisfly abundance in the Manistee River watershed"; overall abundance peaks within an hour or two after sunset — [Nocturnal flight periodicity of caddisflies in a large Michigan river](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02705060.2013.780187); related: [Analysis of the caddisflies of the Manistee River watershed](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273760440_Analysis_of_the_caddisflies_Trichoptera_of_the_Manistee_River_Watershed_Michigan); [When to sample adult caddisflies: five-year study of a first-order Michigan stream](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02705060.2018.1431968)

### Inferences
- Michigan angler names map to genera as follows (inferred from the Muskegon chart plus Troutnut): "Little Black Caddis" = Chimarra aterrima (and small dark Brachycentridae on the Muskegon), "Grannom/Mother's Day" = Brachycentrus, "Spotted/Cinnamon Sedge" = Hydropsyche, "Green/Little Sister Sedge" = Cheumatopsyche, "White Miller" = Nectopsyche, "October Caddis/Great Autumn Brown Sedge" = Pycnopsyche (not the western Dicosmoecus).
- Houghton's finding that date beats temperature for caddis flight timing means fixed calendar windows are more defensible for Michigan caddis than for mayflies like Hendrickson and Hex, whose emergence is explicitly temperature-gated.

### Gaps
- The species-by-species flight-period table from Houghton 2015 (which would give exact Michigan weeks for Brachycentrus, Hydropsyche, etc.) is behind paywalls/403 on ResearchGate and Environmental Entomology; only the abstract was obtained.
- No Michigan source gave emergence timing for Helicopsyche, Neophylax, or Micrasema; Neophylax (a fall emerger elsewhere) and Helicopsyche are on the Michigan checklist but not on any Michigan angler chart found.

## Key Question 3: Stoneflies that matter in Michigan and their timing

### Takeaway
Michigan's stonefly sequence is: tiny winter stones (Capniidae, Allocapnia spp.; January-April, #16-20), Early Black Stones (Taeniopteryx nivalis/Allocapnia; late February-April, #10-14, midday), Early Brown Stones (Strophopteryx fasciata-type; mid March-early May), Yellow Sallies (Isoperla spp.; May-June, into summer), Golden Stones (Acroneuria/Paragnetina; mid May-August, nocturnal/crawl-out), and the Giant Black Stone Pteronarcys dorsata (mid May-July, sporadic, nocturnal). Nymphs, not adults, are the main angling stage for all but the early black stones and Yellow Sallies.

### Cited Findings
- Checklist [scientific]: Michigan records include Allocapnia granulata, A. minima, A. pygmaea, A. vivipara (Capniidae); Taeniopteryx burksi, T. nivalis, T. parvula, Strophopteryx fasciata, Oemopteryx glacialis (Taeniopterygidae); Isoperla bilineata, cotta, decepta, dicala, frisoni, lata, marlynia, nana, richardsoni, signata, slossonae, transmarina (Perlodidae); Acroneuria abnormis, frisoni, internata, lycorias; Paragnetina media ("common in collections"); Agnetina capitata, flavescens (Perlidae); Pteronarcys dorsata ("widespread in state") and P. pictetii; Chloroperlidae Haploperla brevis, H. orpha, Suwallia marginata — [Aquatic Insects of Michigan, Plecoptera species list](https://www.aquaticinsects.org/sp/Plecoptera/sp_pom.html)
- Michigan chart timing [angler]: Tiny Black Stonefly #16-20, 1/1-4/30, noon-4pm; Early Black Stonefly #10-14, 2/21-4/30, noon-4pm; Early Brown Stonefly #10-14, 3/15-5/10, 1pm-7pm; Medium Brown Stonefly #6-10, 5/1-6/30, afternoons; Yellow Sally #10-12, 5/1-6/30, afternoons; Golden Stonefly #4-6, 5/15-6/30, any time; Giant Stonefly #2-8, 5/15-6/30, any time; Big Golden Stonefly #6-8, 6/21-8/7 — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); U.P.: Tiny Black Stonefly #16-20 January 14-May 14; Early Black Stonefly #10-14 March 7-May 14, noon-4 PM; Giant Stonefly/Salmonfly #2-8 May 29-July 14 — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/); statewide: Taeniopteryx nivalis Early Black Stone March-April #10-16; "Pteronarcys" Giant Stonefly mid-May-June #4; Isoperla Yellow Sally late May-June #12-14 — [Motor City Anglers](https://mca.fish/pages/michigan-hatch-chart) (the MCA chart's species name "Pteronarcys californica" is a western species; the Michigan species per UMMZ is P. dorsata)
- Early black stones, Michigan detail [angler]: Allocapnia and Taeniopteryx are "extremely abundant" in Michigan's alkaline, groundwater-fed trout streams; peak emergence February through May; nymphs "start to stir when water temps hit 36F," most active on warm sunny afternoons 1-4 PM; nymphs migrate in drift and crawl on snow along banks; females perform egg-laying flights with wings "beating continuously"; #12-14; patterns: Sly Stone Wiggle (tungsten, articulated nymph) and Sly Sister Stone (CDC-wing adult); the Muskegon tailwater is named — [Hallowed Waters, Cold Stoned](https://hallowedwaters.com/2024/03/cold-stoned/); the same author notes the hatch is "extremely difficult to predict and time" because it is weather-driven — [Gray Drake Lodge blog, Early Black Stonefly Hatch](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2020/03/the-early-black-stonefly-hatch-more.html); Muskegon chart: Allocapnia/Taeniopteryx mid-February through May, drift migrations, females oviposit on warm afternoons — [Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Early stones for steelhead [angler, MI]: small nearly black nymphs hatch late winter/early spring and are found crawling on shelf ice; pattern about half an inch long with iron-blue dun fur body, gray squirrel tail and grizzly hackle — [Schrems West Michigan TU](https://swmtu.org/fishing/nymph-fishing-for-steelhead); the Little Black Stonefly (Allocapnia granulata) is listed among the top Michigan steelhead flies, peak late February through April, best on sunny days in clear water — [Current Works, Top 5 Steelhead Flies for Michigan](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/top-5-steelhead-flies/)
- Golden stones [non-MI biology + MI angler]: Acroneuria lycorias emerges early June through mid-July and "is a major species in the Midwest"; emerges by crawling out on shore early morning, afternoon or evening; nymphs migrate from riffles to slower bank water before emergence, so nymphs should be fished tight to the banks — [Troutnut, Acroneuria lycorias](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/1042/Stonefly-Acroneuria-lycorias-Golden-Stone); Muskegon: "golden stones" #14 (chart labels them Isoperla) mid-June through summer, late night and early morning — [Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Yellow Sallies [non-MI]: Isoperla is a very common trout-stream genus (about 60 species); hatches sporadically May through much of summer; nymphs crawl toward calmer shoreline water before emerging and are dislodged into the drift; I. bilineata is the classic "Yellow Sally" — [Troutnut, Isoperla](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/1115/Stonefly-Isoperla-Yellow-Sallies); [Troutnut, Isoperla bilineata](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/1150/Stonefly-Isoperla-bilineata-Yellow-Sally); Au Sable aggregator chart: Yellow Sallie #12-16 May-Aug — [Fly Shack Au Sable](https://www.flyshack.com/HatchChart.aspx?RiverID=1406)
- Pteronarcys dorsata [non-MI]: the largest common eastern stonefly, up to 2 inches, nymphs take 2-4 years; mostly nocturnal so "generally less important" than western salmonflies, but nymph imitations "produce some very large trout" and egg-laying adults can be fished locally — [Troutnut, Pteronarcys dorsata](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/975/Stonefly-Pteronarcys-dorsata-Giant-Black-Stonefly); [wiflyfisher, P. dorsata nymph — WI](https://wiflyfisher.com/blog/2021/10/25/pteronarcys-dorsata-nymph-giant-stonefly/); Muskegon: giant black stoneflies June evenings, sporadic, "not a major hatch; effective after dark" — [Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html); Au Sable guide notes stoneflies "showing up" and "producing quality trout" in early May — [Streamside](https://michigan-streamside.com/hendrickson-hatch-dark-light-rising-trout/)

### Inferences
- The "Early Brown Stonefly" of Michigan charts (mid March-early May, #10-14) most likely corresponds to Strophopteryx fasciata and/or Taeniopteryx spp. on the UMMZ list; no Michigan source names it explicitly.
- Michigan's Golden Stones are Acroneuria (lycorias, abnormis) and Paragnetina media; Supinski's Muskegon chart labels its #14 "golden stones" as Isoperla, which is a size/genus mismatch and probably refers to large Yellow Sallies.

### Gaps
- No scientific emergence-timing data specific to Michigan stoneflies were found; timing rests entirely on angler charts.
- No Michigan source gave hatch dates for Paragnetina media despite UMMZ calling it common.

## Key Question 4: Midges, crane flies, black flies and other insects (winter and steelhead nymphing)

### Takeaway
Michigan winter trout and steelhead fishing revolves around Chironomid midges (#20-28, warm afternoons near springs, heaviest November-April on the Muskegon), Allocapnia/Taeniopteryx black stonefly nymphs (late February-April), Hexagenia nymphs (the 50-year-old Spring Wiggler is a Hex nymph imitation fished year-round), and green Rhyacophila caddis larvae; crane flies appear in Michigan charts only as yellow adults alongside Sulphurs, and no Michigan-specific source was found that treats dragonflies, damselflies, aquatic beetles or black flies as river hatches of angling importance.

### Cited Findings
- Muskegon (southern LP) [angler]: January-April "Midges/Scuds": Simulium (black fly) and Chironomid midges #20-24 with black bodies, scuds #18, on warm afternoons, "heavy winter hatches near springs" with trout surface-feeding; November-December: tiny BWOs #24 and black midges #22 "mid-December onward," fed on "rigorously" — [Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Northern LP and U.P. charts [angler]: Midge #20-28, 6/1-10/30 (Trails to Trout); Midges #20-28, June 15-November 13, "anytime" (Superior Flies) — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/) (note: these charts omit winter midges even though the Muskegon chart shows them as the main winter food)
- Crane flies [angler]: Muskegon "yellow crane flies" #16 hatch "along with sulphers" (late May-June) and are "often confused with mayflies" — [Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html); general (non-MI) pattern sources describe crane fly larvae as 1-3 inch worm-like larvae most useful during high water when dislodged — [Dakota Angler, Cranefly Larva](https://flyfishsd.com/blogs/blog/cranefly-larva-crane-fly-nymph); [Orvis, Swimming Crane Fly Larva](https://howtoflyfish.orvis.com/fly-tying-videos/nymph-flies/831-swimming_crane_fly_larva)
- Steelhead nymphing, Michigan [angler]: the Spring Wiggler "was tied to imitate a Hexigenia nymph, has been around for over fifty years" (yellow chenille body, fox squirrel tail) and is effective year-round, especially winter; early black stonefly nymphs; a green Rhyacophila caddis larva on the Muskegon; winter fishing peaks around 4 PM when temperatures rise slightly — [Schrems West Michigan TU, Nymph Fishing for Steelhead](https://swmtu.org/fishing/nymph-fishing-for-steelhead); Little Black Stonefly (Allocapnia granulata) ranked among Michigan's top steelhead flies, peak late February-April, sunny days, clear water; a weighted stonefly as point fly with an egg on a tag — [Current Works](https://www.current-works.com/fly-fishing-articles/top-5-steelhead-flies/); Michigan Trout Unlimited's magazine ran an article "Steelhead and the Snowfly" (Spring 2022) on winter stoneflies and steelhead — [Michigan Trout, Spring 2022](https://issuu.com/www.michigantu.org/docs/michigan_trout_spring_2022_-_final_web/s/14953108) (not retrieved)
- Hex nymphs year-round [angler entomology]: trout in prime habitat may encounter "up to 2,000 nymphs per day during non-hatch periods" (Caucci & Nastasi via Troutnut) — [Troutnut, Hexagenia limbata](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/32/mayfly-hexagenia-limbata-hex)
- Dragonfly/damselfly [non-MI, general]: dragonfly nymph patterns are described as stillwater/backwater tools ("slow moving backwaters and lakes"), fished deep with a hopping retrieve — [Orvis News, Dragonfly Nymphs for Trout](https://news.orvis.com/fly-fishing/natural-reflections-dragonfly-nymphs-for-trout)
- Terrestrials on Michigan charts (for sequence context) [angler]: Green Oak Worm #8-12 5/15-7/10; Beetles 6/1-9/30; Ants #12-24 6/15-9/30; Crickets 7/1-10/15; Grasshoppers #6-12 7/15-9/30 — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); Muskegon brown flying ants #16-22 mid-August to mid-September on hot afternoons — [Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)

### Inferences
- The Muskegon chart's "Simulium" midge entry indicates black flies (Simuliidae) are present in the winter/spring drift, but no Michigan source treats a black fly hatch as a fishable event; they are relevant mainly as tiny dark larva/pupa imitations.
- For a Michigan fly-pattern catalogue, the winter/steelhead nymph box implied by these sources is: black stonefly nymph #12-16, midge larva/pupa #20-24 (black), Hex nymph/Spring Wiggler #6-8, green caddis larva #12-16, plus eggs.

### Gaps
- No Michigan river source was found for Chironomid species identity, for crane fly larvae (Tipula) as trout food, or for aquatic beetles, dragonflies or damselflies on rivers. These should be treated as minor/unsupported for Michigan rivers.
- The Michigan TU "Steelhead and the Snowfly" article was not retrieved and may contain the best Michigan-specific winter stonefly detail.

## Key Question 5: Definitive Michigan emergence sequence and regional timing differences

### Takeaway
No single authoritative statewide chart exists; the most complete public Michigan chart is Trails to Trout's (calibrated to the Au Sable/Manistee), with Superior Flies publishing the same framework recalibrated for the Upper Peninsula, and Matt Supinski's Muskegon chart the most detailed for the southern/mid-state tailwaters. The charts agree that mid-state rivers (Muskegon, Pere Marquette) run 1-2 weeks earlier than the Au Sable/Manistee, "Tip of the Mitt" rivers (Sturgeon, Pigeon, Black) 1-2 weeks later, and U.P. rivers (Fox, Carp, Ontonagon, Escanaba, Two Hearted) 2-4 weeks later.

### Cited Findings
- Regional offsets [angler]: Trails to Trout baseline is "northern rivers (Au Sable, Manistee)"; "Mid-state rivers (Muskegon, Pere Marquette): Subtract 1-2 weeks"; "Tip of the Mitt rivers (Sturgeon, Pigeon, Black): Add 1-2 weeks"; "Upper Peninsula rivers (Fox, Carp, Ontonagon): Add 2-4 weeks" — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/)
- Superior Flies (U.P. baseline): "Tip-Of-The-Mitt - Subtract 1 Week," "Northern Rivers - Subtract 2 Weeks," "Mid-State Rivers - Subtract 3 Weeks"; emergence dates "are approximate and actual timing varies" — [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/)
- FlyFishFinder: Tip of the Mitt rivers about 1-2 weeks later; U.P. rivers (Fox, Whitefish, Ontonagon listed) 2-3 weeks later than northern LP — [FlyFishFinder, Michigan](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/fly-hatches-michigan/)
- Worked example of the offset (Hex): Trails to Trout northern LP peak 6/21-7/7 vs Superior Flies U.P. peak July 5-21; Hendrickson northern LP peak 5/1-14 vs U.P. peak May 14-21; Trico northern LP 7/20 start vs U.P. Aug 3 start — [Trails to Trout](https://www.trailstotrout.com/resources/michigan-hatch-chart/); [Superior Flies](https://www.superior-flies.com/hatch-chart/)
- Au Sable "Big Five" sequence [angler]: Hendricksons (first), Sulphurs, Brown Drakes, Isonychia, and (by implication, the fifth) Hex — [Old Au Sable Fly Shop](https://www.oldausable.com/the-hatches); Au Sable major hatches summarized as Hex (late June), Brown Drake (May), White Fly (August) and BWO, with the Holy Water and Main Branch fished month by month — [RiverReports, Au Sable](https://www.riverreports.com/river-intel/rivers/au-sable-river-michigan-fly-fishing)
- Pere Marquette sequence [angler]: Brown Drakes in May, Hex late June (defining event), Sulphurs through July, hoppers August, fall Mahogany Duns mid-September through October — [FlyFishFinder, Pere Marquette](https://flyfishfinder.com/pages/hatches/pere-marquette-river/); Gray Drakes from mid-May on the PM and Muskegon — [Hawkins Outfitters, Gray Drake](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/gray-drake/)
- Muskegon (tailwater below Croton Dam) sequence [angler]: winter midges/scuds (Jan-Apr) -> early black stones (mid-Feb-May) -> Black Quill/Quill Gordon (about May 10) -> Sulphurs (3rd week May-June) -> Gray Drakes (3rd week May-3rd week July), Brown Drake, increasing Hex "along silted banks" -> Stenonema Cahills/March Browns (June-Sept) -> Isonychia (June-Sept) -> Tricos, tiny BWOs, summer caddis (mid-July-Oct) -> flying ants (mid-Aug-mid-Sept) -> BWOs/midges to first snow; Trico timing "correlates with bottom-draw bubbler releases"; the river is "eutrophic," alkaline gravel/rock/vegetation "ideal for 90% of bug life" — [Gray Drake Lodge Muskegon chart](http://graydrakelodgeandoutfitters.blogspot.com/2013/05/new-muskegon-river-hatch-cycle-chart.html)
- Upper Peninsula hatches named by a U.P. guide service: "Blue Winged Olives, Sulphurs, Caddis, Isonychia, Brown Drakes, Cahills, and Ephron Luekons," season last Saturday in April through September 30, float trips mid-May through late June — [Rivers North Guide Service](https://riversnorth.net/brookandbrowntrout.html)
- Hex progression within Michigan: lakes in northern Michigan early-mid June, rivers 7-10 days later — [Hawkins Outfitters, Hex](https://hawkinsoutfitters.com/hexagenia-limbata/); Hex hatches "move northward through the Upper Midwest" — [Troutnut, Hexagenia limbata](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/32/mayfly-hexagenia-limbata-hex)
- Aggregator charts (Fly Shack, FlyFishFinder, Big Y) exist for the Au Sable and Pere Marquette but list dates that conflict with guide sources (e.g., Fly Shack's Au Sable chart shows March Brown and Hendrickson starting in March) and give no scientific names — [Fly Shack Au Sable](https://www.flyshack.com/HatchChart.aspx?RiverID=1406); [Fly Shack Pere Marquette](https://flyshack.com/HatchChart.aspx?RiverID=1408); [Big Y Fly Co, Pere Marquette](https://www.bigyflyco.com/peremarquette.asp)

### Inferences
- A defensible statewide model: take the Trails to Trout northern-LP dates as the baseline; apply -1 to -2 weeks for the Muskegon, Pere Marquette, Rogue and other southern/mid-state rivers (the Muskegon and Huron, being warmer/tailwater or southern, are also where Ephoron and Stenonema are proportionally more important); +1 to +2 weeks for Boardman/Jordan/Sturgeon/Pigeon/Black; +2 to +4 weeks for U.P. rivers (Two Hearted, Fox, Escanaba, Ontonagon).
- The Boardman River is not named in any chart found; geographically it fits the northern-LP baseline rather than the Tip-of-the-Mitt offset.

### Gaps
- No chart specifically for the Two Hearted, Fox, Escanaba, Ontonagon, Boardman, Rogue or Huron was found; the Fred Waara (U.P.) TU chapter does not publish a hatch chart online — [Fred Waara TU](https://fredwaaratu.org/)
- Gates Lodge, Mason-Griffith Founders TU, Schmidt Outfitters and Baldwin Bait & Tackle hatch charts were not retrievable as web pages during this search.

## Key Question 6: Scientific evidence on temperature, photoperiod and degree-day cues

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed and university sources give hard thresholds for only a few species: Hexagenia limbata subimagos do not emerge until water reaches 20 C (68 F) and cohorts in a Manitoba lake required about 1,800-2,030 degree-days above 10 C to complete development; Ephemerella subvaria emergence is universally tied to water in the 50-55 F range (angler-entomology sources, no Michigan degree-day model found); Baetis species are multivoltine with photoperiod and temperature jointly setting brood timing and summer diapause; and, importantly for caddis, Houghton's five-year Michigan study found calendar date predicts flight timing better than water temperature.

### Cited Findings
- Hexagenia limbata [scientific]: "Subimago emergence does not occur until water temperatures have reached 20 degrees Celsius"; nymphal development is temperature-dependent (about 1 year in warm lake regimes, 2 years in cooler streams, 3-4 in cold regions); eggs can survive more than a year at 8 C, allowing overwintering — [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Hexagenia_limbata/)
- Hexagenia limbata and Ephemera simulans degree-days [scientific, Manitoba]: three H. limbata life-history types accumulated 1848, 2030 and 1806 degree-days above 10 C; life histories were 2 years (immediate egg hatch), 2 years (9 months as overwintering eggs + 15 months as nymphs), and 14-15 months — [Flannagan & Heise 1987, J. N. Am. Benthol. Soc.](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/1467310)
- Hexagenia limbata Michigan waters [scientific]: a USGS-authored study documents the life cycle in the St. Marys River between Lakes Superior and Huron — [USGS pubs record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1000336) (content not retrieved)
- Ephemerella subvaria [angler entomology]: emergence 50-55 F (Arbona 55-65 F) — [Troutnut](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/7/Mayfly-Ephemerella-subvaria-Hendrickson/); sporadic in mid-40s F, prolific at 50-55 F — [Flyfishing Entomology](http://flyfishingentomology.com/Article%20The%20Hendrickson%20Hatch.htm); univoltine, emergence follows first warm days after snowmelt at mid-40s to low-50s F — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephemerella_subvaria)
- Ephemera simulans [angler entomology]: emergence begins about 50 F water; nymphs mature in one to two years — [Troutnut, Ephemera simulans](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/35/mayfly-ephemera-simulans-brown-drake)
- Ephemerella invaria: 52-60 F — [Troutnut](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/11/Mayfly-Ephemerella-invaria-Sulphur-Dun); Maccaffertium vicarium: mid-50s F — [Troutnut](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/601/Mayfly-Maccaffertium-vicarium-March-Brown); Neoleptophlebia adoptiva: after water has been above 50 F "for a few days" — [Troutnut](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/51/Mayfly-Paraleptophlebia-adoptiva-Blue-Quill); Ephoron: 65-70 F — [Troutnut](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/820/Mayfly-Ephoron-White-Flies); Brachycentrus: low 50s F — [MidCurrent](https://midcurrent.com/v2/caddisfly-hatches-from-february-through-april-flies-timing-and-presentation-for-every-stage/); early black stones: nymphs active from 36 F — [Hallowed Waters](https://hallowedwaters.com/2024/03/cold-stoned/)
- Baetis [scientific, non-MI]: "Photoperiod and water temperature are the main environmental features shaping Baetis species niches," water temperature influencing hatching time, nymph growth and voltinism — [Hydrobiologia 2026, four co-occurring Baetidae](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10750-026-06342-5); in a multivoltine baetid, photoperiod likely interacts with temperature to cue summer diapause, and elevated water temperature prolonged diapause and shifted the last generation later into autumn — [Scientific Reports 2016, Increased temperature delays late-season phenology](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep38022); emergence phenology responses to temperature are species-specific across aquatic insects — [Finn et al. 2022, Diversity and Distributions](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ddi.13472); Baetis tricaudatus produces three broods (late March-April, late June, September) with progressively smaller flies — [Troutnut](https://www.troutnut.com/hatch/240/mayfly-baetis-tricaudatus-blue-winged-olive)
- Caddisflies, Michigan [scientific]: over five years on a northern Lower Michigan first-order stream, "Date was a better predictor of flight periodicity than water temperature" for nearly all 27 species; temperature mattered more at the open meadow site than the forest site — [Houghton 2015, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26339996/)

### Inferences
- The degree-day figures for Hex (about 1,800-2,000 DD > 10 C per generation) explain why Michigan's cold groundwater rivers run a two-year Hex cycle and why river hatches trail nearby lakes by 7-10 days (lakes warm to 20 C sooner).
- Combining the 20 C Hex threshold with Michigan angler dates implies Au Sable/Manistee Hex water typically reaches about 68 F in the third week of June, and U.P. rivers 2-3 weeks later; this is an inference, not a measured claim.
- Angler "temperature triggers" for Hendrickson (50-55 F) and Sulphur (52-60 F) come from angling entomology (Arbona, Caucci & Nastasi) rather than controlled studies; they should be presented as rules of thumb.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed degree-day or photoperiod model for Ephemerella subvaria, Ephemera simulans (in rivers), or Brachycentrus emergence was found; no MSU Extension, MSU Entomology, MSU Enviroweather or UM Biological Station publication on trout-stream insect emergence timing was located in this search.
- The full text of Flannagan & Heise (1987), the St. Marys River Hex paper, and Houghton (2015) could not be retrieved, so species-level Michigan flight-week tables and river-specific Hex temperature data remain unverified beyond the abstracts.
